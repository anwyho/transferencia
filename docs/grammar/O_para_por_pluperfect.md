# Bundle O — Para vs por + pluperfect (Lessons 50–53)

The bundle where Spanish's **two "for"s** get sorted out — *para* (destined-for /
in-order-to) vs *por* (because-of / time / exchange) — and where the past grows a deeper
layer: *había* + participle = "I **had** done" (the past before the past). Along the way:
no *-ing* after a preposition (use the to-form), and two different "could"s — *podía*
(was able to) vs *podría* (would be able to).

The recurring move: **don't translate "for" or "could" by reflex — decide which meaning
you want, then pick the Spanish word that carries it.**

> **Grammar terms** appear in *(italics)* next to the plain label, e.g. **to-form verb**
> *(infinitive)*. Plain label first so it reads like Mad Libs; the real term is there when
> you want it. **Slots** are written in `[brackets]` — fill them in.

---

## 1. Word groups

Words sorted by **the job they do in a sentence**, not by dictionary order. Drill the group, not the word.

### The two "for"s *(prepositions para / por)* — pick by meaning, not by reflex
| Spanish | English | job |
|---------|---------|-----|
| `para` | for (destined for) / in order to | something/someone is the **destination** or the **purpose** |
| `por`  | for (because of) / for (a length of time) / for (in exchange) | the **cause**, a **time span**, or a **swap** |

`para` points **forward** to a goal or recipient; `por` points **back** to a cause (or names a duration/exchange). See the decision table in §2.

### After-preposition pronouns *(prepositional pronouns)* — special shapes for "me" and "you"
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `mí`   | me  | only `mí` and `ti` change shape after a preposition |
| `ti`   | you (informal) | no accent on `ti`, accent on `mí` |
| `él / ella / nosotros` | him / her / us | regular pronoun, unchanged |
| `conmigo` | with me | irregular: **never** `con mí` — one word |

`para mí`, `de ti`, `por él` — the preposition just sits in front. Only "me/you-informal" get the `mí/ti` shapes.

### Past-helper *haber* *(auxiliary; line-past for the pluperfect)* — builds "I had ___"
| Spanish | English |
|---------|---------|
| `había`    | I / he / she / you-formal had |
| `habías`   | you had |
| `habíamos` | we had |
| `habían`   | they had |
| `habría`   | would have (irregular contraction, see §2) |

Always the **line-past** of *haber* under here — it's the only choice for "had + done" (§2, S6).

### The two "could"s *(poder: line-past vs conditional)* — past vs hypothetical
| Spanish | English | when |
|---------|---------|------|
| `podía` | could / was able to | the **past** — "I couldn't do it yesterday" |
| `podría`| could / would be able to | the **future/hypothetical** — "I could do it tomorrow" |

Both come from *poder*; the `e` drops in `podría` (*podería* → `podría`). Most past "could"s are `podía`.

### Hooks for "would" *(conditional `-ía` ending)*
| Spanish | English |
|---------|---------|
| `me gustaría` | I would like (the canonical "would" hook) |
| `me quedaría` | I would stay |
| `cambiaría`   | I would change/exchange |

The `-ía` tail = "would". Pluck it off *gustar* → *gustaría* and reuse the pattern.

### Little words
| Spanish | English | term |
|---------|---------|------|
| `por qué` | why (because of what) | question word |
| `porque`  | because | conjunction (never `porque de` for "because of" — use `por`) |
| `ya`      | already (usually sentence-front) | adverb |
| `ayer`    | yesterday | adverb |
| `aquí`    | here | adverb |
| `día`     | day (masculine despite `-a`: *el día, los días*) | noun |
| `todos los días` | every day | phrase |

### False friends — look Latin, mean something else
| Spanish | looks like | actually means |
|---------|-----------|----------------|
| `actual` / `actualmente` | actual | **current / currently** (*mi trabajo actual* = my current job) |
| `discutir` | discuss | mostly **to argue** (use `hablar` for "discuss") |
| `creer` vs `crear` | — | believe vs create (both give `creo` — context decides) |

## 2. Sentence structures (drill these)

Each structure: a **shape** line (the only "code"), the slots you can swap, worked examples, then a **Drill** the student finishes out loud or on paper.

---

### S1 — "for me / for you" the destined-for way *(para + prepositional pronoun)*
**shape:** `[thing] + para + [mí/ti/él/ella]`

`para` = it's **destined for** that person. The `mí/ti` shapes appear after the preposition.

```
Este es para ti        This one is for you (masc.)
Estos son para ti      These are for you
Lo compro para ti      I'm buying it for you (it'll be yours)
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** for me · for him · this one is for you · I'm buying it for her

---

### S2 — "because of you" / the cause-and-time `por` *(por for cause, duration, exchange)*
**shape:** `[clause] + por + [cause / time span / thing swapped]`

`por` covers three jobs English crams into "for": **because of**, **for a length of time**, **in exchange for**.

| sentence | English | which job |
|----------|---------|-----------|
| `Lo compro por ti` | I'm buying it because of you | because of (your influence) |
| `Por esta razón, no quiero ir` | For this reason, I don't want to go | because of |
| `He venido por ti` | I have come because of you | because of |
| `Me quedo por tres días` | I'm staying for three days | time span |
| `Cambiaría este por ese` | I'd change this one for that one | exchange/swap |

**Note:** "because of" is **never** `porque de` — always `por`. And `por` doubles as the multiplication word (*ocho por ocho* = 8×8), so online it's written "x": *gracias x venir*.

**Drill:** because of you · for this reason I don't want to go · I'm staying for three days · I'd change this for that

---

### S3 — para vs por: the decision *(choosing the right "for")*
**shape:** `... + [para | por] + ...`

Same English "for you" — two Spanish sentences, two meanings:

| Spanish | English | reading |
|---------|---------|---------|
| `Lo compro para ti` | I'm buying it for you | **destined for** you — it'll be yours |
| `Lo compro por ti`  | I'm buying it because of you | your **influence** — not necessarily yours |
| `Estoy para ti` | I'm (there) for you | at your service / available |
| `Estoy por ti`  | I'm here because of you | you're the **cause** I'm here |

**Decision table — which "for"?**

| If "for" means… | use | example |
|-----------------|-----|---------|
| destined for someone | `para` | `para ti` |
| in order to (purpose) | `para` | `Estoy aquí para verte` |
| because of | `por` | `por ti` |
| for a span of time | `por` | `por tres días` |
| in exchange for | `por` | `este por ese` |

**Drill:** I'm buying it for you (it's yours) · I'm buying it because of you · I'm here for you (available) · I'm here because of you

---

### S4 — "in order to ___" *(para + infinitive of purpose)*
**shape:** `[clause] + para + [verb-toform]`

When English "to" really means "**in order to**", you **must** add `para`. The to-form alone won't carry that meaning.

```
Estoy aquí para verte               I am here (in order) to see you
Hemos hablado para resolverlo       We've spoken (in order) to resolve it
Te he llamado para hablarlo         I've called you (in order) to speak about it
Hemos hablado para intentar resolverlo   We've spoken to try to resolve it
```

You can't say *hemos hablado a resolverlo* or *hemos hablado resolverlo* — you need `para`.

**Drill:** I am here to see you · we've spoken to resolve it · I've called you to speak about it

---

### S5 — no `-ing` after a preposition: use the to-form *(preposition + infinitive)*
**shape:** `[preposition: para/por/de/en/con/a] + [verb-toform]`

After **any** Spanish preposition, use the **to-form** *(infinitive)*, never the `-ing` form. Dragging in English `-ing` here is **negative language transfer**.

| sentence | English | preposition |
|----------|---------|-------------|
| `Gracias por venir` | Thanks for coming | `por` + `venir` (not *viniendo*) |
| `Gracias por llamarme` | Thanks for calling me | `por` + `llamarme` |
| `Estoy cansada de trabajar` | I'm tired of working | `de` + `trabajar` |
| `Es una forma de hacerlo` | It's a way of doing it | `de` + `hacerlo` |
| `No creo en discutir` | I don't believe in arguing | `en` + `discutir` |

Also: general statements use the to-form too — *Nadar es saludable* (swimming is healthy), **not** *nadando*.

**Drill:** thanks for coming · thanks for calling me · I'm tired of working · I don't believe in arguing

---

### S6 — "I had ___" the pluperfect *(haber line-past + participle)*
**shape:** `(ya) + [había/habías/habíamos/habían] + [participle]`

To say the **past-before-the-past** ("I had eaten"), use the **line-past** of *haber* + the participle. The participle never changes, same as always.

```
Había comido            I had eaten
Ya había comido         I had already eaten
Ya había cenado         I had already dined
Lo había perdido        I had lost it
```

`ya` (already) usually sits at the **front** — least emphatic, most natural. Contrast two people with `yo`/`él`:

```
Él quería cenar conmigo, pero yo ya había cenado
  He wanted to dine with me, but I had already dined
Lo buscaba porque yo lo había perdido
  He was looking for it because I had lost it
```

**había paradigm (line-past of *haber*):**

| person | form | + participle |
|--------|------|--------------|
| I / he / she / you-formal | `había` | `había comido` |
| you (informal) | `habías` | `habías comido` |
| we | `habíamos` | `habíamos comido` |
| they | `habían` | `habían comido` |
| (would-have, irregular) | `habría` | `habría perdido` |

`habría` drops the `e` (*habería* → `habría`) just like `podría` — "I **would have** ___": *Lo habría perdido* = I would have lost it.

**Drill:** I had eaten · I had already dined · I had lost it · I would have lost it

---

### S7 — the two "could"s: `podía` vs `podría` *(line-past vs conditional of poder)*
**shape:** `(no) + [podía | podría] + [verb-toform](+[pronoun])`

| sentence | English | which |
|----------|---------|-------|
| `No podía hacerlo ayer` | I couldn't do it yesterday | `podía` — **past** |
| `No podía hacerlo porque no tenía el tiempo` | I couldn't do it because I didn't have the time | `podía` — past |
| `Podríamos hacerlo` | We could do it | `podría` — **hypothetical/future** |

Make `podía` by chopping `-er` and adding the line-past `-ía`: *podía, podías, podía, podíamos, podían*. Make `podría` with the would-ending `-ía` on *poder*, `e` dropping out.

**Drill:** I couldn't do it yesterday · I couldn't do it because I didn't have the time · we could do it (tomorrow)

---

### S8 — bumping certainty: present → future → would *(present / future `-é` / conditional `-ía`)*
**shape:** `me quedo` → `me quedaré` → `me quedaría`

Same verb, three degrees of commitment. Future adds `-é` (`-ás, -á, -emos, -án`) to the **full** to-form; "would" adds `-ía`.

```
Me quedo por tres días       I'm staying for three days    (as if scheduled)
Me quedaré por tres días     I will stay for three days    (more committed)
Me quedaría por tres días    I would stay for three days   (hypothetical)
Me quedaría por tres días, pero tengo que trabajar
  I would stay for three days, but I have to work
```

**Drill:** I'm staying for three days · I will stay for three days · I would stay, but I have to work

## 3. Same idea, said other ways

- **"I was eating"** → `comía` (line-past) **OR** `estaba comiendo` (*estar* + `-iendo`). Mostly interchangeable. Same for *Tomaba un curso* = *Estaba tomando un curso* (I was taking a course).
- **The `-ing` form lives only with *estar*** for "right now": `Estoy comiendo` = I'm eating. The pronoun can ride either end: `Estoy explicándolo` = `Lo estoy explicando` (I'm explaining it).
- **Make `-ando/-iendo`:** chop `-ar` → `-ando` (*hablar* → *hablando*); chop `-er/-ir` → `-iendo` (*comer* → *comiendo*).
- **English `-ing` adjectives → `-ante/-iente`**, not the verb form: *interesante* (interesting), *irritante*, *frustrante*, *la luna menguante* (the waning moon — not *menguando*), *un ser viviente* (a living being).
- **Days of the week from planets:** *lunes* (luna/moon), *martes* (Mars), *miércoles* (Mercury), *jueves* (Jupiter, with j), *viernes* (Venus) — more visibly than English.

## 4. Emphasis & bends

- **Decide the "for" first.** `para` = destination/purpose (points forward); `por` = cause/duration/exchange (points back). The whole bundle hinges on §2–S3.
- **`mí/ti` only after prepositions.** Everywhere else the regular pronoun stays. And **`conmigo`**, never *con mí*.
- **`ya` goes up front.** *Ya había comido* is the natural, low-emphasis order.
- **`había` is the only helper for "had + done".** Don't reach for any other tense of *haber* here — the line-past is locked in.
- **Two "could"s, one decision:** past → `podía`, hypothetical → `podría`. Most past "could"s are `podía`; the dot-past (preterite) refinement comes later.
- **Contrast with `yo`/`él`.** Drop the subject by default, but throw it in to keep two people straight: *Él quería… pero yo ya había…*.

## Carried-in lexicon (from earlier bundles)

- `gracias por venir` — "thanks for coming", the original hook for the no-`-ing`-after-preposition rule (introduced L50, drilled L51–52).
- `estoy / estás / está / estaba` — *estar* for states/locations (earlier bundles); reused for `estoy para/por ti` and the `estar + -iendo` continuous.
- Demonstratives `este / ese / esta / esa / estos / esos` (Tracks 47, 50) — fuel for the exchange-`por` swaps: *cambiaría este por ese*.
- Line-past `-aba / -ía` endings (Tracks 46–49) — the same `-ía` that builds `podía`, `quería`, `había`.
- `participle` (`comido, ido, hablado, perdido, cenado`) — unchanged after *haber*, same rule as the earlier present-perfect bundle.
