# Bundle Z — Closeout + Dialects (Lessons 88–90)

The last bundle. You close out the irregular **point-in-the-past** family (*puse, hice,
hizo, vine, traje, fui*), learn to **build your own rules** from patterns you hear, and
then take a tour of the **dialects** you'll actually meet — Spain's *leísmo* and *vosotros*,
the aspirated *s*, and *vos* in the Río de la Plata.

By now the moving parts are all familiar — this bundle is about finishing the irregular
spine and handing you the analytical tools to keep going on your own:

```
[ irregular base ]   [ mixed -ar/-er-ir endings ]   [ no accent ]
   pus-, hic-,           -e -iste -o -imos -ieron       (puse, not pusé)
   vin-, traj-, fu-
```

> **Grammar terms** appear in *(italics)* next to the plain label, e.g. **point-in-past**
> *(preterite)*. Plain label first so it reads like Mad Libs; the real term is there when
> you want it. **Slots** are written in `[brackets]` — fill them in.

## 1. Word groups

Words sorted by **the job they do in a sentence**, not by dictionary order. Drill the group, not the word.

### Irregular point-past verbs *(preterite; irregular root + mixed endings, no accent)* — "I did ___" (one moment, finished)
| Spanish | English | base | note |
|---------|---------|------|------|
| `puse`  | I put   | `pus-` | *poner*; the *-pose* in compose/expose/oppose |
| `hice`  | I did / I made | `hic-` | *hacer*; but **`hizo`** for he/she (c→z) |
| `vine`  | I came  | `vin-` | *venir*; *vino* = "he came" **and** "wine" |
| `traje` | I brought | `traj-` | *traer*; *traje* also = "suit" (clothing) |
| `fui`   | I was / I went | `fu-` | *ser* **and** *ir* share this — context decides |

All take the **same mixed endings** (`-e -iste -o -imos -ieron`) and **no accent**: *puse*, not *pusé*.

### Past-mood verbs *(past subjunctive; built from the they-form of point-past)* — "if I were / that I did"
| Spanish | English | built from |
|---------|---------|------------|
| `fuera`   | were / went (hypothetical) | `fueron` → drop `-on` |
| `tuviera` | had (hypothetical)         | `tuvieron` → drop `-on` |
| `hiciera` | did / made (that…)         | `hicieron` → drop `-on` |
| `trajera` | brought (that…)            | `trajeron` → drop `-on` |
| `dijera`  | said (that…)               | `dijeron` → drop `-on` |

Always built from the **they-form**, never the to-form — that's what carries the irregularity over.

### Rule-discovery words *(determiners + quantifiers; agreement puzzles)*
| Spanish | English | the puzzle |
|---------|---------|------------|
| `demasiado` | too / too much / too many | changes for **nouns**, not for **adjectives** |
| `el agua`   | the water (fem.!)         | short fem. *a-…-a* takes `el` |
| `un` / `algún` / `ningún` | a / some / no | drop `-o` before a **masculine** noun |

### Dialect words *(regional variants)*
| Spanish | English | where |
|---------|---------|-------|
| `le` / `les` | him/her/you-formal/them (as object) | Spain (*leísmo*) |
| `vosotros`   | you-all (informal plural) | Spain only |
| `vos`        | you (alternative to *tú*) | Río de la Plata |
| `podés` / `querés` | you can / you want | *vos* forms |

### Little words
| Spanish | English | term |
|---------|---------|------|
| `de más` | extra / spare ("of more") | the *de + más* inside *demasiado* |
| `si`     | if (sets up the hypothetical) | conjunction |
| `quería que` | I wanted that… (+ past mood) | triggers past subjunctive |

## Closeout rules — the last irregular spine

Not sentence structures, but the rules that finish the point-past family and hand you the analytical method.

### A. The last irregular point-past verbs
Same recipe as the whole irregular family: **irregular base + mixed endings + no accent.**

```
poner  → pus-  → puse, pusiste, puso, pusimos, pusieron
hacer  → hic-  → hice, hiciste, HIZO, hicimos, hicieron
venir  → vin-  → vine, viniste, vino, vinimos, vinieron
traer  → traj- → traje, trajiste, trajo, trajimos, trajeron
```

- **Spelling switch `c → z` in `hizo`.** *hic-* + *-o* would spell *"hico"* (hard sound). To
  keep the soft "s" sound, the *c* flips to *z*: **`hizo`** (he/she made/did).
- **`fui` does double duty.** *fui* is the point-past of **both** *ser* AND *ir*. *Fui* = "I
  was" **or** "I went"; *fue* = "he was" **or** "he went." Context tells you which.
- **Dropped *i* in the they-form.** It "should" be *fuieron, trajieron, dijieron* — awkward to
  say, so the *i* falls away: **`fueron, trajeron, dijeron`**.

### B. Building the past mood from irregulars
Recipe is unchanged: take the **they-form** of the point-past, drop `-on`, add the normal
mood-tense endings. Going from *they* (not the to-form) is precisely what carries the
irregularity over.

```
fueron    → fuera, fueras, fuera, fuéramos, fueran     "if I were"
tuvieron  → tuviera, tuvieras, tuviera, tuviéramos, tuvieran   "if I had"
hicieron  → hiciera, hicieras, hiciera, hiciéramos, hicieran   "that I did"
```

Practical escape hatch: if an irregular is too hard in the moment, swap a more regular
synonym. *Decir* is brutally irregular for "would" (*te diría*) → use *contar* (*te contaría*).

### C. Become your own rule-maker (three puzzles)
You now have enough tools to **work out the rest yourself**. Break a pattern apart, compare
cases, find the rule. Three worked examples:

1. **`demasiado`** (= *de más* + *-iado*, "of more"). With a **noun**, it agrees in gender +
   number: *demasiadas casas, demasiados coches*. With an **adjective**, it does **not** change:
   *demasiado agresivo / agresiva / agresivos / agresivas* — the adjective moves, *demasiado* sits still.
2. **`el` with feminine words.** Short feminine words that are **two syllables AND begin and end
   in *a*** take *el* in the singular to dodge the *la-ala* sound clash: *el agua, el ala, el aula*.
   They're **still feminine** — the adjective proves it: *el agua frí**a*** (not *frío*). Longer
   *a-* words keep *la*: *la almohada, la alfombra*.
3. **`uno / alguno / ninguno` drop `-o`** before a **masculine** noun: *un hombre, algún hombre,
   ningún hombre*. Standing alone (no noun) the full form stays: *Quiero uno. No quiero ninguno.*
   Feminine never shortens: *una mujer, alguna mujer, ninguna mujer*. Note the written accent on
   *algún / ningún* (stress lands on the final syllable once the *o* drops).

## Dialects — what you'll actually hear

The differences between dialects are **minor**; you now have the know-how to identify and
understand them. If you like, adopt the dialect of the people you practice with — the more you
sound like what you hear, the more you'll understand back.

### Spain — *leísmo*
When referring to **people** (him/her/you-formal/them), most of Spain uses **`le` / `les`**
instead of *lo / la / los / las*.
- *Le veo.* — I see him/her/you-formal. *(Spain)*
- *Lo veo.* — I see it. *(Spain)* / I see him/it. *(Latin America — uses *lo/la* even for people)*

### Spain — *vosotros*
**`vosotros`** (you-all, informal plural) is used in most of Spain and basically nowhere in
Latin America. Worth listening for if you'll practice Spain Spanish.

### Aspirated *s*
In many accents (Caribbean, southern Spain, parts of Latin America) the *s* goes breathy or
disappears:
- */ehtá/* for *está* · */cómo ehtá/* for *cómo está* · */ehpero/* for *espero*

### Río de la Plata — *vos*
**`vos`** (around Buenos Aires and Montevideo, ~30% of the Spanish-speaking world) is another
word for *tú*, used alone or mixed with *tú*. The verb stress lands on the **end**, so the
diphthong (vowel split *e→ie*, *o→ue*) **doesn't happen**:
- *Tú puedes → Vos **podés*** (you can)
- *Tú quieres → Vos **querés*** (you want)

### Pan-Hispanic accent
Speakers who travel between countries develop a **mixed accent**, and may switch depending on
who they're talking to — that's normal; even natives do it.

## 2. Sentence structures (drill these)

Each structure: a **shape** line (the only "code"), the slots you can swap, worked examples, then a **Drill** the student finishes out loud or on paper.

---

### S1 — "I ___ed" (one finished moment) *(irregular preterite)*
**shape:** `[irregular-point-past]`

The irregular point-past table — note `fui` = was/went, and `hizo` (c→z):

| verb | I | he/she/you-formal | we | they |
|------|---|-------------------|-----|------|
| poner | puse | puso | pusimos | pusieron |
| hacer | hice | **hizo** | hicimos | hicieron |
| venir | vine | vino | vinimos | vinieron |
| traer | traje | trajo | trajimos | trajeron |
| ser/ir | fui | fue | fuimos | fueron |

```
¿Dónde lo pusiste?   Where did you put it?
Vino con vino.       He came with wine.
Traje un traje.      I brought a suit.
No fui.              I didn't go. (or: I wasn't.)
No fue.              He didn't go. (or: he wasn't.)
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** I put it · he made it · they came · I brought a suit · I didn't go

---

### S2 — "If I were ___" (hypothetical past) *(past subjunctive after *si*)*
**shape:** `si + [past-mood] (+ rest)`

- past-mood: `fuera` · `fueras` · `tuviera` · `hiciera`

```
Si yo fuera tú.      If I were you.
Si fuera usted.      If I were you (formal).
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** if I were you · if I were you (formal) · if you were here

---

### S3 — "If I had it, I'd tell you it" *(conditional sentence: si + past mood, then conditional)*
**shape:** `si + [past-mood], [conditional]`

Spanish loves to **end with the object** — the *lo* tucks in nicely at the close.

```
Si lo tuviera, te lo contaría.   If I had it, I would tell you (it).
```

(*contaría* = the regular stand-in for the brutally irregular *diría*.)

**Drill (EN → ES):** if I had it, I'd tell you it · if I had a car, I'd come

---

### S4 — "I wanted [someone] to do it" *(quería que + past subjunctive)*
**shape:** `quería que + [past-mood]`

`quería que` triggers the **past mood** on the next verb.

```
Quería que lo hicieran.    I wanted them to do it.
Quería que lo hiciéramos.  I wanted us to do it.
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** I wanted them to do it · I wanted us to do it

---

### S5 — Agreement puzzles: `demasiado`, `el agua`, `un/algún/ningún` *(determiner/quantifier agreement)*
**shape:** `[quantifier/article] + [noun or adjective]`

```
demasiadas casas        too many houses     (with noun → agrees)
demasiado agresiva      too aggressive (f.) (with adjective → no change)
el agua fría            the cold water      (fem., but el + fría)
un hombre / algún hombre / ningún hombre   a / some / no man (masc. → dropped -o)
una mujer / ninguna mujer                  a / no woman (fem. → full form)
Quiero uno. No quiero ninguno.             I want one. I want none. (standalone → full)
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** too many cars · too aggressive (f.pl.) · the cold water · some man · no woman · I want one

## 3. Same idea, said other ways

- **`fui`** = "I was" **and** "I went" — *ser* and *ir* fully merge in the point-past. Same form, two verbs.
- **`vino`** = "he came" (from *venir*) **and** "wine." *Vino con vino* = "He came with wine."
- **`traje`** = "I brought" (from *traer*) **and** "suit." *Traje un traje* = "I brought a suit."
- **`te diría` ↔ `te contaría`** — same "I'd tell you," but swap the wildly irregular *decir* for the regular *contar* when you're stuck.
- **`le veo` vs `lo veo`** — same "I see him" depending on dialect (Spain's *leísmo* vs Latin America).
- **`tú` ↔ `vos`** — same "you," different region; *vos* just shifts the stress so the vowel doesn't split (*podés*, not *puedes*).

---

## 4. Emphasis & bends

- **No accent on irregular point-past.** *puse*, not *pusé*; *hice*, not *hicé*. The irregular base already carries the stress.
- **`c → z` only in `hizo`.** It's a spelling fix to keep the soft sound, not a new rule — the *c* would otherwise go hard before *o*.
- **Past mood comes from the they-form.** Always *fueron → fuera*, never the to-form — that's the whole point: irregularities ride along.
- **Don't be fooled by `el agua`.** The article flips for sound; the word stays feminine, so the adjective stays feminine too (*fría*).
- **`-o` drops before masculine only.** *un / algún / ningún* in front of a masculine noun; everywhere else the full form (*uno, alguno, ninguno*; *una, alguna, ninguna*) stands.
- **Listening ≠ speaking.** You'll catch by ear less than you can say. Deconstruct what you hear the same way you built sentences up — just in reverse. Don't aim for perfection; expose yourself, make mistakes, enjoy the journey. You're already a Spanish speaker.

## Carried-in lexicon (from earlier bundles)

- **Irregular point-past family** — *pus-/hic-/vin-/traj-/fu-* extend the base-changing pattern already seen in *pud-* (poder) and *hub-* (haber) from earlier bundles.
- **Past mood after `si`** — the *si fuera* hypothetical reuses the past-subjunctive-after-*si* rule introduced earlier (around L83).
- **Diphthong on the stressed syllable** (*e→ie*, *o→ue*) — recalled to explain why *vos podés / querés* **don't** split the vowel (stress moved to the end).
- **Direct-object pronouns `lo/la/los/las`** — the standard set, contrasted here with Spain's *le/les*.
- **Adjective–noun agreement** (gender + number) — the engine behind the *demasiado* and *el agua fría* puzzles.
