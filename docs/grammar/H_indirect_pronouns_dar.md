# Bundle H — Indirect pronouns + dar + agreement intro (Lessons 24–27)

The bundle where the little words *me, te, nos* learn a second job: not just "me/you/us"
but **"to me / for me"** — Spanish skips the extra "to" and "for". You also get *dar* (to
give), the two-pronoun stack (*te lo doy* = "I give it to you"), and your first taste of
adjectives, which in Spanish sit **after** the noun and **agree** with it.

Everything here snaps together from a few moving parts:

```
[ subject ]   [ indirect ]   [ direct ]   [ changed verb ]   ( + adjective after noun )
   yo            te             lo            doy                 el auto rojo
 (optional)   (to/for you)    (it/the thing) (the action)      (adj follows the noun)
```

> **Grammar terms** appear in *(italics)* next to the plain label, e.g. **to-form verb**
> *(infinitive)*. Plain label first so it reads like Mad Libs; the real term is there when
> you want it. **Slots** are written in `[brackets]` — fill them in.

## 1. Word groups

Words sorted by **the job they do in a sentence**, not by dictionary order. Drill the group, not the word.

### Indirect-object pronouns *(indirect-object pronouns)* — "to / for ___", and they go BEFORE the changed verb
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `me` | me / to me / for me | one word covers all three — no extra `a`/`para` |
| `te` | you / to you / for you | informal |
| `nos`| us / to us / for us | |

The big idea: **don't translate the "to" or "for".** *Me habla* = "he speaks **to** me";
*me lo compra* = "he buys it **for** me." The `me` already carries the to/for.

### `dar` (to give) *(irregular present)* — the verb this bundle is built around
| Spanish | English | who |
|---------|---------|-----|
| `doy`   | I give        | yo |
| `das`   | you give      | tú (informal) |
| `da`    | he/she gives, you give | él/ella/usted |
| `damos` | we give       | nosotros |
| `dan`   | they / you-guys give | ellos/ustedes |

`doy` rhymes with the `-oy` you already know from `voy` and `soy`.

### Other verbs that show up here
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `quedar`   | to remain / be left over | reflexive `me quedo` = "I stay" (see §3) |
| `llamar`   | to call / phone | `ll` = /y/; reflexive `me llamo` = "my name is" |
| `cocinar`  | to cook | regular `-ar`: `cocino, cocinas, cocina, cocinamos, cocinan` |
| `comprar`  | to buy | `me lo compra` = he buys it for me |
| `hablar`   | to speak | `me habla` = he speaks to me |
| `encontrar`| to find | o → ue split: `encuentro` (see Bundle on splitting) |

### Numbers 6–10 *(cardinal numbers)*
| Spanish | English | hook |
|---------|---------|------|
| `seis`  | six   | |
| `siete` | seven | "set"-piece; links to *Septiembre/Setiembre* |
| `ocho`  | eight | *oct*agon, *Oct*ober |
| `nueve` | nine  | *Nov*ember; o splits to ue (`nove → nueve`) |
| `diez`  | ten   | *Dec*ember |

### Articles — match the noun's gender *(definite/indefinite articles)*
| Spanish | English | gender |
|---------|---------|--------|
| `el` / `la`   | the | masc / fem |
| `un` / `una`  | a   | masc / fem |
| `los` / `las` | the (plural) | masc / fem |

`-ión` words are **feminine**: *la situación, la opinión*. Gender belongs to the **word**,
not the object — *coche/carro/auto* are all masc, *máquina* is fem, all mean "car".

### Little words
| Spanish | English | term |
|---------|---------|------|
| `algo`  | something | pronoun |
| `aquí` / `acá` | here (Spain / Lat. Am.) | adverb |
| `hasta` | until | preposition |
| `en`    | in | preposition |
| `esta noche` | tonight | time phrase |
| `cómo`  | how (→ *¿cómo te llamas?*) | question word |

## Two-pronoun stacking — the big rule of this bundle

Not a sentence structure yet, but the rule every drill below leans on.

When an indirect pronoun (*me/te/nos*, the **person**) and a direct pronoun (*lo/la/los/las*,
the **thing**) appear together, the **person comes first, the thing second**:

```
te (you) + lo (it)  →  te lo  →  te lo doy   I give it to you
me (me)  + lo (it)  →  me lo  →  me lo dan   they give it to me
nos (us) + lo (it)  →  nos lo →  ¿nos lo dan? are they giving it to us?
```

Build the **verb first**, then attach the little words. "I want to give it to you" → first
get *quiero dar*, then add *te lo*.

## Adjectives go AFTER the noun

Opposite of English. And they **agree**:

```
el auto rojo        the red car       (masc → rojo)
la casa roja        the red house     (fem  → roja)
las casas grandes   the big houses    (plural noun → plural adj +s)
```

`-o` ↔ `-a` for gender; `+s` for plural — same regular endings as nouns.

## 2. Sentence structures (drill these)

Each structure: a **shape** line (the only "code"), the slots you can swap, worked examples, then a **Drill** the student finishes out loud or on paper.

---

### S1 — "to/for me" with one little word *(indirect-object pronoun)*
**shape:** `[me/te/nos] + [changed verb]`

The pronoun sits **before the changed verb**. Don't move it just because the English has
"to" or "for".

```
me habla         he speaks to me
me hablan        they speak to me
me lo compra     he buys it for me
te lo compro     I buy it for / from you   (context decides)
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** he speaks to me · they speak to me · he buys it for me · I buy it for you

---

### S2 — Stack two: indirect + direct, "I give it to you" *(indirect + direct object clitics)*
**shape:** `[me/te/nos] + [lo/la/los/las] + [changed verb]`  *(person first, thing second)*

This is the headline pattern. Order is fixed: **person → thing → verb**.

| build | result | English |
|-------|--------|---------|
| te + lo + doy  | `te lo doy`    | I give it to you |
| me + lo + dan  | `me lo dan`    | they give it to me |
| nos + lo + dan | `¿nos lo dan?` | are they giving it to us? |
| me + lo + habla| `me lo habla`  | he's speaking to me about it |

**Drill:** I give it to you · they give it to me · he buys it for me · are they giving it to us?

---

### S3 — Future by context: present verb + a time word *(present-for-future)*
**shape:** `[stacked-pronoun sentence] + [time word]`

The verb stays **present**; a word like *mañana* (tomorrow) makes it future.

```
te lo doy mañana       I'll give it to you tomorrow
¿me lo da mañana?      are you giving it to me tomorrow?
nos quedamos hasta las nueve   we're staying until nine
```

**Drill:** I'll give it to you tomorrow · are you giving it to me tomorrow? · we're staying until nine

---

### S4 — Helper + to-form: pronoun can go two places *(clitic placement: proclisis vs enclisis)*
**shape:** `[helper] [to-form]+[pronoun]`  **OR**  `[pronoun] [helper] [to-form]`

With a helper + a "to" form, the little words can either **glue to the end** of the to-form,
or **sit before the helper**. Both are correct.

| stuck on end | before the verb | English |
|--------------|-----------------|---------|
| `quiero dártelo`   | `te lo quiero dar`   | I want to give it to you |
| `¿quieres hacérmelo?` | `¿me lo quieres hacer?` | do you want to do it for me? |
| `voy a quedarme`   | `me voy a quedar`*   | I'm going to stay |

The reflexive matches the **doer**: *quieres quedarte* (you stay → `te`), not `me`.

**Drill:** I want to give it to you (both ways) · do you want to do it for me? · can you cook something for me? (`¿me puedes cocinar algo?`)

---

### S5 — Reflexive "stay": `me quedo` *(reflexive verb)*
**shape:** `[me/te/nos] + quedar-form (+ place / time)`

Spanish doesn't say "I stay" — it says "I remain myself." The *me/te/nos* is **required**,
not emphatic. Set: *me quedo, te quedas, se queda, nos quedamos, se quedan*.

```
me quedo                 I stay
me quedo aquí            I'm staying here
me quedo aquí hasta las diez   I'm staying here until ten
no te quedas aquí        you're not staying here
¿por qué no te quedas aquí?    why aren't you staying here?
```

**Drill:** I stay · I'm staying here · I'm staying here until ten · you're not staying here · why aren't you staying here?

---

### S6 — Noun + adjective (after, agreeing) *(attributive adjective agreement)*
**shape:** `[article] [noun] [adjective]`

Adjective **follows** the noun and **agrees** in gender and number.

```
la situación global       the global situation
la cámara digital         the digital camera
el auto rojo              the red car
las casas grandes         the big houses
los autos grandes         the big cars
```

**Drill:** the global situation · the red car · the red house (`la casa roja`) · the big houses · the big cars

---

### S7 — Put it together: reflexive + place + noun-adjective *(building the backbone)*
**shape:** `[me/te/nos] [quedar-form] + en + [article] [noun] [adjective]`

Find the **verb backbone first**, then hang "in / the / red / car" off it.

```
me quedo en el auto rojo         I'm staying in the red car
me he quedado en el auto rojo    I have stayed in the red car
he comprado un auto rojo         I have bought a red car
me he comprado un auto rojo      I have bought myself a red car
```

**Drill:** I'm staying in the red car · I have stayed in the red car · I have bought a red car · I have bought myself a red car

## 3. Same idea, said other ways

- **"My name is…"** → `me llamo …` ("I call myself…"), and **"What's your name?"** →
  `¿cómo te llamas?` ("how do you call yourself?"). Spanish routes both through *llamar*.
- **Helper-verb shortcut.** When you don't know a verb's form, reach for `puedo / quiero /
  tengo que / debo` + the to-form: `no puedo encontrarlo` (I can't find it) is easier to
  build on the fly than the more native `no lo encuentro`.
- **Spanish often skips "can".** `¿Ves el auto?` ("do you see the car?") is more natural than
  `¿puedes ver el auto?`. Both work.
- **Question = intonation only.** `¿Te lo compro?` (rising tone) = "shall I buy it for you?"
  — no extra "shall" word, just `¿…?` and your voice.
- **`me/te/nos` is one word doing three jobs:** "me", "to me", "for me" — let context pick.

---

## 4. Emphasis & bends

- **Big subject pronouns float; little words don't.** `yo, tú, él, ella, usted, ustedes` can
  sit at the start, middle, or end (`¿usted me lo da mañana?` / `¿me lo da usted mañana?`).
  But `me, te, nos, lo` stay locked **before the changed verb** (or on the to-form's tail).
- **Reflexive emphasis needs the subject pronoun.** The `me` in `me quedo` is mandatory, not
  emphatic. To stress *who*, add the subject: `yo me quedo`, `nosotros nos quedamos`.
- **Have-past + reflexive: the pronoun goes BEFORE `haber`.** `me he quedado` (I have stayed)
  — never `he me quedado`, never `he quedado me`.
- **Build by rule, not by feel.** Verb backbone first, little words after, adjective after
  the noun. If your rule is wrong, the mistake teaches you — guessing by "sounds right" freezes you.
- **`ustedes` = "you guys".** Plural of `usted`; word ends in a consonant, so add `-es`
  (`usted → ustedes`), not just `-s`.
