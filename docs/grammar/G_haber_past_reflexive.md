# Bundle G — Haber-past + reflexive intro (Lessons 21–23)

The bundle where Spanish gets a **past tense** — and it's the easy one. You learn the
helper *haber* (he/has/ha/hemos/han), snap a **past participle** (*hablado, perdido*) onto
the back of it, and you can suddenly say "I have spoken / we have lost / have you
celebrated?" You also meet the full set of **subject pronouns** (*yo, tú, él…*) and tidy up
where the little **object pronouns** (*me, te, lo…*) sit — including the reflexive trick
*me he perdido* ("I have got lost" = "I have lost myself").

Everything here is built from three moving parts that you snap together:

```
[ little word? ]   [ haber ]   [ past participle ]
   me / lo / no       he           perdido
   (before haber)   (helper)     (the action, -ado / -ido)
```

> **Grammar terms** appear in *(italics)* next to the plain label, e.g. **past helper**
> *(auxiliary verb)*. Plain label first so it reads like Mad Libs; the real term is there
> when you want it. **Slots** are written in `[brackets]` — fill them in.

---

## 1. Word groups

Words sorted by **the job they do in a sentence**, not by dictionary order. Drill the group, not the word.

### Past helper *haber* *(auxiliary verb: haber + past participle)* — start the past, mean "I/you/he… have ___"
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `he`    | I have        | the /e/ side — same trick as `sé` from *saber* |
| `has`   | you (informal) have | /a/ side **+s** for informal "you" |
| `ha`    | he/she/it has, you (formal) have | bare /a/ side |
| `hemos` | we have       | /e/ side; we = plural of I |
| `han`   | they / you-guys have | /a/ side **+n** for plural |

Just two vowels — /a/ and /e/ — give you the whole verb. The `h` is **silent**: *he* = /e/, *ha* = /a/.

### Subject pronouns *(subject / "big" pronouns)* — emphasis or clarity only
| Spanish | English | when |
|---------|---------|------|
| `yo`    | I | stress *who*; otherwise `-o`/`he` already says it |
| `tú`    | you (informal) | |
| `él` / `ella` | he / she | |
| `usted` | you (formal) | from *vuestra merced*, "your honor"; **flexible** word order |
| `nosotros` / `nosotras` | we (masc-mixed / all-fem) | pronounce /s/, never *nozotros* |
| `ellos` / `ellas` | they (masc-mixed / all-fem) | `ll` = /y/ (*eyos*) in most dialects |
| `ustedes` | you guys / you (formal plural) | consonant + `-es`, not just `-s` |

### Object pronouns *(object / "small" pronouns; `me/te/nos` double as reflexive)* — go **before** *haber*
| Spanish | English | example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| `me` | me / myself     | `me he perdido` (I've got lost) |
| `te` | you / yourself  | `te has perdido` (you've got lost) |
| `lo` / `la` | him-it / her-it | `lo he terminado` (I've finished it) |
| `los` / `las` | them (masc / fem) | `los he terminado` (I've finished them) |
| `nos` | us / ourselves  | `nos hemos perdido` (we've got lost) |

`lo` is the **base / neuter** object word (use it for a situation or idea with no gender):
change `o → a` for feminine, add `-s` for plural. The little word **never splits** the
`haber`+participle pair.

### `-ma` nouns *(Greek-origin masculine nouns)* — look feminine, are masculine
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `el problema` | the problem | NOT *la problema* |
| `el sistema`  | the system  | |
| `el programa` | the program | |
| `el aroma`    | the aroma   | |
| `el panorama` | the panorama | |
| `el clima`    | the climate | |

The `-a` usually says feminine, but these came through Greek (neuter → masculine), so they
take **`el`**. Contrast `la casa` (the house) — a regular `-a` feminine.

### Little words
| Spanish | English | term |
|---------|---------|------|
| `no`    | not (goes in front of *haber*) | negation |
| `por`   | for (a period of time) / per | preposition |
| `dónde` | where | interrogative |
| `por qué` | why | interrogative |
| `mucho` | a lot / much | quantifier |

## The past-participle factory — the big rule of this bundle

Not a sentence structure, but it fills the `[participle]` slot for every drill below.

```
-ar verb   →   drop -ar, add -ado   →   participle
 hablar         hablado                  (spoken)
 celebrar       celebrado                (celebrated)
 tomar          tomado                   (taken)

-er / -ir verb →   drop -er/-ir, add -ido   →   participle
 vender             vendido                    (sold)
 perder             perdido                    (lost)
 venir              venido                     (come — hence bien·venido!)
```

`-ar → -ado`, everything else `→ -ido`. (In the *whole* language, `-er` and `-ir` verbs
differ in exactly one spot — the "we" form, `-emos` vs `-imos` — and the participle isn't
it.) This "have-past" is used **far more** in Spanish than in English; when in doubt, reach for it.

## 2. Sentence structures (drill these)

Each structure: a **shape** line (the only "code"), the slots you can swap, worked examples, then a **Drill** the student finishes out loud or on paper.

---

### S1 — "I have ___ed" *(present perfect: haber + past participle)*
**shape:** `[haber] + [participle]`

- haber: `he` · `has` · `ha` · `hemos` · `han`
- participle: `hablado` · `celebrado` · `tomado` · `vendido` · `perdido` · `terminado`

```
he hablado       I have spoken
hemos vendido    we have sold
ha hablado       he/she has spoken
han hablado      they have spoken
he tomado        I have taken
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** I have spoken · we have sold · they have lost · he has finished · I have taken

---

### S2 — Make it negative: "I haven't ___ed" *(negation)*
**shape:** `no + [haber] + [participle]`

`no` sits **in front of *haber***, exactly like English. Nothing else moves.

```
no he hablado       I haven't spoken
no has celebrado    you haven't celebrated
```

**Drill:** I haven't spoken · you haven't celebrated · we haven't lost

---

### S3 — Hook a pronoun on the front: "I have ___ed it / them" *(proclitic object pronoun)*
**shape:** `[pronoun] + [haber] + [participle]`

With `haber`, the little word goes **BEFORE *haber*** — never between *haber* and the
participle. The two changed words (`he` + `terminado`) sit together untouched.

| build | result | English |
|-------|--------|---------|
| lo + he + terminado | `lo he terminado` | I have finished it |
| los + he + terminado | `los he terminado` | I have finished them |
| lo + hemos + anticipado | `lo hemos anticipado` | we have anticipated it |
| lo + hemos + perdido | `lo hemos perdido` | we have lost it |

NOT *hemos lo anticipado*. **Drill:** I have finished it · I have finished them · we have anticipated it · we have lost it

---

### S4 — Reflexive past: "I have got lost" *(reflexive: me/te/nos + haber + participle)*
**shape:** `[me/te/nos] + [haber] + [participle]`

English "I got lost"; Spanish says "I have lost **myself**." Same logic as *me preparo*
("I ready myself" = I get ready). The reflexive `me/te/nos` slots in just like any little
word — before *haber*. (Spoken fast, the /e/ of `me` blends into the /e/ of `he`: *me he* → smooth *me-* .)

```
me he perdido     I've got lost     (I have lost myself)
te has perdido    you've got lost
nos hemos perdido we've got lost
```

`tú has perdido` = you have lost (something); `te has perdido` = you have got *yourself*
lost. The little word `te` is the whole difference.

**Drill:** I've got lost · you've got lost · we've got lost

---

### S5 — Build a question: "Have you ___ed?" *(yes/no & wh- question)*
**shape:** `¿([wh-word]) ([pronoun]) + [haber] + [participle]?`

No word-order flip needed — wrap it in `¿…?`, add `no` or a wh-word out front.

| sentence | English |
|----------|---------|
| `¿Has celebrado?` | Have you celebrated? |
| `¿No has celebrado?` | Haven't you celebrated? |
| `¿Por qué no has celebrado?` | Why haven't you celebrated? |
| `¿Dónde lo has celebrado?` | Where have you celebrated it? |
| `¿Has sentido el aroma?` | Have you smelt the aroma? |

**Drill:** Have you celebrated? · Haven't you celebrated? · Why haven't you celebrated? · Where have you celebrated it?

---

### S6 — Add emphasis with a subject pronoun *(overt subject pronoun for contrast)*
**shape:** `[subject] + [sentence]`

Only when you want to stress **who**, or to disambiguate `ha` (he/she/it/you-formal). Word order matches English.

```
he perdido          I have lost            (neutral)
yo he perdido       *I* have lost
tú has perdido      *you* have lost
él ha perdido       *he* has lost   (vs ella ha perdido — *she* has lost)
yo me he perdido    *I* have got lost      (subject + reflexive stack)
```

**Drill:** *I* have lost · *you* have lost · *we* have got lost · *she* has lost

---

### Paradigm — *haber* (present), the engine of every drill above
| person | form | "have ___" example | English |
|--------|------|--------------------|---------|
| yo (I)            | `he`    | he perdido     | I have lost |
| tú (you informal) | `has`   | has perdido    | you have lost |
| él/ella/usted     | `ha`    | ha perdido     | he/she/you have lost |
| nosotros (we)     | `hemos` | hemos perdido  | we have lost |
| ellos/ellas/ustedes | `han` | han perdido    | they/you-guys have lost |

Two vowels (/a/, /e/), one silent `h`. Verbs take **`-mos`** for "we" (`hemos`), never `-s` — `-s`/`-n` are the noun/adjective plural and the *informal-you* / *plural* markers.

## 3. Same idea, said other ways

- **Two "haves":** `tener` = to possess (*tengo un coche*), and `tener que` = "have to"; `haber` = the "I have eaten / I have gone" helper that builds this past. Different jobs, never swap them.
- **`he` and `sé`:** the *only* two verbs whose I-form drops to a bare vowel-stub like this — *haber → he*, *saber → sé*. Learn them as a pair.
- **`comprender` = `entender`:** both mean "to understand" — use either (*¿Has entendido el problema?*).
- **`usted` floats:** `usted lo ve` **or** `lo ve usted` — the formal subject moves freely; but `lo` does **not** — it stays glued before the verb.
- **`tú` vs `te`:** English never changes "you," but Spanish does — `tú ves` (you see) vs `te ve` (he sees you), just like he/him.

---

## 4. Emphasis & bends

- **Drop the subject.** `he` already means "I have," so `yo` is for stress or to clear up `ha`. Default to `he perdido`, not `yo he perdido`, unless you're contrasting.
- **Little word lives *before* `haber`.** `lo he terminado`, `me he perdido`, `no he hablado` — never between `haber` and the participle. The helper + participle pair is sacred and unsplittable.
- **`-ar → -ado`, the rest `→ -ido`.** The single spelling rule for participles. `hablado`, `celebrado`, `tomado` vs `vendido`, `perdido`, `venido`.
- **`-ma` = `el`.** Don't trust the `-a`: `el problema`, `el sistema`, `el clima`. Greek strikes again.
- **Reflexive is just a little word.** Adding `me/te/nos` turns "have lost" into "have got (myself) lost." Same slot, before `haber`.

## Carried-in lexicon (from earlier bundles)

- `no` — front-loaded negation, now sitting before `haber` (Bundle A/B). Same instinct as `no quiero`.
- Object pronouns `me, te, lo, los` first met gluing to a to-form verb's tail (Bundle B, S3); with `haber` they jump to the **front** instead — same words, new position.
- The `-ción → -ar` word factory (Bundle B) feeds participles: *anticipación → anticipar → anticipado*, *celebración → celebrar → celebrado*.
- `te quiero` ("I love you," literally "I want you") — the `te` object word reused here as the reflexive `te`.
- e-splitting (`tempo → tiempo`, like `bien`) and feminine `-ión` endings (`anticipación, celebración`) carry straight in.
