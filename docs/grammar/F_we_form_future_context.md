# Bundle F — We-form + *ir* + future-via-context (Lessons 18–20)

The bundle where the verb system *closes the loop*. You add the **we** form (so all five
persons are now on the table), tame the doubly-irregular **ir** ("to go") by working
backwards from `vamos`, and learn the trick that gets you the future for free: the present
tense plus a **time word** (*mañana, esta noche, pronto*). Object pronouns also learn a new
seat — **in front of** a changed verb.

Everything here snaps onto the engine you already have:

```
[ subject ]   [ (pronoun) ]   [ verb (changed) ]   [ time word ]
   (drop it)     la / nos          vivimos             mañana
                 (before verb)    (-amos/-emos/-imos)  (= future)
```

> **Grammar terms** appear in *(italics)* next to the plain label, e.g. **changed verb**
> *(conjugated finite verb)*. Plain label first so it reads like Mad Libs; the real term is
> there when you want it. **Slots** are written in `[brackets]` — fill them in.

---

## 1. Word groups

Words sorted by **the job they do in a sentence**, not by dictionary order. Drill the group, not the word.

### We-form endings *(first-person plural)* — the "we ___" tail
| family | ending | example | English |
|--------|--------|---------|---------|
| `-ar` | **`-amos`** | `hablamos` | we speak |
| `-er` | **`-emos`** | `entendemos` | we understand |
| `-ir` | **`-imos`** | `vivimos` | we live |

The **only** place `-er` and `-ir` split apart in the whole language. Everywhere else they're identical (see rule box below).

### *ir* — to go *(irregular verb)* — built backwards from `vamos`
| Spanish | English | who |
|---------|---------|-----|
| `voy` | I go / I'm going | I |
| `vas` | you go | you (informal) |
| `va`  | he/she/it goes, you (formal) go | he/she/it/usted |
| `vamos` | we go / let's go | we |
| `van` | they go, you guys go | they/ustedes |

`ir` is two letters and doubly weird — `voy` comes from one old verb, `vamos/va/vas/van` from another. Don't memorize that; just hold `voy` ("I'm going on a *voy*age") and `vamos`, then strip the ending off `vamos` to find the rest.

### Object pronouns *(direct object · reflexive)* — go **before** a changed verb
| Spanish | English | example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| `me`  | me      | `me ves` (you see me) |
| `te`  | you (informal) | `te veo` (I see you) |
| `lo`  | him / it (masc.) | `lo veo` (I see him) |
| `la`  | her / it (fem.)  | `la veo` (I see her) |
| `los` | them (masc./mixed) | `los vemos` |
| `las` | them (fem.) | `las ves` |
| `nos` | us / ourselves / each other | `nos vemos` (see you) |

Singular `lo/la` → plural `los/las` is just **+s**. `nos` carries all three of "us / ourselves / each other".

### Future-context words *(temporal adverbs)* — turn a present verb into the future
| Spanish | English | literal |
|---------|---------|---------|
| `mañana` | tomorrow / morning | |
| `más tarde` | later | "more late" |
| `esta noche` | tonight | "this night" |
| `pronto` | soon | English "pronto" |
| `la semana que viene` | next week | "the week that comes" |

Add any of these and a plain present verb means the **future** — same as English "I see her tomorrow."

### Question & location words
| Spanish | English | term |
|---------|---------|------|
| `dónde` | where | interrogative |
| `¿a dónde?` | (to) where | `a` = "to" + dónde |
| `aquí` | here (Spain) | adverb |
| `acá` | here (Latin America) | adverb |

### Little words
| Spanish | English | term |
|---------|---------|------|
| `siempre` | always | adverb (flexible position) |
| `y` | and | conjunction (single letter) |
| `que` | that / which | relative / linker |
| `tengo que` | I have to | obligation phrase |

## "We" form, `ir`, and future — the three rules of this bundle

Not sentence structures, but the machinery the drills below run on.

**1. We-form endings depend on the verb family.** `-ar` → `-amos`, `-er` → `-emos`, `-ir` → `-imos`. This is the **only** spot `-er`/`-ir` diverge.

```
hablar  → hablamos    (we speak)
entender→ entendemos  (we understand — NOT entienden; we vs they differ)
vivir   → vivimos     (we live)
```

A split-vowel verb (*e→ie*, *o→ue*) un-splits in the we-form, because the stress moves off the root: *prefiero* but *preferimos*; *viene* but *venimos*.

**2. Work backwards to find a form — never guess.** Reduce to the smallest known piece, find it, build up. From `vamos` you see the `/a/` and recover `va, vas, van`. "No guessing at all — it's the only rule."

**3. Present + time word = future.** No new tense needed. `La veo` = "I see her"; `La veo mañana` = "I'm seeing her tomorrow." `vamos a` + to-form is the explicit "we're going to ___".

## 2. Sentence structures (drill these)

Each structure: a **shape** line (the only "code"), the slots you can swap, worked examples, then a **Drill** the student finishes out loud or on paper.

---

### S1 — "We ___" *(first-person plural)*
**shape:** `[verb-stem]+[-amos / -emos / -imos]`

Pick the ending by the verb's family — that's the whole move.

- `-ar`: `hablamos` · `tomamos`
- `-er`: `entendemos` · `perdemos` · `vendemos` · `vemos`
- `-ir`: `vivimos` · `venimos` · `preferimos`

```
hablamos        we speak
vivimos         we live
preferimos      we prefer
perdemos        we lose
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** we live · we understand · we prefer · we come · we lose

---

### S2 — Build all of `ir` from `vamos` *(irregular verb, worked backwards)*
**shape:** `[voy / vas / va / vamos / van]`

Hold `voy` and `vamos`; strip `vamos` to its `/a/` and the rest fall out.

```
voy        I go / I'm going
vamos      we go / let's go
va         he/she goes
vas        you go
van         they go
```

**Drill:** I'm going · we go · they go · you (informal) go · he goes

---

### S3 — "I want / can / must / have to go" *(modal/obligation + infinitive)*
**shape:** `[helper] + ir`

Plug the bare `ir` into helpers you already own. `tengo que` is the softer "have to"; `debo` is the heavier "must".

| build | result | English |
|-------|--------|---------|
| quiero + ir | `quiero ir` | I want to go |
| puedo + ir | `puedo ir` | I can go |
| debo + ir | `debo ir`  | I must go |
| tengo que + ir | `tengo que ir` | I have to go |

```
tengo que ir ahora        I have to go now
tengo que ir esta noche   I have to go tonight
```

**Drill:** I want to go · I can go · I must go · I have to go now · I have to go tonight

---

### S4 — Pronoun **in front** of a changed verb *(proclitic object pronoun)*
**shape:** `[pronoun] + [verb-changed]`

With a *changed* (conjugated) verb the little word sits **in front**: `la veo`, not *veo la*. (On a to-form verb it still glues to the tail — see S6.)

| sentence | English |
|----------|---------|
| `la veo` | I see her |
| `lo veo` | I see him / it |
| `nos vemos` | we see each other / see you |
| `las ves` | you see them (fem.) |

```
la veo más tarde     I'm seeing her later
las vemos pronto     we're seeing them (fem.) soon
```

**Drill:** I see her · we see him · you see them (fem.) · I see you

---

### S5 — Future via context: present + time word *(temporal adverb signalling future)*
**shape:** `[pronoun]+[verb-changed] + [time word]`

No new tense. The time word does all the future-work, exactly like English "I see her tonight."

- time word: `mañana` · `más tarde` · `esta noche` · `pronto` · `la semana que viene`

```
la veo esta noche              I'm seeing her tonight
la vemos pronto                we're seeing her soon
hablamos la semana que viene   let's speak next week
```

**Drill:** I'm seeing her later · we're seeing them soon · you're seeing her tomorrow · let's speak next week

---

### S6 — "Going to ___" + where the pronoun can sit *(periphrastic future; clitic placement)*
**shape:** `[ir-form] a + [verb-toform]+[pronoun]`  **OR**  `[pronoun] + [ir-form] a + [verb-toform]`

`vamos a verla` = "we're going to see her." With the to-form the pronoun can **glue to its tail** *or* **float to the front of the `ir`-form** — both correct.

| sentence | English |
|----------|---------|
| `vamos a verla` | we're going to see her |
| `¿vas a verlo pronto?` | are you going to see him soon? |
| `¿vas a vernos pronto?` = `¿nos vas a ver pronto?` | are you going to see us soon? |

```
vamos a verla              we're going to see her
¿vas a verlo pronto?       are you going to see him soon?
```

**Drill:** we're going to see her · are you going to see him soon? · are you going to see us soon? (both placements)

---

### S7 — Stack negative + question + "why" *(layered negation/interrogative)*
**shape:** `¿por qué + no + [pronoun]+[verb] + [time word]?`

Build it in layers: state it, drop `no` in front, wrap `¿…?`, then prepend `por qué`.

```
las ves pronto              you see them soon
no las ves pronto           you don't see them soon
¿no las ves pronto?         aren't you seeing them soon?
¿por qué no las ves pronto? why aren't you seeing them soon?
```

**Drill:** you see them soon → aren't you seeing them soon? → why aren't you seeing them soon? · why aren't you going to see us soon?

---

### S8 — Ask & answer "where" *(interrogative `dónde` / `a dónde`)*
**shape:** `¿(a) dónde + [verb-changed]?`  ·  answer with `aquí` / `acá`

`a` = "to", so `¿a dónde vas?` asks the *destination*. Bury a question inside a statement with `quiero saber dónde…`.

```
¿dónde viven?                 where do they live?
¿a dónde vas?                 where are you going?
vivo aquí                     I live here
quiero saber dónde viven      I want to know where they live
```

**Drill:** where do they live? · where are you going? · I live here · I want to know where they live

## 3. Same idea, said other ways

- **`vamos`** = "we go" **and** "let's go." Same for `hablamos` = "we speak" / "let's speak" — the we-form doubles as the "let's ___" command.
- **`nos vemos`** = literally "we see each other," used as a casual goodbye ("see you"), often + `ciao`. The future is *implicit* — no time word needed.
- **`aquí` vs `acá`** — "here" in Spain vs Latin America; both understood everywhere.
- **`tengo que ir` vs `debo ir`** — both "I gotta go," but `tengo que` is lighter than the heavier `debo` ("I must").
- **Future, two ways:** `la veo mañana` (present + time word) and `voy a verla` (going-to) both point at the future; the time word is what makes the plain present read as future.

---

## 4. Emphasis & bends

- **Drop the subject.** The ending already names the person — `vivimos`, not *nosotros vivimos*. Bring back an overt word only to **contrast**: `pero él vive aquí` ("but HE lives here").
- **Pronoun seat depends on the verb.** Changed verb → pronoun **in front** (`la veo`). To-form verb → pronoun **on the tail** (`verla`), or floated in front of the `ir`-helper (`nos vas a ver`).
- **`siempre` floats.** `siempre perdemos` or `perdemos siempre` — same meaning, position is free.
- **No guessing.** Shrink the thought to its smallest piece, find that piece, build up. Don't memorize whole sentences.
- **Watch the vowel-harmony trap.** Starting *vivir* you may drift to "veve" because the ending's *e* invites a matching *e*. Stop, peel `-ir` off `vivir`, you have `viv-`, add `-e` → `vive`.
