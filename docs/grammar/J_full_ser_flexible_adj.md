# Bundle J — Full *ser* + flexible adjectives (Lessons 31–33)

The bundle where the other "to be" comes online. You already had *estar* (states);
now you get the complete present tense of **ser** (characteristics) — *soy, eres, es,
somos, son* — plus the big idea that one Spanish adjective can mean two English things
depending on which "to be" you pick (*aburrido* = boring **or** bored, *listo* = smart
**or** ready).

Everything here is built from three moving parts you snap together:

```
[ subject ]   [ ser / estar ]   [ adjective ]
   yo            soy / estoy        feliz
 (optional)    (characteristic     (agrees in
               vs. state)          gender/number)
```

> **Grammar terms** appear in *(italics)* next to the plain label, e.g. **to-form verb**
> *(infinitive)*. Plain label first so it reads like Mad Libs; the real term is there when
> you want it. **Slots** are written in `[brackets]` — fill them in.

---

## 1. Word groups

Words sorted by **the job they do in a sentence**, not by dictionary order. Drill the group, not the word.

### *ser* forms *(present tense of ser; "characteristic" copula)* — for who/what something **is**
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `soy`   | I am             | `-oy` ending, like *estoy, voy, doy* |
| `eres`  | you (informal) are | the odd one — anchor it to *es*, and to *¿De dónde eres?* |
| `es`    | he / she / it is, you (formal) are | |
| `somos` | we are           | work backwards from *son*: drop `-n`, add `-mos` |
| `son`   | they / you-all are | |

Only **four** verbs in all of Spanish end their "I" form in `-oy`: *voy, doy, estoy, soy*.

### Flexible adjectives *(adjectives that shift meaning with ser vs. estar)* — same word, two English meanings
| Spanish | with *ser* (characteristic) | with *estar* (state) |
|---------|------------------------------|----------------------|
| `aburrido` | boring        | bored        |
| `listo`    | smart / clever | ready        |
| `bueno`    | good (person / at something) | attractive / looks hot |
| `feliz`    | a happy person | happy (right now) |
| `libre`    | a free person  | free (from work, exams) |

English uses two different words; Spanish uses one and you pick *ser* or *estar*.

### Picky adjectives *(adjectives locked to one copula)* — no choice
| Spanish | English | locked to |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| `ocupado`    | busy     | **estar** only (*soy ocupado* is bad Spanish) |
| `preocupado` | worried  | usually **estar** (state); *ser* = worry-prone person |
| `casado`     | married  | dialects disagree — use the *persona* trick |

### Subject pronouns *(subject pronouns)* — emphasis only
| Spanish | English |
|---------|---------|
| `yo` | I |
| `tú` | you (informal) |
| `él / ella` | he / she |
| `usted` | you (formal) |
| `nosotros / nosotras` | we (m / f) |
| `ellos / ellas` | they (m / f) |
| `ustedes` | you-all |

Drop them by default — the verb ending already says who. Add one only to stress *who*.

### Little words
| Spanish | English | term |
|---------|---------|------|
| `de`    | from / of | preposition |
| `tan`   | so | adverb of degree |
| `cómo`  | how / what … like | interrogative |
| `dónde` | where | interrogative |
| `persona` | person (always feminine) | the safety-net noun |

## Bundle-specific rules

**The `-oy` "I" form.** *soy* joins the tiny `-oy` club (*voy, doy, estoy, soy*). Everything else
takes a plain `-o`. There are only these four — memorize the club, not a rule.

**No Spanish word starts with `st-`, `sp-`, or `s + consonant`.** Spanish slaps an `e-` on the front:

```
state       → estar          school   → escuela     (and the o splits to ue)
Spanish     → español        special  → especial
Spain       → España         spatial  → espacial
student     → estudiante     strict   → estricto
                             spectacular → espectacular
```

**`ustedes`.** It's the plural of *usted* (formal), but across most of the Spanish-speaking world
*ustedes* is just everyday "you guys" — not formal at all once plural. Only parts of Spain keep it formal.

**Adjective agreement.** `-o` adjectives flip to `-a` for feminine and add `-s` for plural
(*aburrido → aburrida → aburridos*). Adjectives ending in **`-e`** (like *libre*) do **not** change
for gender — only for number.

**The *persona* safety net.** When unsure whether an adjective takes *ser* or *estar*, route it through
the feminine noun *persona*. *Soy una persona libre / casada / ocupada / feliz* is always correct —
even a man says *persona* (it's feminine for everyone), because now the adjective describes *persona*,
a clean *ser* case.

**ser paradigm — present tense**

| person | form | English |
|--------|------|---------|
| yo | `soy` | I am |
| tú | `eres` | you (informal) are |
| él / ella / usted | `es` | he / she is, you (formal) are |
| nosotros/as | `somos` | we are |
| ellos/as / ustedes | `son` | they / you-all are |

## 2. Sentence structures (drill these)

Each structure: a **shape** line (the only "code"), the slots you can swap, worked examples, then a **Drill** the student finishes out loud or on paper.

---

### S1 — "I am ___" with *ser* (characteristics) *(ser + noun/adjective)*
**shape:** `[ser-form] + [noun / adjective]`

- ser-form: `soy` · `eres` · `es` · `somos` · `son`
- after it: `estudiante` · `posible` · `humano`

```
soy estudiante      I am a student
es posible          it is possible
yo soy              I am (with emphasis)
ustedes son         you guys are
nosotras somos      we are (f)
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** I am a student · it is possible · they are (m) · we are (f) · you (informal) are

---

### S2 — Flexible adjective: *ser* vs *estar* flips the meaning *(meaning-bearing copula choice)*
**shape:** `[ser-form] + [adj]`  (characteristic)   vs.   `[estar-form] + [adj]`  (state)

Same adjective, two English words. *ser* = what someone **is like**; *estar* = how they **are right now**.

| ser (characteristic) | estar (state) |
|----------------------|---------------|
| `es aburrido` — he is boring | `está aburrido` — he is bored |
| `soy listo` — I am smart | `estoy listo` — I am ready |
| `es bueno` — he is good (person / at it) | `está bueno` — he is attractive |
| `soy feliz` — I'm a happy person | `estoy feliz` — I'm happy (now) |

**Drill:** he is boring · he is bored · I am smart · I am ready · he is attractive · I'm a happy person

---

### S3 — Agreement: flip the adjective for gender & number *(adjective agreement)*
**shape:** `[ser/estar-form] + [adj agreeing in gender/number]`

`-o` adjectives: `-o` (m) → `-a` (f) → add `-s` (plural). `-e` adjectives (*libre, feliz*) ignore gender.

```
es aburrida           she is boring
son aburridos         they are boring
están aburridos       they are bored
estamos listos        we are ready
somos personas ocupadas   we are busy people
soy una persona libre     I am a free person   (libre never changes for gender)
```

**Drill:** she is boring · they are boring · they are bored · we are ready · we are busy people · I am a free person

---

### S4 — "Where are you from?" *(de + dónde + ser)*
**shape:** `de + dónde + [ser-form]`

"From" is a **characteristic**, so it takes *ser*. Contrast with location *(estar)*, which drops the *de*.

```
¿De dónde es?        Where is he from? (formal)
¿De dónde eres?      Where are you from? (informal)
¿De dónde son?       Where are they from?
¿Dónde están?        Where are they? (location → estar, no de)
```

`¿De dónde eres?` is everywhere — let it cement the irregular *eres* in your ear.

**Drill:** where is he from? · where are you (informal) from? · where are they from? · where are they (located)?

---

### S5 — "What is he like?" vs "How is he doing?" *(cómo + ser/estar)*
**shape:** `cómo + [ser-form]`  (what … like)   vs.   `cómo + [estar-form]`  (how … doing)

```
¿Cómo es?                  What is he like?      (characteristic)
¿Cómo está?                How is he? / doing?   (state)
quiero saber cómo es       I want to know what he's like
quiero saber cómo está     I want to know how he's doing
```

`quiero saber` ("I want to know") slots a known helper in front of either question.

**Drill:** what is he like? · how is he doing? · I want to know what he's like · I want to know how he's doing

---

### S6 — "So ___" with *tan*, and emphasis with a subject pronoun *(intensifier tan; overt subject pronoun)*
**shape:** `[ser/estar-form] + tan + [adj]`   ·   `[subject] + [sentence]`

`tan` = "so", used just like English. A subject pronoun in front adds emphasis (it's optional).

```
estoy tan cansado          I am so tired
estoy tan aburrida         I am so bored
es tan aburrido            it is so boring
nosotros estamos listos    we are ready (emphatic)
eres muy lista             you are very smart
```

**Drill:** I am so tired · I'm so bored · it is so boring · *we* are ready (emphatic) · you are very smart

## 3. Same idea, said other ways

- **One adjective, two English words.** *aburrido* covers "boring" **and** "bored"; *listo* covers "smart"
  **and** "ready"; *bueno* covers "good" **and** "attractive". The *ser/estar* choice — not a second word —
  carries the difference.
- **Dodge a hard call with *persona*.** Unsure if it's *ser* or *estar*? Say *soy una persona ___* and the
  adjective just describes *persona* (clean *ser*). *Soy una persona ocupada / casada / feliz* always works.
- **`ser` itself can mean "being".** *un ser humano* = "a human being"; following the noun, *humano* acts
  like an adjective. *El humano* (the human) makes it a noun instead.
- **Two ways to anchor *eres*.** Relate it to *es*, or lean on *¿De dónde eres?* — you'll hear that
  sentence constantly.

---

## 4. Emphasis & bends

- **`ser/estar` is a tool, not an obstacle.** English forces a single "is"; Spanish lets you nail the
  nuance — *está bueno* (looks hot) vs *es bueno* (a good person). Lean into it.
- **Drop the subject.** The verb ending already says who; *yo, nosotros,* etc. are for stress only —
  *nosotros estamos listos* makes "*we* are ready" emphatic.
- **`e-` on the front.** Any English `st-/sp-/s+consonant` word becomes `est-/esp-/es-` in Spanish:
  *estudiante, español, escuela*. Bonus: the *o* in *school* splits to *ue* (*escuela*), same split as
  *bon → bueno*.
- **Agreement is regular.** `-o → -a` (feminine), `+s` (plural). `-e` adjectives (*libre, feliz*) skip the
  gender flip and only take `+s`.
- **`de` hides inside English.** *de* = "from / of" surfaces in borrowed words: *deliver* = *de* (from) +
  *liver* (free, *liberar*); *decipher* = *de* (from) + *cipher* (zero) → "go from zero".
