# Bundle V — Mood Expansion (Lessons 74–77)

The bundle where the **mood tense** *(subjunctive)* stops being a one-off trick and becomes a
whole toolkit. You collect new triggers — *para que* "so that", *donde/como/que/cuando* +
mood = "wherever/however/whatever/whenever", *hay/había* questioning whether a thing even
exists, and *es + adjective + que* "it's terrible/possible/important that…" — plus a third
stem-change pattern (**e → i**), the *seguir* family, and the irregular mood root *sea*.

The recurring move stays the same:

```
[ trigger ]   [ que / connector ]   [ verb → mood tense ]
  para           que                   vuelvas
es posible       que                   vayamos
  hay alguien    que                   hable
```

> **Grammar terms** appear in *(italics)* next to the plain label, e.g. **mood tense**
> *(subjunctive)*. Plain label first so it reads like Mad Libs; the real term is there when
> you want it. **Slots** are written in `[brackets]` — fill them in.

## 1. Word groups

Words sorted by **the job they do in a sentence**, not by dictionary order. Drill the group, not the word.

### e→i stem-changers *(stem-changing verbs, e→i type)* — the *e* turns into *i* when stressed (it does **not** split into *ie*)
| Spanish | English | I-form | note |
|---------|---------|--------|------|
| `elegir` | to choose | `elijo` | think "elect"; `elección` = election. g→j before o/a keeps the soft sound |
| `seguir` | to follow / continue / carry on | `sigo` | gu→g before o/a keeps the hard sound |
| `conseguir` | to get / manage to | `consigo` | `seguir` family — same e→i |
| `perseguir` | to chase | `persigo` | `seguir` family — same e→i |

Pattern: `elijo, eliges, elige, elegimos, eligen` — *i* everywhere except **nosotros**
(`elegimos`), where the stress lands elsewhere. `seguir`: `sigo, sigues, sigue, seguimos, siguen`.

### o→ue stem-changer carried into the mood *(stem-changing verb, o→ue type)*
| Spanish | English | present | mood |
|---------|---------|---------|------|
| `volver` | to return / come back / go back | `vuelvo, vuelves, vuelve, volvemos, vuelven` | `vuelva, vuelvas, vuelva, volvamos, vuelvan` |

The `o→ue` split survives into the mood tense — same shape, different ending. (Spanish
`revolver` = to stir, not to return.)

### Irregular mood roots *(irregular subjunctive stems)* — memorize these bases
| Spanish | from | English |
|---------|------|---------|
| `sea, seas, sea, seamos, sean` | `ser` | (mood) be |
| `vaya, vayas, vayamos, vayan` | `ir` | (mood) go |

`o sea` (literally "or it might be") is everyday filler — "I mean… / uhh…".

### Existence verb *(impersonal* haber*)* — "there is / there must be"
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `hay` | there is / there are | from `haber`; **never** changes for singular vs plural |
| `había` | there was / there were | past of `hay`; also invariable — resist `habían` |
| `tiene que haber` | there has to be / there must be | "be" here = **exist**, so `haber`, not ser/estar |
| `debería haber` | there should be | `debería` = should + `haber` = exist |

Quick test: if the "be" really means **exist** (and neither ser nor estar fits) → it's `haber`.

### The little word *también* and friends
| Spanish | English | term |
|---------|---------|------|
| `también` | also / as well | `tan + bien` (n→m before b); mirrors English "as well" |
| `tan` | so (`tan cansado` = so tired) | adverb |
| `así` | like this / like that | adverb |
| `alguien` | somebody | pronoun |
| `algo` | something | pronoun |
| `aquí` | here | adverb |
| `más tarde` | later | adverb phrase |
| `pronto` | soon | adverb |

### "For me": *por mí* vs *para mí* *(preposition contrast)*
| Spanish | English | use |
|---------|---------|-----|
| `por mí` | on my behalf / in my place | someone acts **instead of** me — `No quiero que elijas por mí` |
| `para mí` | for me | something **destined** for me |

## Bundle rules — the new mood triggers

These are what *fires* the mood tense in this bundle. Not sentence shapes themselves (those
are §2), but the switches that flip a verb into the mood.

- **`para que` + mood = "so that" / "in order that".** Whenever the doer changes, you must use
  `para que` + mood — you **cannot** shortcut to `para + infinitive`. `Hemos cocinado para que
  coman` = we've cooked so they eat. (`Hemos cocinado para comer` = …so *we* eat.)
- **`para` alone = "in order to"** when English "to" means "in order to" and the doer is the
  same. (Casual speech shortens `para` → `pa`: `pa que vuelvas`.)
- **`donde / como / que / cuando` + mood = "wherever / however / whatever / whenever".** The
  `-ever` of English is **built into** the mood-tense flip. `donde quieres` (a known place) →
  `donde quieras` (any place).
- **`hay` / `había` + `que` + mood questions whether the thing exists.** If you're not sure it
  exists, the verb after `que` goes mood. If you're stating it as fact, stay in plain present
  (or the line-past for a past characteristic).
- **`tiene que haber` / `debería haber` + `que` + mood** — "there has to be / should be
  something **that** [mood]", because the something isn't known to exist yet.
- **`es` + adjective + `que` + mood.** `Es terrible que…`, `Es posible que…`, `Es importante
  que…`, `No es necesario que…`. The `que` is the trigger. But **`si` does not trigger it**:
  `Es terrible si comes esto` stays plain.
- **`qué` + adjective + `que` + mood** — the exclamation "how [adjective] that…!" `¡Qué fuerte
  que esté aquí!` = how heavy/hardcore that he's here!
- **`haz` is the irregular positive command of `hacer`** (not `hace`). `Hazlo` = do it. It often
  sits in front of the wherever/however/whatever structures below.
- **`hecho` is the irregular past participle of `hacer`** (after `haber`): `He hecho` = I have
  done. `Lo he hecho para que vuelvas` = I've done it so you come back. (`hecho` alone = "fact".)

## 2. Sentence structures (drill these)

Each structure: a **shape** line (the only "code"), the slots you can swap, worked examples, then a **Drill** the student finishes out loud or on paper.

---

### S1 — "so that ___" *(purpose clause: para que + subjunctive)*
**shape:** `[clause] + para que + [verb-mood]`

- clause: `hemos cocinado` · `lo he hecho` · `lo han hecho`
- mood verb: `coman` · `vuelvas` · `volvamos`

Use this **whenever the doer changes**. You cannot collapse it into `para + infinitive`.

```
Hemos cocinado para que coman.            We've cooked so that they eat.
Lo he hecho para que vuelvas.             I've done it so that you come back.
Lo han hecho para que volvamos.           They've done it so that we come back.
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** We've cooked so that you come back · I've done everything so that you also come back · They've done it so that we eat

---

### S2 — "in order to ___" (same doer) *(purpose clause: para + infinitive)*
**shape:** `[clause] + para + [verb-toform]`

When the doer **doesn't** change, drop `que` and use the to-form. No mood tense.

```
Hemos cocinado para comer.                We've cooked (in order) to eat.
Nos hemos encontrado para hablar.         We've met up (in order) to speak.
```

**Drill:** We've cooked to eat · We've met up to speak · I've done it to come back

---

### S3 — "wherever / however / whatever / whenever" *(indefinite relative + subjunctive)*
**shape:** `[command/verb] + [donde|como|lo que|cuando] + [verb-mood]`

Swap the connector; the `-ever` rides on the mood flip. Known thing → plain present; any/unknown thing → mood.

| build | result | English |
|-------|--------|---------|
| hazlo + donde + quieras | `hazlo donde quieras` | do it wherever you want |
| hazlo + como + te guste | `hazlo como te guste` | do it however you like |
| haz + lo que + prefieras | `haz lo que prefieras` | do whatever you prefer |
| te sigo + adonde + vayas | `te sigo adonde vayas` | I'll follow you wherever you go |
| — + cuando + necesites | `cuando necesites` | whenever you need |

Contrast: `donde quieres` = where you want (a known place) · `donde quieras` = wherever (any place).

**Drill:** do it wherever you want · do it however you like · do whatever you prefer · I'll follow you wherever you go · whenever you need

---

### S4 — Questioning whether it exists: "is there anybody who ___?" *(hay + que + subjunctive)*
**shape:** `hay/había + [noun] + que + [verb-mood?]`

If you're **unsure** the thing exists, mood. If you're **stating** it exists, plain present (or line-past for a past trait).

| sentence | English | why |
|----------|---------|-----|
| `¿Hay alguien aquí que hable español?` | Is there anybody here who (might) speak Spanish? | existence in question → mood |
| `Hay alguien aquí que quiere hablar con usted.` | There's somebody here who wants to speak with you. | real, known person → present |
| `Hay dos personas aquí que hablan español.` | There are two people here who speak Spanish. | stated as fact → present |
| `Había dos personas aquí que hablaban español.` | There were two people here who spoke Spanish. | past characteristic → line-past |

**Drill:** Is there anybody here who speaks Spanish? · There's somebody here who wants to speak with you · There were two people here who spoke Spanish

---

### S5 — "there has to be / should be something that ___" *(tiene que haber / debería haber + subjunctive)*
**shape:** `tiene que haber / debería haber + [noun] + que + [verb-mood]`

The "be" = **exist**, so it's `haber`. The thing isn't known to exist yet → mood after `que`.

```
Tiene que haber algo que puedas hacer.    There has to be something you can do.
Debería haber algo para comer.            There should be something to eat.
```

**Drill:** There has to be something you can do · There should be something to eat · There must be somebody who speaks Spanish

---

### S6 — "it's ___ that you ___" *(es + adjective + que + subjunctive)*
**shape:** `es + [adjective] + que + [verb-mood]`

- adjective: `terrible` · `posible` · `importante` · (negated) `no es necesario`
- The `que` is the trigger. `si` instead of `que` keeps it **plain** (no mood).

| sentence | English |
|----------|---------|
| `Es terrible que comas esto.` | It's terrible that you eat this. |
| `Es posible que vayamos más tarde.` | It's possible we (might) go later. |
| `Es importante que lo encontremos pronto.` | It's important that we find it soon. |
| `No es necesario que responda.` | It's not necessary that I respond. |
| `Es terrible si comes esto.` | It's terrible **if** you eat this. *(si → no mood)* |

Equivalent of the last negation: `No hace falta que responda` = I don't need to respond.

**Drill:** It's terrible that you eat this · It's possible we go later · It's important that we find it soon · It's not necessary that I respond

---

### S7 — Exclaiming with mood: "how ___ that ___!" *(qué + adjective + que + subjunctive)*
**shape:** `¡qué + [adjective] + que + [verb-mood]!`

`qué + adjective` = "how [adjective]" (`qué fuerte` = how heavy, `qué lindo` = how nice,
`qué bueno` = how good). Add `que` + mood for the full "how [adj] that…!".

```
¡Qué fuerte que esté aquí!                How hardcore that he's here!
Está bien que preguntes.                  It's okay that you ask.
```

`fuerte` = strong, but colloquially "hardcore / heavy / full-on"; `¡Qué fuerte!` = wow, heavy.

**Drill:** How hardcore that he's here! · It's okay that you ask · How incredible that he should come

## 3. Same idea, said other ways

- **`para que coman` vs `para comer`** — same English "to/so": use `para que` + mood when the
  doer changes, plain `para` + to-form when it's the same person.
- **`no es necesario que responda` = `no hace falta que responda`** — "it's not necessary that
  I respond" = "I don't need to respond." Two phrasings, one mood trigger.
- **`donde quieres` vs `donde quieras`** — "where you want" (a set place) vs "wherever you want"
  (any place). The `-s` ending alone flips known → open-ended.
- **`comes lo que prefieras` vs `come lo que prefieras`** — the statement (present `comes`) sounds
  softer/sweeter than the bare order (`come`, dropped `-s`). Formal order goes full mood:
  `coma lo que prefiera` / plural `coman lo que prefieran`.
- **`sea lo que sea`** — "whatever it is / whatever will be," the mood doubled around itself.

## 4. Emphasis & bends

- **`que` is the usual fuse.** Across this whole bundle the mood tense tends to fire right after
  `que` — `para que`, `es posible que`, `hay alguien que`, `qué fuerte que`. Spot the `que`, suspect the mood.
- **`si` does not fire it.** `Es terrible si comes esto` stays plain. `que` triggers; `si` doesn't.
- **Existence is a choice, not a rule.** With `hay`/`había`, *you* decide: questioning the thing's
  existence → mood; asserting it → present (or line-past for a past trait). The grammar follows your stance.
- **e→i ≠ e→ie.** `elegir`/`seguir` turn *e* into a plain *i* when stressed; they do **not** split
  into *ie*. Nosotros (`elegimos`, `seguimos`) keeps the bare *e* because the stress moves off it.
- **The mood tense is a value system.** Spanish marks subjectivity and uncertainty because it
  *cares* to. It has structural limits (only certain slots) and meaning limits (only certain
  feelings), and real use varies by dialect and idiolect — listen actively rather than chase perfect rules.

## Carried-in lexicon (from earlier bundles)

- **Mood tense after `cuando` + future event** — `cuando necesites` reuses the L73 rule (mood for a not-yet-real future moment).
- **Irregular mood root `vaya/vayamos`** for `ir` first arrived in L73; reused here in `te sigo adonde vayas`, `es posible que vayamos`.
- **Stem-change patterns `e→ie` and `o→ue`** — `volver` (o→ue) carries its split into the mood (`vuelva`); this bundle adds the third pattern, **e→i**.
- **Reflexive `-se` to leave the doer unnamed** — `romperse` (`no quiero que se rompa` = I don't want it to break) and `encontrarse` (to meet up) reuse the earlier no-blame reflexive.
- **`haber` = "to exist"** and the irregular participle `hecho` after `haber` — extended here into `tiene que haber`, `debería haber`.
- **Line vs point in the past** — `hablaban` (line, a characteristic) vs `hablaron` (point, one act) decides which past fits the `que` clause in S4.
