# Bundle D — Regular present + -go family start (Lessons 11–13)

The bundle where verbs stop meaning only "I ___" and start meaning **he / she / you /
they** too. You learn the two verb families (*-ar* vs *-er/-ir*), the **key vowel** that
runs every ending, and the two-letter switches that change *who*: **-s** = "you", **-n** =
"they". You also get the little pronouns moving to the **front** of a changed verb, two
more word factories (*-tion → -ción*, *-ity → -idad*), and the first irregular cluster —
the **-go family** (*hago, tengo, vengo, salgo*).

Everything below hangs on one move: take the to-form, **chop the ending, add the right vowel +
tail** for the person you mean.

```
[ to-form ]  →  chop -ar/-er/-ir  →  key vowel (a / e)  →  +tail  →  changed verb
  hablar              habl-                  a                +n        hablan  (they speak)
  vender              vend-                  e                +s        vendes  (you sell)
```

> **Grammar terms** appear in *(italics)* next to the plain label, e.g. **changed verb**
> *(conjugated / finite verb)*. Plain label first so it reads like Mad Libs; the real term is
> there when you want it. **Slots** are written in `[brackets]` — fill them in.

---

## 1. Word groups

Words sorted by **the job they do in a sentence**, not by dictionary order. Drill the group, not the word.

### -ar verbs *(first-conjugation verbs; key vowel /a/)* — the big, **open** group; every new verb joins here
| Spanish | English | I-form |
|---------|---------|--------|
| `hablar`     | to speak (silent *h*)   | `hablo` |
| `tolerar`    | to tolerate             | `tolero` |
| `eliminar`   | to eliminate / delete   | `elimino` |
| `masticar`   | to chew                 | `mastico` |
| `colaborar`  | to collaborate          | `colaboro` |
| `contaminar` | to contaminate          | `contamino` |
| `comprar`    | to buy                  | `compro` |
| `llegar`     | to arrive               | `llego` |
| `esperar`    | to wait / to hope       | `espero` |
| `googlear`   | to google (a brand-new verb — joins *-ar*) | `googleo` |

New verbs always come in as **-ar** (*tuitear*, *facebookear*, *googlear*). It's the living group.

### -er / -ir verbs *(second/third-conjugation verbs; key vowel /e/)* — the **closed**, historic group
| Spanish | English | I-form |
|---------|---------|--------|
| `comer`  | to eat        | `como` |
| `vender` | to sell ("vending machine") | `vendo` |
| `morder` | to bite (cf. *mordant*) | `muerdo`* |
| `hacer`  | to do / make (silent *h*) | `hago`† |
| `poder`  | to be able / can | `puedo`† |

No new *-er/-ir* verbs are being minted. *(\*stem-changes and †irregulars arrive below — listed here only so you place the family.)*

### Object pronouns *(object / clitic pronouns)* — go **before** the changed verb
| Spanish | English | example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| `lo` / `los` | it / him · them (masc.) | `lo vendo` (I'm selling it) |
| `la` / `las` | it / her · them (fem.)  | `las vendo` (I'm selling them) |
| `me` | me / to me   | `me esperan` (they wait for me) |
| `te` | you (informal) | `te quiero` (I want you) |
| `nos`| us / to us   | `nos lo venden` (they sell it to us) |

Use **`la/las` for feminine things, not just people** — learners overuse `lo/los`. Plural is regular **+s**.

### Question / linking words
| Spanish | English | term |
|---------|---------|------|
| `qué`     | what  | interrogative |
| `por qué` | why   | interrogative |
| `cómo`    | how / like / as | interrogative (also = *como* "I eat") |
| `algo`    | something | indefinite (NOT a clitic — goes **after** the verb) |
| `mucho`   | much / a lot | adverb |
| `claro`   | clear / "of course" | adjective / filler |

## Endings, key vowels & the word factories — the big rules of this bundle

### The conjugation engine (present tense)
Chop `-ar/-er/-ir`, then add a tail built on the **key vowel**: `/a/` for *-ar*, `/e/` for *-er/-ir*.

| who | tail | *-ar* (hablar) | *-er* (vender) |
|-----|------|----------------|----------------|
| I *(1sg)*                  | `-o`        | `hablo`   | `vendo` |
| he/she/it, you-formal *(3sg)* | key vowel | `habla`   | `vende` |
| you informal *(2sg)*       | key vowel + `s` | `hablas` | `vendes` |
| they / you-guys *(3pl)*    | key vowel + `n` | `hablan` | `venden` |

Two tails that work on **every** verb type: **`-s` = "you" (informal)**, **`-n` = "they / you guys."**

> **Stress, not spelling, marks the past.** `tolero` (stress on *-le-*) = "I tolerate."
> `toleró` (stress on final *-o*) = "he tolerated." Build syllable by syllable: *e-li-mi-no*.

### Word factory 1 — `-tion → -ción → -ar`  (carried in from Bundle B)
`duración → durar`, `exageración → exagerar`, `masticación → masticar`, `colaboración → colaborar`.

### Word factory 2 — `-ity → -idad`  (new; always feminine, takes `la`)
`diversity → la diversidad`, `nationality → la nacionalidad`, `opportunity → la oportunidad`,
`clarity → la claridad`. Rip the ending off to guess the adjective: *claridad → claro*,
*generosidad → generoso*, *sinceridad → sincero*. (Exception: *común* "common" breaks the rule.)

### The -go family (irregular "I" forms)
A handful of common verbs **don't** just add `-o` — they pop a **`g`** in:

| to-form | I-form | English |
|---------|--------|---------|
| `hacer` | `hago`  | I do / make |
| `tener` | `tengo` | I have |
| `venir` | `vengo` | I come |
| `salir` | `salgo` | I leave / go out |

The most common verbs tend to be the most irregular. Their **other** persons stay regular: *hace, haces, hacen*.

## 2. Sentence structures (drill these)

Each structure: a **shape** line (the only "code"), the slots you can swap, worked examples, then a **Drill** the student finishes out loud or on paper.

---

### S1 — "I ___" with a changed verb, no helper *(simple present, 1sg)*
**shape:** `[verb-stem]+o`

Chop the to-form, add `-o`, shift the stress back one syllable. This is a full sentence on its own.

```
hablo      I speak / I'm speaking
vendo      I sell / I'm selling
espero     I wait / I hope
compro     I buy
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** I tolerate · I eat · I arrive · I collaborate

---

### S2 — Who's doing it: the -s / -n / key-vowel switch *(present paradigm, all persons)*
**shape:** `[verb-stem] + [key vowel](+s / +n)`

Same stem, swap the tail to change *who*.

| build | result | English |
|-------|--------|---------|
| habl- + a       | `habla`   | he/she speaks · you (formal) |
| habl- + a + s   | `hablas`  | you (informal) speak |
| habl- + a + n   | `hablan`  | they / you guys speak |
| vend- + e       | `vende`   | he/she sells |
| vend- + e + s   | `vendes`  | you (informal) sell |
| vend- + e + n   | `venden`  | they sell |

**Drill:** he chews · you (informal) chew · they collaborate · she sells · you (informal) sell · they eat

---

### S3 — Pronoun **before** the changed verb *(proclitic object pronoun)*
**shape:** `[pronoun] + [changed-verb]`

When the verb is changed (not a to-form), the little word goes **in front** — and that's the **only** spot if there's no to-form to grab.

```
lo quiero      I want it
lo vendo       I'm selling it
las vendo      I'm selling them (fem.)
me esperan     they wait for me   (no Spanish word for "for" needed)
lo hacen       they're doing it
```

**Drill:** I'm selling it · they're doing it · they wait for me · I want it · she's selling them (fem.)

---

### S4 — When there's a to-form too, you get a CHOICE *(proclitic vs enclitic placement)*
**shape:** `[pronoun] [helper] [verb-toform]`  **OR**  `[helper] [verb-toform]+[pronoun]`

Same meaning, two legal spots: float the pronoun to the front, or fuse it to the to-form's tail.

| front (proclitic) | tail (enclitic) | English |
|-------------------|-----------------|---------|
| `lo quiero ver`   | `quiero verlo`     | I want to see it |
| `lo quiero vender`| `quiero venderlo`  | I want to sell it |
| `lo debo invitar` | `debo invitarlo`   | I must invite him |

Rule: the choice **only exists when a to-form is present**. A bare changed verb forces the front (S3).

**Drill (give both forms):** I want to sell it · I want to see it · I must invite him

---

### S5 — Stacking two pronouns *(clitic cluster: indirect before direct)*
**shape:** `[me/te/nos] + [lo/la/los/las] + [changed-verb]`

When two pile up, the **personal one** (*me, te, nos*) comes first, then *lo/la/los/las*. `algo` is **not** a clitic — it sits after the verb.

```
me lo venden       they're selling it to me
nos lo venden      they're selling it to us
no me lo venden    they don't sell it to me
nos venden algo    they're selling us something   (algo after the verb)
```

**Drill:** they're selling it to us · they don't sell it to me · they're selling us something · they're selling it to me

---

### S6 — "Can / must" + to-form (English drops the "to") *(modal + infinitive)*
**shape:** `[poder/deber changed] + [verb-toform](+[pronoun])`

`puedo venir` literally = "I am able to come" — Spanish keeps no extra "to."

```
puedo venir          I can come
puedes venir         you can come
¿puedes venir?       can you come?
no puedes venir      you can't come
quiero saber por qué no puedes venir   I want to know why you can't come
```

**Drill:** I can come · can you come? · why can't you come? · I want to know why you can't come

---

### S7 — Negatives and questions wrap the whole cluster *(negation; interrogative)*
**shape:** `(¿)(por qué) no [pronoun-cluster] [changed-verb](?)`

`no` sits in front of the cluster; `¿…?` and `por qué` wrap around the outside. Nothing inside moves.

```
no venden                  they're not selling
no lo venden               they're not selling it
¿por qué no lo venden?     why aren't they selling it?
¿por qué no me lo venden?  why don't they sell it to me?
no me esperan              they're not waiting for me
```

**Drill:** they're not selling it · why aren't they selling it? · why don't they sell it to us? · they're not waiting for me

---

## 3. Same idea, said other ways

- **`hablo`** = "I speak" **and** "I'm speaking." Spanish present covers simple + continuous; same for `espero`, `vendo`.
- **`me esperan`** = "they wait for me" / "they await me" — no separate word for "for."
- **`hablarlo`** already means "to speak about it"; no Spanish word for "about" needed on a to-form.
- **Pronoun, two homes:** `lo quiero ver` and `quiero verlo` are the **same sentence** — front or tail, your choice (S4).
- **`claro`** = "clear" (adjective) **and** "of course" (conversational filler). Same word.
- **`como`** = "I eat," "how," and "like / as" — context sorts them out.

---

## 4. Emphasis & bends

- **Two bites, not one.** Build the verb first, *then* the object: "she has them" vs "they have her" only differ by which bite you get right.
- **`-s` = you, `-n` = they, everywhere.** This holds across verb types — *hablas/haces/puedes/estás*, *hablan/hacen/pueden*. Learn the two tails once.
- **Don't let vowels harmonize.** Your mouth may want to say *espere* after *espe-*; run back to the to-form *esperar* and rebuild. You drive, not the vowels.
- **`g` slips into the I-form** for *hacer/tener/venir/salir* → *hago/tengo/vengo/salgo*; every other person stays regular.
- **Gender lives in the WORD, not the thing.** *La persona* is feminine even for a man. `-a`, `-ión`, `-idad` endings ⇒ feminine ⇒ `la`. Adjectives match: `generoso/a/os/as`.
- **`algo` is not a clitic.** It refuses the front slot: *vende algo*, *nos venden algo* — always after the verb.
