# Bundle T — Demonstratives + Mood-Tense Intro (Lessons 68–70)

Two big arrivals. First, the **this/that/those** family (*este, ese,* + the odd neuter *esto*),
a clean gender-and-number grid you can read off like a table. Second, the **mood tense**
(the *subjunctive*) — Spanish's tense for wishes, needs, and feelings — built by one trick:
take the I-form and swap the group vowel.

The mood tense almost always rides in on a fixed frame you can drill mechanically:

```
[ subjective verb ]   que   [ verb in mood tense ]
    quiero             que        esperes
  (want/need/prefer)  (that)    (group-vowel swapped)
```

> **Grammar terms** appear in *(italics)* next to the plain label, e.g. **mood tense**
> *(subjunctive)*. Plain label first so it reads like Mad Libs; the real term is there when
> you want it. **Slots** are written in `[brackets]` — fill them in.

---

## 1. Word groups

Words sorted by **the job they do in a sentence**, not by dictionary order. Drill the group, not the word.

### Pointing words — "this" *(demonstrative adjectives, near)* — sit in front of the noun, match it
| Spanish | English | matches |
|---------|---------|---------|
| `este`  | this    | masculine singular (*este hombre*) |
| `esta`  | this    | feminine singular (*esta noche*) |
| `estos` | these   | masculine plural (*estos hombres*) |
| `estas` | these   | feminine plural (*estas noches*) |

Memory hooks: `este` borrows the /e/ from `el`; `estos` borrows the `-os` from `los`.

### Pointing words — "that" *(demonstrative adjectives, far)* — same family, drop the **t** of *est-*
| Spanish | English | matches |
|---------|---------|---------|
| `ese`   | that    | masculine singular (*ese hombre*) |
| `esa`   | that    | feminine singular (*esa noche*) |
| `esos`  | those   | masculine plural (*esos hombres*) |
| `esas`  | those   | feminine plural (*esas noches*) |

`esta → esa`: just drop the `t`. The whole "that" row is the "this" row minus one letter.

### Vague pointing words *(neuter demonstratives)* — no specific noun, no gender
| Spanish | English | use |
|---------|---------|-----|
| `esto`  | this    | "this" in the air — *No creo esto.* |
| `eso`   | that    | "that" in the air — *Nada me gustó más que eso.* |

Use these when you're **not** pointing at a known noun ("I can't believe this"). Not masculine, not feminine.

### Naming words *(nouns)* — watch the gender surprise
| Spanish | English | gender |
|---------|---------|--------|
| `hombre`| man   | masculine |
| `noche` | night | feminine |
| `día`   | day   | **masculine** despite the `-a` (*el día, todo el día*) |
| `vez / veces` | time / times | feminine (recall *otra vez*) → *cuántas veces* |
| `necesidad` | necessity | feminine (`-ity → -idad`) |
| `falta` | lack | feminine — *falta de respeto, falta de consideración* |

### Action words *(verbs)* — the new ones this bundle leans on
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `creer`     | to believe | two e's |
| `esperar`   | to wait / to hope | |
| `necesitar` | to need | from *necesidad* |
| `preferir`  | to prefer | *prefiero* — the `e` splits to `ie` |
| `faltar`    | to lack / be missing / be short of | |
| `quedarse`  | to stay (reflexive) | *se queda / se queden* |
| `dar`       | to give | mood-tense *des* |

### Little words
| Spanish | English | term |
|---------|---------|------|
| `tan`   | so (e.g. *tan cansado* = so tired) | intensifier |
| `que`   | that (the linker, **no** accent) | conjunction |
| `lo que`| what (when it's **not** a question) | relative |
| `cuántas veces` | how many times | interrogative (fem., from *vez*) |
| `de` / `sobre` | about | preposition |
| `hace falta` | it's necessary ("it makes lack") | fixed expression |
| `pronto`| soon (Spanish) | adverb |
| `aquí`  | here | adverb |

## The mood tense — the big rule of this bundle

The **mood tense** *(subjunctive)* shows your subjectivity — wishes, needs, preferences, feelings —
rather than plain facts. English has a faint version: "It's important that you **are** here" (fact)
vs "It's important that you **be** here" (wish/imperative). Spanish makes it a full tense.

**How to build it: take the present form, then SWAP THE GROUP VOWEL.**

```
-ar verbs   borrow the  -er/-ir endings  →  habla → hable,  hablo → hable,  hablan → hablen
-er/-ir verbs borrow the -ar  endings    →  come  → coma,   como  → coma,   comen → coman
```

| present | mood tense | English |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| hablo / habla | `hable`  | I / he speak(s) → mood |
| hablas        | `hables` | you speak → mood |
| hablan        | `hablen` | they speak → mood |
| hablamos      | `hablemos` | we speak → mood (`-ar` we-form → `-emos`) |
| como / come   | `coma`   | I / he eat(s) → mood |
| comen         | `coman`  | they eat → mood |
| comemos       | `comamos`| we eat → mood (`-er/-ir` we-form → `-amos`) |

Two things to lock in:
- Just like the present, the **I-form and the he/she/it/you-formal form are identical** in mood tense (*hable, coma*).
- For "we", `-ar` goes to **`-emos`** and `-er/-ir` goes to **`-amos`**. Do **not** drag in the odd `-ir` present /i/ (*vivimos, salimos*) — mood uses /e/ for the `-ar` group.

Mood tense comes in two flavors: **obligatory** (use it or it sounds wrong — §2, S5/S6) and **optional** (used to color a sentence with feeling). And remember: it's not just a structure, it's a **feeling** — train your ear for subjectivity.

## 2. Sentence structures (drill these)

Each structure: a **shape** line (the only "code"), the slots you can swap, worked examples, then a **Drill** the student finishes out loud or on paper.

---

### S1 — Point at a thing: "this/that ___" *(demonstrative + noun)*
**shape:** `[demonstrative] + [noun]`  *(the demonstrative agrees in gender + number)*

The full grid — pick the cell that matches the noun:

| | masc. sg. | fem. sg. | masc. pl. | fem. pl. |
|--------|-----------|----------|-----------|----------|
| this / these | `este` | `esta` | `estos` | `estas` |
| that / those | `ese`  | `esa`  | `esos`  | `esas`  |

```
esta noche        this night (= tonight)
ese hombre        that man
estos hombres     these men
esas noches       those nights
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** this man · that night · these nights · those men · this night (tonight)

---

### S2 — Flip the order for flavor *(post-nominal demonstrative)*
**shape:** `el/la + [noun] + [demonstrative]`

Same meaning, demonstrative moved **after** the noun (possibly an Arabic-influenced structure).

```
el hombre este    this man
el hombre ese     that man
```

**Drill:** this man (flipped) · that man (flipped)

---

### S3 — Vague "this/that" with no noun *(neuter demonstrative)*
**shape:** `[verb] + esto / eso`

When there's no specific noun behind "this/that", reach for the neuter `esto` / `eso`.

```
No creo esto.                    I don't believe this.
Nada me gusta más que esto.      I don't like anything more than this.
Nada me gustó más que eso.       Nothing pleased me more than that.
```

**Drill:** I don't believe this · nothing pleased me more than that · I don't like anything more than this

---

### S4 — "so ___ (that)…": `tan` + adjective *(intensifier + agreement)*
**shape:** `tan + [adjective]` *(adjective still matches gender/number)* `(+ que + [result])`

`tan` = "so". The adjective after it keeps matching its subject.

| sentence | English |
|----------|---------|
| `tan cansado` / `tan cansada` | so tired (m / f) |
| `Estaba tan cansada.` | I was so tired. |
| `Estábamos tan cansados que dormimos todo el día.` | We were so tired that we slept all day. |
| `Estábamos tan cansados cuando llegamos que dormimos todo el día.` | We were so tired when we arrived that we slept all day. |

The line-in-past `estaba / estábamos` sets a past frame, so the ambiguous verbs after it (*llegamos, dormimos*) read as past too.

**Drill:** so tired (f) · I was so tired · we were so tired that we slept all day

---

### S5 — The mood frame: "[want/need/prefer] that you ___" *(trigger + que + subjunctive)*
**shape:** `[subjective verb] + que + [verb in MOOD tense]`

This is the whole game. Spanish doesn't say "I want you to wait" — it says **"I want THAT you wait"**, and the verb after `que` flips to mood tense.

| build | result | English |
|-------|--------|---------|
| quiero + que + esperes | `Quiero que esperes.` | I want you to wait. |
| quiero + que + lo esperes | `Quiero que lo esperes.` | I want you to wait for him. |
| necesito + que + lo prepare | `Necesito que lo prepare.` | I need you (formal) to prepare it. |
| prefiero + que + se quede | `Prefiero que se quede.` | I prefer that he stays. |
| hace falta + que + comamos | `Hace falta que comamos pronto.` | It's necessary that we eat soon. |

Triggers seen here: `quiero` (want), `necesito` (need), `prefiero` (prefer), `hace falta que` / `es necesario` (it's necessary). All force the mood tense after `que`.

**Drill:** I want you to wait · I need you (formal) to prepare it · I prefer that he stays · it's necessary that we eat soon

---

### S6 — Mood frame, expanded: negate, pluralize, clarify *(subjunctive with pronouns)*
**shape:** `[trigger] (no?) + que + [pronoun] + [MOOD verb] (+ a él/ella/usted)`

The frame from S5 takes the same add-ons your other verbs do.

| sentence | English |
|----------|---------|
| `Prefiero que se queden.` | I prefer that they stay. |
| `Prefiero que no se queden aquí.` | I prefer that they don't stay here. |
| `¿Quieres que te espere?` | Do you want me to wait for you? |
| `¿Quieres que yo te espere?` | Do you want **me** to wait for you? (clarified) |
| `Necesito que usted lo prepare.` | I need **you** to prepare it. (clarified) |
| `Prefiero que se lo des a él / a ella.` | I prefer that you give it to him / to her. |

To clear up an ambiguous `me/te/se`, drop in `yo, tú, él, ella, usted` — Spanish leans on these only for clarity here. (`le → se` still applies before `lo/la`: *se lo des*.)

**Drill:** I prefer that they don't stay here · do you want me to wait for you? · I need you (formal) to prepare it · I prefer that you give it to her

---

### S7 — "what" that isn't a question: `lo que` *(relative pronoun)*
**shape:** `[verb] + lo que + [clause]`

`qué` (with accent) is "what" **only** in questions. For "what" as a connector, use `lo que`.

```
Vi lo que estás haciendo.              I saw what you're doing.
Yo cociné lo que comen.                I cooked what they eat.
Necesito que prepare lo que falta.     I need you to prepare what's missing.
```

It stacks with the mood frame: *No quiero que hables sobre lo que hablamos.* = I don't want you to speak about what we spoke about. (Use `hablábamos` or a time word like *el otro día* to force the past reading.)

**Drill:** I saw what you're doing · I cooked what they eat · I don't want you to speak about what we spoke about

## 3. Same idea, said other ways

- **"It's necessary"** → `hace falta que…` (literally "it makes lack that…") **or** `es necesario que…` — same meaning, both followed by mood tense.
- **`esto` vs `este`:** use `este/esta/...` when a real noun is in play (*este hombre*); use the neuter `esto/eso` when there isn't (*no creo esto*).
- **`hablamos`** can be present **or** past — same form. Force the past with a time word (*el otro día*) or the past-line form *hablábamos*.
- **Demonstrative order is flexible:** *ese hombre* and *el hombre ese* mean the same thing.
- **`qué` vs `lo que`:** question "what" takes the accent (*¿Qué comen?*); connector "what" is `lo que` (*lo que comen*).

---

## 4. Emphasis & bends

- **The mood frame is mechanical:** `[trigger] + que + [vowel-swapped verb]`. Once you spot a wanting/needing/preferring verb pushing into a `que`, swap the next verb's group vowel. That's 90% of it.
- **`no` and the subject pronoun bend, the frame doesn't.** Negate inside the `que` clause (*que no se queden*), and add `yo/usted/a él` only to clarify who's who — not by default.
- **Agreement never sleeps.** `tan cansado/cansada/cansados`, `estos hombres`, `esas noches` — gender and number keep matching even with `tan` and the pointing words.
- **Watch the gender traps:** `día` is masculine (*el día*) though it ends in `-a`; `vez` is feminine (*cuántas veces*, *otra vez*). When unsure, recall how you saw the word before.

- `esta noche` / `anoche` — "tonight" / "last night" (Lesson 64); the hook used to crack the whole *this/that* family.

- Question accents `cuándo, cuánto, qué` (Lesson 67) — contrast with the unaccented linkers `que` and `lo que`.
