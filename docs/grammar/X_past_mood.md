# Bundle X — Past Mood Tense (Lessons 81–83)

The bundle that finishes the mood tense by pushing it **into the past**. You learn to back-shift
the mood (*quería que* + *comieran*), build that past-mood form straight off the they-form of the
point-past, dodge the mood entirely with a wording trick (*lo importante es que*), and wire up the
big hypothetical: **si [past-mood], [conditional]** — "if I could, I would."

Everything here is one move repeated: a trigger word (almost always **`que`** or **`si`**) flips the
verb after it into the mood, and *which* mood — present or past — just matches the time you're in.

```
[ trigger ]      [ subject ]   [ mood verb ]        [ time ]
quería que          ellos        comieran          → past mood
no es relevante que  -           encuentren        → present mood
si                  yo           pudiera           → past mood (hypothetical)
```

> **Grammar terms** appear in *(italics)* next to the plain label, e.g. **mood verb**
> *(subjunctive)*. Plain label first so it reads like Mad Libs; the real term is there when
> you want it. **Slots** are written in `[brackets]` — fill them in.

---

## 1. Word groups

Words sorted by **the job they do in a sentence**, not by dictionary order. Drill the group, not the word.

### Mood triggers *(subjunctive triggers)* — the word in front that flips the next verb into the mood
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `que`     | that          | the big alarm bell — `que` most often means "mood ahead" |
| `si`      | if            | **only** when hypothetical-about-the-past (see §2, S6) |
| `no quiero que` | I don't want (that) | wanting + `que` → mood |
| `quería que`    | I wanted (that)     | line-past want + `que` → **past** mood |
| `esperaba que`  | I was hoping (that) | line-past hope + `que` → past mood |
| `necesitábamos que` | we needed (that) | line-past need + `que` → past mood |
| `es relevante que` | it's relevant that | *ser/estar* + adj + `que` → mood |
| `era relevante que` | it was relevant that | line-past of *ser* (`era`) + adj + `que` → past mood |
| `para que` | so that | the `que` cousin of plain `para`; pulls the mood |

`que` is the thread through this whole bundle. See a `que` after a feeling/judgement → reach for the mood.

### Past-mood verb forms *(imperfect subjunctive)* — built from the **they-form of the point-past**
| from they-point-past | drop `-on`, add… | result | English |
|----------------------|------------------|--------|---------|
| `hablaron` | → `-a`   | `hablara`   | (that I/he/she/you-formal) spoke |
| `comieron` | → `-a`   | `comiera`   | (that I/he/she) ate |
| `encontraron` | → `-a` | `encontrara` | (that …) found |
| `compraron` | → `-a`  | `comprara`  | (that …) bought |
| `pudieron`  | → `-a`  | `pudiera`   | (if I) could |
| `hubieron`  | → `-a`  | `hubiera`   | (if I) had |
| `vieron`    | → `-a`  | `viera`     | (if I) saw |

**One form covers both line-past and point-past** in the mood — no split here.

### "Would" verbs *(conditional)* — full to-form + `-ía`, the second half of an *if* sentence
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `gustaría`  | would like   | memory hook for the `-ía` sound (*me gustaría*) |
| `cocinaría` | I would cook | full infinitive `cocinar` + `-ía` |
| `cancelaría`| I would cancel | + pronoun: `lo cancelaría` |
| `comprendería` / `entendería` | I/he would understand | both verbs = "understand" |
| `habría`    | I/he/you-formal would have | `e` falls out — **not** *habería* |
| `habríamos` | we would have | + past participle: `habríamos ido` |

### "Again" + "happen" verbs *(verb periphrasis; lexical set)*
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `volver a` + verb | to do (verb) **again** | literally "return to (do)"; nothing to do with returning |
| `volví a llamarte` | I called you again | `volver a` as plain "again" |
| `pasar`   | to pass / to happen | easiest "to happen" verb |
| `ocurrir` | to occur / happen   | |
| `suceder` | to happen / succession | |

`volver` needs the connector `a` because it's a verb of movement.

### Little words & helpers
| Spanish | English | term |
|---------|---------|------|
| `otra vez` | again / another time | adverbial |
| `se me`    | on me (it ___s on me) | reflexive + indirect "on me" |
| `era`      | was / used to be | irregular line-past of *ser* |
| `lo` + adj | the ___ thing | neuter article: `lo importante` = the important thing |
| `mi / tu / su` | my / your-informal / his-her-your-formal-their | `su` covers all four |
| `increíble` | incredible / unbelievable | from *creer* → *creíble* → *increíble* (no "d") |

## 2. Sentence structures (drill these)

Each structure: a **shape** line (the only "code"), the slots you can swap, worked examples, then a **Drill** the student finishes out loud or on paper.

---

### S1 — "I don't want it to ___ on me" *(present mood after a trigger; reflexive `se me`)*
**shape:** `no quiero que + [se me] + [mood verb] (+ otra vez)`

After `no quiero que` we always use the mood. `se me` = the thing happens *on me*. Stem-changers flip
their vowel for the mood: `cerrar` (e→ie) → `cierre`.

```
no quiero que la puerta se me cierre            I don't want the door to close on me
no quiero que la puerta se me cierre otra vez   I don't want the door to close on me again
no quiero que vuelva a pasar                    I don't want it to happen again
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** I don't want the door to close on me · I don't want it to happen again · I don't want the door to close on me again

---

### S2 — "to do it again" with `volver a` *(verb periphrasis: volver a + infinitive)*
**shape:** `volver a + [verb-toform](+[pronoun])`

`volver a` + a to-form verb = "do it **again**." The `a` is required (`volver` is motion). In the mood
it becomes `vuelva a` / `vuelvan a`.

| build | result | English |
|-------|--------|---------|
| vuelve a + cerrarse | `vuelve a cerrarse` | it closes again |
| no quiero que + vuelva a + pasar | `no quiero que vuelva a pasar` | I don't want it to happen again |
| vuelven a + hacerlo | `vuelven a hacerlo` | they do it again |
| volví a + llamarte | `volví a llamarte` | I called you again |

**Drill:** it closes again · they do it again · I called you again · I don't want it to happen again

---

### S3 — Build the past mood tense *(imperfect subjunctive paradigm)*
**shape:** `they-form of point-past → drop -on → add ending`

Take the **they-form of the point in the past**, lop off `-on`, and hang the endings on. One form does
both line-past and point-past.

| person | ending | *hablar* | *comer* | *organizar* |
|--------|--------|----------|---------|-------------|
| I / he / she / it / you-formal | `-a` | `hablara` | `comiera` | `organizara` |
| you (informal) | `-as` | `hablaras` | `comieras` | `organizaras` |
| we | `-´amos` (accent on the *a*) | `habláramos` | `comiéramos` | `organizáramos` |
| they | `-an` | `hablaran` | `comieran` | `organizaran` |

Irregulars come pre-baked in the they-point-past, so you just drop `-on`: `pudieron → pudiera`,
`hubieron → hubiera`, `vieron → viera`.

**Drill:** speak (we) · eat (they) · buy (you-informal) · could (I) · had (you-informal) · saw (I)

---

### S4 — Back-shift the mood: "I wanted them to ___" *(past trigger + past mood)*
**shape:** `[line-past feeling] que + [past mood verb]`

Put the feeling verb in the line-past (`quería`, `esperaba`, `necesitábamos`), keep `que`, and the verb
after it goes **past mood**. English "would call" just becomes the past mood — no separate "would."

```
quería que comieran con nosotros        I wanted them to eat with us
esperaba que me llamara                 I was hoping he would call me
necesitábamos que hablara con nosotros  we needed him to speak with us
```

Watch the `que`: `necesitábamos hablar con él` ("we needed to speak with him") has **no `que`** and so
**no mood** — it's just a to-form. Add `que` and you force the mood.

**Drill:** I wanted them to eat with us · I was hoping he would call me · we needed him to speak with us · (contrast) we needed to speak with him

---

### S5 — Judgement + `que` triggers the mood; match the time *(ser/estar + adj + que)*
**shape:** `[ser/estar + adj] que + [mood verb]`

After `es relevante que`, `está bien que`, `es increíble que` → mood. Use **present mood** for now/future,
**past mood** for the past. `era` is the line-past of *ser* (mnemonic: a line in the past is an *era*).

| sentence | English | which mood |
|----------|---------|------------|
| `no es muy relevante que lo encuentren` | it's not very relevant that they find it | present mood |
| `no es muy relevante que lo encontraran` | it's not very relevant that they found it | past mood |
| `era muy relevante que lo encontraran` | it was very relevant that they found it | past mood (`era`) |
| `es increíble que vuelvan a hacerlo` | it's incredible that they do it again | present mood |
| `es increíble que volvieran a hacerlo` | it's incredible that they did it again | past mood |

**Drill:** it's not relevant that they find it · it's not relevant that they found it · it was relevant that they found it · it's incredible that they did it again

---

### S6 — The hypothetical "if": `si [past mood], [conditional]` *(second conditional)*
**shape:** `si + [past mood verb], [lo] + [conditional verb]`

When "if" goes **hypothetical-about-the-past** (English "if I were / if I could"), the verb after `si`
goes past mood, and the other half is the conditional ("would"). English swaps *was → were* for this;
Spanish does the same job with the past mood. (Normal present "if" does **not** take the mood.)

```
si pudiera cocinar, cocinaría              if I could cook, I would cook
si pudiera cancelarlo, lo cancelaría       if I could cancel it, I would cancel it
si lo viera, lo comprendería / entendería  if I saw it, I would understand it
si vieras su casa                          if you saw their house
```

`poder` goes irregular in the point-past (`pod- → pud-` → `pudieron → pudiera`); `ver` keeps an extra
*e* (`vieron → viera`).

**Drill:** if I could cook, I would cook · if I could cancel it, I would cancel it · if I saw it, I would understand it · if you saw their house

---

### S7 — The "if-had" past hypothetical: `si hubiera + participle, habría + participle` *(third conditional)*
**shape:** `si + [hubiera/hubieras…] + [past participle], habría + [past participle]`

For "if you **had** told me…" use the past mood of `haber` (`hubiera`, `hubieras`, `hubiéramos`) + a
participle, then "would have" = `habría` + participle. `habría`, not *habería* — the `e` falls out.

```
si me lo hubieras contado, habría ido       if you had told me it, I would have gone
si hubiéramos sabido, habríamos ido         if we had known, we would have gone
```

**Drill:** if you had told me it, I would have gone · if we had known, we would have gone

## 3. Same idea, said other ways

- **`pasar` / `ocurrir` / `suceder`** all = "to happen." `pasar` is the easy everyday one.
- **`volver a` + verb** = "again" — `volví a llamarte` is just "I called you again," no returning involved.
- **"would call me" = past mood, no extra word:** `esperaba que me llamara`. Spanish folds the "would" into the verb.
- **`comprender` and `entender`** both = "to understand." Pick either: `comprendería` / `entendería`.
- **One past-mood form, two pasts:** the same `comiera` covers both line-past and point-past meanings.
- **Alternate flavor:** there's a second past-mood ending, `-ase / -iese` (`hablase`, `comiese`, `entendiese`, `comiésemos`). Same meaning, different dialect/style — **recognize** it, no need to use it.

---

## 4. Emphasis & bends

- **`que` is the alarm bell.** A `que` after a feeling, want, or judgement is your cue to flip the next verb into the mood. This is the single habit of the bundle.
- **Match the mood to the time.** Same trigger, two moods: present mood (`encuentren`) for now/future, past mood (`encontraran`) for the past. Nothing else changes.
- **Dodge the mood when it's in the way.** Reword `es importante que` → `lo importante es que` (after `es que` you're just stating a fact — no mood). Compare `es increíble que vuelvan` vs `lo increíble es que vuelven`. Same trick: `volví a llamarte para organizar` (no mood) vs `…para que organizáramos` (mood).
- **"The X thing" = `lo` + adjective.** Not *la cosa importante* — just `lo importante`, `lo increíble`, `lo espectacular`.
- **No `que`, no mood.** `necesitábamos hablar con él` stays a plain to-form; only adding `que` (`necesitábamos que hablara`) forces the mood.
