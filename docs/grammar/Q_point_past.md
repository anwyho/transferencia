# Bundle Q — Point-Past Intro (Lessons 58–60)

The bundle where the past gets a second gear. You already have the "line in the past"
(the ongoing, background past); now you meet the **point in the past** — the one-shot,
finished action (English "I spoke", "I bought", "I sent"). It rides almost entirely on
**accents**: the same letters mean different things depending on which vowel is stressed,
so this is the bundle where written accents stop being decoration and start being
meaning.

Everything here is built from a stem plus a stressed ending:

```
[ stem ]   +   [ point-past ending ]   →   finished action
 habl-           -é  (I)  /  -ó  (he/she/you-formal)
 com-            -í  (I)  /  -ió (he/she/you-formal)
```

> **Grammar terms** appear in *(italics)* next to the plain label, e.g. **point in the
> past** *(preterite)*. Plain label first so it reads like Mad Libs; the real term is
> there when you want it. **Slots** are written in `[brackets]` — fill them in.

---

## 1. Word groups

Words sorted by **the job they do in a sentence**, not by dictionary order. Drill the group, not the word.

### Hook verbs *(model verbs / conjugation anchors)* — memorize these two, the rest follow
| Spanish | English | covers |
|---------|---------|--------|
| `hablar` | to speak | all `-ar` verbs |
| `comer`  | to eat   | all `-er` AND `-ir` verbs |

Two hooks carry the whole tense: `hablar` for the `-ar` pattern, `comer` for the `-er/-ir` pattern.

### `-ar` verbs *(first-conjugation verbs)* — take `-é` / `-ó` in the point-past
| Spanish | English |
|---------|---------|
| `hablar`  | to speak |
| `comprar` | to buy |
| `enviar`  | to send (cf. "envoy") |
| `olvidar` | to forget |
| `durar`   | to last (cf. "endure", "duration") |
| `parar`   | to stop |
| `esperar` | to wait / to hope |

### `-er / -ir` verbs *(second/third-conjugation verbs)* — take `-í` / `-ió` in the point-past
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `comer`     | to eat        | |
| `ver`       | to see        | regular here: `vi` / `vio` |
| `salir`     | to leave / go out | irregular only in present "I"; regular here |
| `vivir`     | to live       | |
| `perder`    | to lose       | |
| `comprender`| to understand | |

### Negative words *(negative pronouns; double negation)* — Spanish stacks negatives happily
| Spanish | English | example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| `nada`  | nothing | `no vi nada` (I didn't see anything) |
| `nadie` | nobody  | `nadie vio nada` (nobody saw anything) |

Spanish keeps the `no` AND the `nada` — a **double negative** is correct, not a mistake.

### Time & context words *(adverbs / prepositions of time)* — anchor an ambiguous form to a tense
| Spanish | English | term |
|---------|---------|------|
| `ayer`     | yesterday | adverb (points to past) |
| `mañana`   | tomorrow  | adverb (points to future) |
| `cuando`   | when      | conjunction |
| `todavía`  | still / yet (= "all the way": *toda* + *vía*) | adverb |
| `durante`  | during / for (with time) | preposition |
| `sin`      | without   | preposition (takes the to-form: `sin parar`) |
| `siempre`  | always    | adverb (flexible position) |
| `a menudo` | often     | adverbial phrase |

### Little words
| Spanish | English | term |
|---------|---------|------|
| `que`   | than (comparisons) — `más que yo` | conjunction |
| `qué`   | what / that (`espero que`) | interrogative / connector |
| `por qué` | why | interrogative |
| `para` / `para mí` | for / for me (emphasis) | preposition |
| `conmigo` | with me | pronoun + preposition |
| `mucho` | a lot | adverb |

## The point-past, end to end — the big rule of this bundle

Not a sentence structure, but the engine behind every drill below. **One** new past tense,
two endings per person, all riding on a written accent.

```
-ar verbs (hook: hablar)        -er/-ir verbs (hook: comer)
  I        → -é   hablé           I        → -í    comí
  he/she/  → -ó   habló           he/she/  → -ió   comió
  you-formal                      you-formal
```

The accent is **load-bearing**, not optional:

```
HÁ-blo   = I speak   (present, stress on stem)
ha-BLÓ   = he spoke  (point-past, stress on ending)
ha-BLÉ   = I spoke   (point-past, stress on ending)
```

**The /o/ trap:** English-speakers want `habló` for "I spoke" because /o/ *feels* like "I".
It is not — "I" is `-é` / `-í`. `Habló` and `comió` are **he/she/you-formal**.

## 2. Sentence structures (drill these)

Each structure: a **shape** line (the only "code"), the slots you can swap, worked examples, then a **Drill** the student finishes out loud or on paper.

---

### S1 — "I/he ___" in the point-past, `-ar` verbs *(preterite, first conjugation)*
**shape:** `[stem]+é` (I) · `[stem]+ó` (he/she/you-formal)

- stem: `habl-` · `compr-` · `envi-` · `olvid-`

```
hablé      I spoke
habló      he / she / you-formal spoke
compré     I bought
compró     he bought
no hablé   I didn't speak
no habló   he didn't speak
```

Both endings carry the accent — write it. `hablé` ≠ `hable`, `habló` ≠ `hablo`.

**Drill (EN → ES):** I bought · he bought · I sent · he sent · I didn't speak · he didn't forget

---

### S2 — "I/he ___" in the point-past, `-er/-ir` verbs *(preterite, second/third conjugation)*
**shape:** `[stem]+í` (I) · `[stem]+ió` (he/she/you-formal)

- stem: `com-` · `v-` (ver) · `sal-` · `viv-`

```
comí       I ate
comió      he ate
vi         I saw
vio        he saw
salí       I left
salió      he left
```

`ver` and `salir` are regular here. `vi` / `vio` are short — no accent needed (one syllable).

**Drill:** I ate · he ate · I saw · he saw · I left · she didn't leave yet (`no salió todavía`)

---

### S3 — Stack the little words onto the past *(indirect + direct object pronouns)*
**shape:** `(no) + [indirect] + [direct] + [verb-past]`

Order is **indirect, then direct, then the verb**. No "for" needed — it's baked in.

| build | result | English |
|-------|--------|---------|
| lo + compró | `lo compró` | he bought it |
| me + lo + compró | `me lo compró` | he bought it for me |
| te + lo + envié | `te lo envié` | I sent it to you |
| no + te + lo + envié | `no te lo envié` | I didn't send it to you |

Emphasis option: `lo compró para mí` ("he bought it **for me**") spells out *para mí* for stress.

**Drill:** I sent it · I sent it to you · I didn't send it to you yet · he bought it for me

---

### S4 — "It was done" without a doer *(impersonal / passive `se`)*
**shape:** `se + [verb-past]`

Where English says "it was sent", Spanish says `se envió` — "it sent itself".
**Don't add `lo`** — `envió` already means "it sent".

```
se envió         it was sent
se envió ayer    it was sent yesterday
```

**Drill:** it was sent · it was sent yesterday

---

### S5 — Double negatives *(negative concord)*
**shape:** `no + [verb-past] + nada` · `nadie + [verb-past] + nada`

Keep both negatives. `no ... nada` = "didn't ... anything"; `nadie ... nada` = "nobody ... anything".

```
no vi nada        I didn't see anything
no comí nada      I didn't eat anything
nadie vio nada    nobody saw anything
```

`nadie` can lead the sentence on its own — no extra `no` needed in front.

**Drill:** I didn't see anything · I didn't eat anything · nobody saw anything · why didn't you eat anything? (`¿por qué no comió nada?`)

---

### S6 — The "we" form, and where `-er` breaks ranks *(first-person plural preterite)*
**shape:** `[stem]+amos` (`-ar`) · `[stem]+imos` (`-er/-ir`)

The "we" point-past **collides** with the present for `-ar` and `-ir` — same word, context picks the tense. `-er` is the lone exception: it changes.

| verb | present "we" | past "we" | same? |
|------|--------------|-----------|-------|
| `hablar` (-ar) | `hablamos` | `hablamos` | **same** |
| `vivir` (-ir)  | `vivimos`  | `vivimos`  | **same** |
| `salir` (-ir)  | `salimos`  | `salimos`  | **same** |
| `comer` (-er)  | `comemos`  | `comimos`  | **different** |
| `perder` (-er) | `perdemos` | `perdimos` | **different** |
| `ver` (-er)    | `vemos`    | `vimos`    | **different** |

Why: `-er` and `-ir` split only at the present "we" (`-emos` vs `-imos`). In the past **both** use `-imos`, so `-ir` matches its own present but `-er` no longer does.

```
hablamos ayer            we spoke yesterday
no comimos               we didn't eat
nos perdimos             we got lost (lost ourselves)
no salimos a menudo, pero salimos ayer   we don't go out often, but we went out yesterday
```

**Drill:** we spoke (yesterday) · we ate vs. we eat · we lost vs. we lose · we got lost · we waited a lot yesterday

---

### S7 — Let context carry an ambiguous form *(tense disambiguation by adverb / clause)*
**shape:** `[ambiguous-we-form] + [time word / clause]`

When `hablamos` / `vivimos` could be present or past, a context word decides — or Spanish even uses the present for the **future**.

| sentence | English | what fixes the tense |
|----------|---------|----------------------|
| `hablamos ayer` | we spoke yesterday | `ayer` → past |
| `hablamos mañana` | we'll speak tomorrow | `mañana` → present-as-future |
| `cuando hablamos, comprendí todo` | when we spoke, I understood everything | `comprendí` (past) anchors it |
| `cuando hablamos, comprendo todo` | when we speak, I understand everything | `comprendo` (present) anchors it |

**Drill:** we spoke yesterday · we'll speak tomorrow · when we spoke, I understood everything

---

### S8 — Duration: `durante` and `sin` *(prepositions of time / preposition + infinitive)*
**shape:** `[verb-past] + durante + [time]` · `... + sin + [verb-toform]`

`durante` = "during / for" with a stretch of time (`por` also works: `por dos minutos`).
After a preposition like `sin`, use the **to-form** verb, never an `-ing` form.

```
duró dos minutos              it lasted two minutes
comió durante media hora      he ate for half an hour
habló durante media hora sin parar   he spoke for half an hour without stopping
```

**Drill:** it lasted two minutes · he ate for half an hour · he spoke for half an hour without stopping

---

### S9 — Comparisons after `que`: use `yo` / `él`, not `mí` *(subject pronoun in comparison)*
**shape:** `[verb-past] + más que + [yo / él]`

After `que` ("than"), Spanish keeps the **subject** pronoun. English drifts to "than me" — Spanish stays literal "than I".

```
comí más que él     I ate more than he (did)
comió más que yo    he ate more than I (did)
```

Same habit-break as "Who wants to go? — *I!*" (not "Me!").

**Drill:** I ate more than he did · he ate more than I did

---

## 3. Same idea, said other ways

- **"for" with time** → `por dos minutos` or `durante dos minutos`. Same meaning, two prepositions.
- **`esperar`** = "to wait" **and** "to hope." One verb, two English jobs (cf. `desesperado` = "desperate / un-hoped").
- **`hablamos` / `vivimos` / `salimos`** = present **or** past — Spanish doesn't split them; context does the work.
- **`hablamos mañana`** = a future said in the present tense — "we'll speak tomorrow," no future ending needed.
- **`se envió`** vs a spelled-out doer: the `se` passive sidesteps naming who did it, where English would say "it was sent."

---

## 4. Emphasis & bends

- **The accent is the meaning.** `hablé` (I), `habló` (he), `hablo` (I speak, present): three words, one spine. Drop or misplace the accent and you've said something else. This bundle lives or dies on writing them.
- **Resist /o/ = "I".** The English ear hears /o/ as "I"; in the point-past `-ó` / `-ió` is **he/she/you-formal**. "I" is `-é` / `-í`.
- **Little words stack, then the verb.** `me lo compró`, `te lo envié`, `no te lo envié todavía` — indirect, direct, verb, with `no` out front.
- **`-er` is the odd one in "we".** `comemos` (present) vs `comimos` (past) is the only "we" pair that changes; every `-ar` and `-ir` "we" stays the same in both tenses.
- **Mix the two pasts.** Line-past for the ongoing setup, point-past for the finished hit: `lo iba a hacer pero lo olvidé` — "I was going to do it (`iba`) but I forgot (`olvidé`)."
