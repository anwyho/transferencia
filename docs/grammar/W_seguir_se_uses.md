# Bundle W — Seguir + ojalá + all uses of se (Lessons 78–80)

The bundle where the verb-after-verb choice (*to*-form vs *-ing*-form) starts carrying
meaning, two more mood triggers land (*es esencial que*, *ojalá*), and every loose strand
of **`se`** you've met gets pinned to one board: *sé* (I know), self-*se*, the phonetic
*le → se*, and the "on-me" accidental *se me / se te*.

Three moving parts run through it:

```
[ trigger verb ]   [ link ]   [ next verb form ]
  sigo              (none)      hablando      (-ing → "continue doing")
  empiezo            a          hablar        (to-form → "start to")
  termino            de         vender        (to-form → "finish doing")
```

> **Grammar terms** appear in *(italics)* next to the plain label, e.g. **-ing form**
> *(gerund)*. Plain label first so it reads like Mad Libs; the real term is there when
> you want it. **Slots** are written in `[brackets]` — fill them in.

## 1. Word groups

Words sorted by **the job they do in a sentence**, not by dictionary order. Drill the group, not the word.

### Continue / start / finish verbs *(aspectual / phase verbs)* — they take a second verb after them
| Spanish | English | what follows |
|---------|---------|--------------|
| `seguir` (`sigo`/`sigue`) | to continue / follow | **-ing** form, no link (§2, S1) |
| `empezar` (`empiezo`) | to start | `a` + to-form, **or** -ing (§2, S2) |
| `comenzar` (`comienzo`) | to start (commence) | `a` + to-form, **or** -ing (§2, S2) |
| `terminar` | to finish / terminate | `de` + to-form, **or** -ing (§2, S3) |
| `dejar` | to leave (a thing / message) | takes a noun object (§2, S4) |

`empezar` is the everyday word; `comenzar` ("commence") works too. Both need `a` before a to-form.

### Verbs that need a link to reach the next verb *(verbs requiring a preposition)* — they don't grab the to-form bare
| Spanish | link | example |
|---------|------|---------|
| verbs of motion: `ir`, `volver`, `salir` | `a` | `voy a hablar` |
| `empezar` / `comenzar` | `a` | `empezar a hablar` (to start to speak) |
| `terminar` | `de` | `terminar de vender` (to finish selling) |

The fact that `empezar a` and `terminar de` need a preposition is the **clue** that each also takes an *-ing* form afterward — with a slightly shifted meaning (§2, S2–S3).

### Mood-tense triggers *(subjunctive triggers)* — the verb after `que` goes to its mood form
| Spanish | English |
|---------|---------|
| `es probable que` | it's probable that ("he'll probably…") |
| `es imperativo que` | it's imperative that |
| `es espectacular que` | it's spectacular that |
| `es esencial que` | it's essential that |
| `cuando` (+ future event) | when (in future) |
| `ojalá` | hopefully |

`ojalá` comes from **Arabic** — likely a contraction of *ojo-Allah* ("eye-God"); Spanish was shaped by ~8 centuries of Arabic influence in southern Spain. `ojo` = eye, also "watch out!"

### The `se` words — all of them *(see the dedicated catalogue below)*
| form | English | which `se` |
|------|---------|------------|
| `sé` (accent) | I know | verb, from `saber` |
| `se` | himself / herself / yourself / itself / oneself | reflexive self-`se` |
| `se` (in `se lo`) | (to) him / her / them | phonetic `le/les → se` |
| `se me` / `se te` … | on me / on you … | accidental "on-me" `se` |

### Little words & vocab
| Spanish | English | term |
|---------|---------|------|
| `gratis` | free (no cost) | adjective (→ English "gratuitous") |
| `pronto` | soon | adverb |
| `siempre` | always | adverb |
| `mensaje` (m.) | message | noun |
| `puerta` | door | noun (from `puerto`/port) |
| `cena` | dinner | noun |
| `batería` | battery | noun |
| `conmigo` | with me | pronoun + prep |

Verbs pulled in for examples: `cerrar` (to close, e→ie: `cierra`), `cenar` (to dine), `quemar` (to burn), `acabar` (to finish / run out), `quedar` (to stay / remain), `vender` (to sell), `decir` (to say / tell), `ver` (to see).

## All the uses of `se` — one place (the headline of this bundle)

Four different jobs wear the same little spelling. Sort them by what they do.

### 1. `sé` — the verb "I know" *(1st-person of `saber`)*
Written **with an accent**, and it's a verb, not a pronoun. Often padded out as `lo sé` or `yo sé` because the bare `sé` sounds tiny.
- `Lo sé.` — I know (it).

### 2. Reflexive / self-`se` *(reflexive pronoun, 3rd person)*
No accent. Means himself, herself, yourself (formal), itself, themselves, oneself — the action loops back on the subject.
- `Se queda.` — He stays / remains (himself).
- `La puerta se cierra.` — The door closes (itself) / by itself.

### 3. Phonetic `se` — the `le/les → se` switch *(dative-before-accusative euphonic change)*
When `le`/`les` would land right next to `lo/la/los/las`, Spanish refuses *le lo* and swaps the `le/les` to `se`.
- `Le lo preparo` → **`Se lo preparo`** — I prepare it for him.

### 4. The "on-me" accidental `se` — `se + me/te/le/nos/les` *(unplanned-occurrence / dative of interest)*
Combine reflexive `se` with an indirect-object pronoun to say something happened **to** you / **on** you. English does this with only a few verbs ("it died on me", "it ran out on me"); Spanish uses it widely. See §2, S5 for the full structure.
- `Se me cierra la puerta.` — The door closes on me.
- `La batería se me acaba.` — The battery runs out on me.

You don't *need* the on-me structure to be understood — but you'll hear it everywhere, so recognize it.

## 2. Sentence structures (drill these)

Each structure: a **shape** line (the only "code"), the slots you can swap, worked examples, then a **Drill** the student finishes out loud or on paper.

---

### S1 — "I keep / continue ___-ing" *(seguir + gerund)*
**shape:** `[seguir-form] + [verb]-ando/-iendo`

`seguir` is followed by the **-ing form** *(gerund)* — no preposition — to mean "continue doing X".

- seguir: `sigo` (I) · `sigue` (he/she/formal you)
- verb (-ing): `hablando` · `siendo` (being, from `ser`) · `vendiendo`

```
Sigo hablando.                     I continue speaking.
Sigue hablando.                    He continues speaking.
Es probable que siga hablando.     It's probable that he continues speaking.
Es imperativo que siga siendo gratis.  It's imperative that it continues being free.
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** I keep speaking · He keeps speaking · it's probable that he keeps speaking · it's imperative that it keeps being free

---

### S2 — "I start to ___" vs "I start out ___-ing" *(empezar/comenzar + a + infinitive | + gerund)*
**shape:** `[empezar/comenzar-form] + a + [verb-toform]`  ·  `… + [verb]-ando/-iendo`

With `a` + to-form = **start to** do X. With the bare -ing form = **start out** doing X, right from the very beginning.

```
empezar a hablar                                  to start to speak
Es espectacular que comiencen a hablar español.   It's spectacular that they begin to speak Spanish.
Es espectacular que comiencen hablando español.   It's spectacular that they start out speaking Spanish.
```

In English "begin to speak" vs "begin speaking" is a tiny difference; in Spanish the -ing version really feels like "from the very start".

**Drill:** to start to speak · it's spectacular that they begin to speak Spanish · it's spectacular that they start out speaking Spanish

---

### S3 — "I finish ___-ing" vs "I end up ___-ing" *(terminar + de + infinitive | + gerund)*
**shape:** `[terminar-form] + de + [verb-toform]`  ·  `… + [verb]-ando/-iendo`

With `de` + to-form = **finish** doing X. With the bare -ing form = **end up** doing X.

```
terminar de vender                                       to finish selling
… cuando terminen de venderlos.                          … when you finish selling them.
Terminar vendiéndolos.                                   To end up selling them.
Quiero que hablen conmigo cuando terminen vendiéndolos.  I want you (guys) to speak with me when you end up selling them.
```

Note `cuando terminen` — `cuando` + future still triggers the **mood form** *(subjunctive)*.

**Drill:** to finish selling · when you finish selling them · to end up selling them · I want you guys to speak with me when you end up selling them

---

### S4 — "Leave me a message" + telling someone to *(dejar; indirect command via que + mood)*
**shape (command):** `[dejar-command] + me + [noun]`  ·  **shape (order):** `quiero que / quiero decirle que + [verb-mood]`

`dejar` = to leave (a thing or message) — not `salir` (to go out). The command attaches `me`.

```
Déjeme un mensaje.                       Leave me a message. (formal)
Quiero que me dejes un mensaje.          I want you to leave me a message. (informal)
Quiero decirle que me deje un mensaje.   I want to tell him to leave me a message.
Cuando te deje el mensaje, llámame.      When he leaves you the message, call me.
```

Spanish phrases "tell him to leave a message" as "tell him *that he leaves* a message" — the mood form (`deje`) carries the order. Plain present (`me deja`) would mean he's *currently* leaving one, not an order.

**Drill:** leave me a message (formal) · I want you to leave me a message · I want to tell him to leave me a message · when he leaves you the message, call me

---

### S5 — "It ___-ed on me" (the accidental se) *(se me / se te + verb)*
**shape:** `[se] + [me/te/le/nos/les] + [verb]` (subject noun before or after)

Build it from reflexive `se` + an indirect-object pronoun. Both word orders work: `La puerta se me cierra` and `Se me cierra la puerta`.

| build | result | English |
|-------|--------|---------|
| se + me + cierra | `Se me cierra la puerta` | the door closes on me |
| se + me + quema | `La cena se me quema` | the dinner is burning on me |
| se + me + acaba | `La batería se me acaba` | the battery runs out on me |
| se + te + acaba | `¿La batería se te acaba?` | does the battery run out on you? (informal) |
| se + le + acaba | `¿La batería se le acaba?` | …on you? (formal) |
| se + les + acaba | `La batería se les acaba` | …on them / you all |

Past works identically — just use the point-past verb: `La cena se me quemó` (the dinner got burned on me), `La cena se nos quemó` (…on us).

**Drill:** the door closes on me · the dinner is burning on me · my battery always runs out on me · does the battery run out on you (informal) · the dinner got burned on us

---

### S6 — Phonetic swap: "I prepare it for him" *(le → se before lo/la)*
**shape:** `[se] + [lo/la/los/las] + [verb]`

When `le`/`les` would sit next to `lo/la`, it becomes `se`. Spanish won't say *le lo*.

```
Le lo preparo  →  Se lo preparo     I prepare it for him.
```

**Drill:** I prepare it for him

## 3. Same idea, said other ways

- **`empezar` = `comenzar`** — both "to start", both take `a` + to-form. `empezar` is more common; `comenzar` is the "commence" cognate.
- **"to start to" vs "to start out"** — `comenzar a hablar` vs `comenzar hablando`: the same verb, the link (or its absence) shifts the meaning.
- **"to finish" vs "to end up"** — `terminar de vender` vs `terminar vendiéndolos`: same verb, the `de`/-ing choice does the work.
- **`sigo` / `sigue`** = "I continue / he continues" — one verb covers "follow" and "continue".
- **`se`** wears four hats (verb `sé`, self, phonetic, on-me) — same spelling, four jobs; only the context tells them apart.

---

## 4. Emphasis & bends

- **The link is the meaning.** `seguir` takes -ing bare; `empezar`/`comenzar` take `a`; `terminar` takes `de`. Swap the to-form for an -ing form after `empezar`/`terminar` and the meaning bends to "start out" / "end up".
- **Mood is optional flavor, often.** The most interesting uses of the mood tense are the **optional** ones — they let you add humor, sarcasm, sincerity, or irritation. `ojalá`, `es esencial que`, `es probable que` all flip the next verb to its mood form.
- **`ojalá` carries history.** A fossil of Arabic *ojo-Allah* sitting in everyday Spanish — `Ojalá deje un mensaje` (hopefully she leaves a message), `Ojalá sea pronto` (hopefully it'll be soon, with irregular mood `sea`).
- **The on-me `se` is recognition-first.** You can always say `La batería se acaba` plainly; `se me acaba` just adds "…and it happened to *me*." Drill it to hear it, not because you must produce it.
- **Spelled-out vowels reveal the mood.** Online chat is great practice — the `-e` vs `-a` mood endings jump off the page where the ear might miss them.
