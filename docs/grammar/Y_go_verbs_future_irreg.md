# Bundle Y — Go-verbs + future contractions + irregular point-past start (Lessons 84–87)

The bundle where irregular verbs stop being scary and start being **families**. You meet
the **"go verbs"** (their *I*-form ends in *-go*: *tengo, vengo, salgo*), watch that *-go*
morph predictably across mood, commands, and the future, and tidy up the past — line-past
(almost all regular) and a new irregular point-past family (*tuve, estuve, pude, dije*).

Everything here rides on one idea: **irregularities cluster into groups**. Learn the group,
not the word.

```
[ go-verb I-form ]   →   -go swaps for...   →   what you get
     tengo                  -ga (mood/cmd)        tenga, no tengas
     tengo                  -go dropped (cmd)     ten
     tener (full)           -dr- contraction      tendré / tendría
     tener (full)           tuv- + mixed past     tuve, tuvo
```

> **Grammar terms** appear in *(italics)* next to the plain label, e.g. **mood tense**
> *(present subjunctive)*. Plain label first so it reads like Mad Libs; the real term is there when
> you want it. **Slots** are written in `[brackets]` — fill them in.

## 1. Word groups

Words sorted by **the job they do in a sentence**, not by dictionary order. Drill the group, not the word.

### The "go verbs" *(verbs with irregular -go first-person present)* — their **I-form** ends in `-go`
| verb | I-form | English | note |
|------|--------|---------|------|
| `tener`  | `tengo`  | I have (possession) | only `tengo` has the `-go`; rest is normal: *tienes, tiene, tenemos, tienen* |
| `venir`  | `vengo`  | I come | *vienes, viene, venimos, vienen* |
| `salir`  | `salgo`  | I leave / go out | |
| `poner`  | `pongo`  | I put | |
| `decir`  | `digo`   | I say / tell | extra irregular elsewhere, but still `-go` here |
| `hacer`  | `hago`   | I do / make | |
| `oír`    | `oigo`   | I hear | |
| `traer`  | `traigo` | I bring | takes an extra `i`: t-r-a-**i**-g-o |

Only the **I-form** carries the `-go`. Everything that follows in this bundle is just that
`-go` getting reshaped.

### The `traer` "-tract" family *(Latin tract- cognates)* — translate the *traer* inside as English **-tract**
| Spanish | English |
|---------|---------|
| `extraer`   | to extract |
| `atraer`    | to attract |
| `contraer`  | to contract |
| `distraer`  | to distract |

`me distraigo` = "I get distracted." These inherit `traer`'s `traig-`: *me distraigo*.

### Past-only irregulars *(line-past irregular verbs)* — the **only** three line-past irregulars
| verb | line-past | English | why |
|------|-----------|---------|-----|
| `ser` | `era, eras, era, éramos, eran` | I/you/he… was/were | think English "an era → *era*"; accent stays on first *e* (*éramos*) |
| `ir`  | `iba, ibas, iba, íbamos, iban` | I/you/he… went | landed on the `-aba` sound |
| `ver` | `veía, veías, veía, veíamos, veían` | I/you/he… saw | keeps the extra *e* from *veo* → *veía*, not *vía* |

Everything else in the line-past is regular (see §2, S7). These three are worth memorizing.

### Point-past irregular family *(preterite with irregular stem)* — root changes, mixed endings, no accent
| verb | new base | I-form | English |
|------|----------|--------|---------|
| `tener` | `tuv-`  | `tuve`   | I had |
| `estar` | `estuv-`| `estuve` | I was (states) |
| `poder` | `pud-`  | `pude`   | I was able |
| `decir` | `dij-`  | `dije`   | I said |

`tuve / estuve` go together as a memory pair ("I had / I was").

### Little words
| Spanish | English | term |
|---------|---------|------|
| `vale`  | ok (Spain) | filler |
| `aquí`  | here | adverb |
| `cuando`| when | conjunction |
| `dónde` | where | question word |
| `más tarde` | later | time phrase |
| `esta noche`| tonight | time phrase |
| `quiero que`| I want that… (triggers mood) | trigger phrase |

## Mood, commands, future — how one `-go` reshapes (the big rule of this bundle)

Not a sentence structure, but the single pattern that powers most drills below. Take a
go-verb's `-go` I-form and reshape it four ways:

```
go-verb I-form   →   reshape                          →   result
   tengo             -go → -ga, spread to all persons      tenga, tengas, tengamos, tengan   (mood)
   tengo             formal command = the mood form        tenga / no tengas                 (commands)
   tengo             informal +command = drop the -go      ten                               (command)
   tener (full)      -er/-ir → -dr- (contraction)          tendré (will) / tendría (would)   (future)
```

- **Mood swap:** `-go → -ga`, and that `-ga` base is used for **all persons** — *tenga,
  tengas, tengamos, tengan*. (*venga, salga, ponga, diga, haga, oiga, traiga*.)
- **Formal commands** = the mood form: *Salga* (leave!), *Salgan* (you-all), *Póngalo aquí*
  (put it here — pronoun attaches to the **end** of a positive command).
- **Negative commands** = mood form, pronoun goes **before**: *No lo ponga aquí*, *No salgan*.
- **Informal positive command** = just **drop the `-go`** and stop: *ten, sal, ven, di, pon*.
  (Pronoun still attaches: *Tenlo*, *Dime*.)
- **Future "will" / conditional "would"** = contraction. Most go-verbs swap `-er/-ir` for
  `-dr-`: *tendré, vendré, saldré, pondré*. **Will and would share the same base** — swap
  `-é` for `-ía`. Two extra-irregulars: *hacer → haré / haría*, *decir → diré / diría*.
  *poder* contracts too (drops its *e*: *podré / podría*) but is **not** a go-verb.
  *traer / atraer / distraer* do **not** contract: *traeré, atraeré, distraeré*.

## 2. Sentence structures (drill these)

Each structure: a **shape** line (the only "code"), the slots you can swap, worked examples, then a **Drill** the student finishes out loud or on paper.

---

### S1 — "I want you to ___" (the mood trigger) *(quiero que + present subjunctive)*
**shape:** `quiero que + [go-verb-mood]`

After `quiero que`, the verb goes to the **mood** form (`-go → -ga`). Don't use the plain
present here: *traes* (you bring) becomes *traigas* (that you bring).

```
Quiero que vengas.            I want you to come.
Quiero que lo traigas.        I want you to bring it.
No quiero que me distraigas.  I don't want you to distract me.
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** I want you to come · I want you to bring it · I don't want you to distract me · I want you to put it here

---

### S2 — Formal commands (the mood form, used as an order) *(formal imperative)*
**shape:** `[go-verb-mood](+[pronoun])`

The mood tense by itself works as a formal command/order. Pronoun attaches to the **end**.

| build | result | English |
|-------|--------|---------|
| ponga + lo | `Póngalo aquí.` | Put it here. (formal) |
| diga + me | `Dígame.` | Tell me. (formal — Spanish phone answer) |
| salga | `Salga.` | Leave. (formal) |
| salgan | `Salgan.` | Leave. (formal, you-all) |

**Drill:** Put it here (formal) · Tell me (formal) · Leave (formal) · Tell me when you do it (formal — *Dígame cuando lo haga*)

---

### S3 — Negative commands (mood form, pronoun moves up front) *(negative imperative)*
**shape:** `no + [pronoun] + [go-verb-mood]`

Negative commands also use the mood form, but the pronoun goes **before** the verb.

```
No lo ponga aquí.   Don't put it here. (formal)
No salgan.          Don't leave. (you-all)
```

**Drill:** Don't put it here · Don't leave (you-all) · Don't distract me

---

### S4 — Informal positive command: drop the `-go` *(informal imperative shortcut)*
**shape:** `[I-form − go](+[pronoun])`

For **informal positive** commands, skip the mood — just chop the `-go` off the I-form.

| I-form | command | English | with pronoun |
|--------|---------|---------|--------------|
| `tengo` | `ten`  | have!  | `Tenlo` (have it!) |
| `salgo` | `sal`  | leave! | |
| `vengo` | `ven`  | come!  | |
| `digo`  | `di`   | say/tell! | `Dime` (tell me!) |
| `pongo` | `pon`  | put!   | |

Phone pair: *Dígame* (formal) vs *Dime* (informal). Note *ven* = physical "come here," while
*venga, venga* has drifted into a fixed "come on, come on."

**Drill:** Come! · Leave! · Tell me! (informal) · Have it!

---

### S5 — "I will ___" / "I would ___" (future contractions) *(future & conditional with irregular stem)*
**shape:** `[contracted-base] + [-é/-ía endings]`

Go-verbs squeeze to 2 syllables: the `-er/-ir` becomes `-dr-`. Same base for will and would.

| verb | will | would |
|------|------|-------|
| `tener` | `tendré` | `tendría` |
| `venir` | `vendré` | `vendría` (we: `vendríamos`) |
| `poner` | `pondré` | `pondría` |
| `salir` | `saldré` | `saldría` |
| `hacer` | `haré`   | `haría` (extra-irregular) |
| `decir` | `diré`   | `diría` (extra-irregular) |
| `poder` | `podré`  | `podría` (not a go-verb) |

```
Lo pondré aquí.                            I will put it here.
Vendríamos más tarde, pero no tenemos tiempo.   We would come later, but we don't have time.
Lo haríamos, pero estamos ocupados ahora.  We would do it, but we're busy now.
Te diré más tarde.                         I'll tell you later.
```

Listen for *podría* (would be able to, *poder*) vs *pondría* (would put, *poner*) — one *n*, one not.

**Drill:** I will put it here · We would come later · I'll tell you later · Will you go out tonight? (*¿Saldrás esta noche?*)

---

### S6 — Dodge the future with the present *(present tense for future reference)*
**shape:** `[present verb] + [future time word]`

When a time word makes the future obvious (*más tarde*, *esta noche*), you can stay in the present.

```
Te digo más tarde.     I'll tell you later.   (literally "I tell you later")
¿Sales esta noche?     Are you / will you go out tonight?
```

**Drill:** I'll tell you later (present) · Are you going out tonight?

---

### S7 — Line-past vs point-past, and the irregular point-past family *(imperfect vs preterite)*
**shape (regular line-past):** `[stem] + -aba` (`-ar`) / `[stem] + -ía` (`-er/-ir`)
**shape (irregular point-past):** `[new base] + [mixed endings, no accent]`

The line-past is the safe, regular tense (only *ser/ir/ver* irregular, §1). The point-past
has an irregular family: **(a)** the base changes, **(b)** endings **mix** — `-e`/`-o` from
the `-ar` group plus `-imos`/`-iste`/`-ieron` from the `-er/-ir` group, **(c)** **no written
accent** (regular *tuvé*? no — *tuve*; regular *habló* but irregular *tuvo*).

| verb | base | full point-past |
|------|------|-----------------|
| `tener` | `tuv-`   | tuve, tuviste, tuvo, tuvimos, tuvieron |
| `estar` | `estuv-` | estuve, estuviste, estuvo, estuvimos, estuvieron |
| `poder` | `pud-`   | pude, pudiste, pudo, pudimos, pudieron |
| `decir` | `dij-`   | dije, dijiste, dijo, dijimos, dijeron |

```
¿Dónde estuviste?      Where were you? (point — one specific time)
¿Dónde estabas?        Where were you? (line — the easier fallback)
Tuve que ir.           I had to go. (one specific moment)
Tenía que ir.          I had to go / needed to go. (line, regular)
```

**Avoidance trick:** in the moment you can swap the irregular point-past for the regular
line-past (*estabas* for *estuviste*; *tenía* for *tuve*) — the meaning shifts a touch but
gets across. Don't lean on it forever, though: if you don't take control of a tense, it
controls you.

**Drill:** Where were you? (point) · Where were you? (line) · I had to go (point) · I had to go (line)

## 3. Same idea, said other ways

- **The mood form is reusable:** *tenga* is the mood after `quiero que`, the formal command,
  AND (with `no`) the negative command. One form, three jobs.
- **Two ways to give an informal "tell me":** *Dime* (drop-`-go` shortcut) vs the formal
  *Dígame* (mood + me). Same English, different register.
- **Will ↔ would share a base:** know *tendré* and you know *tendría* — just swap `-é` for
  `-ía`. Never relearn the irregular stem twice.
- **Point-past or line-past for "I had to go":** *Tuve que ir* (one moment) vs *Tenía que ir*
  (line). The line-past is the safe dodge when the point-past escapes you.
- **Present for future:** *Te digo más tarde* and *Te diré más tarde* both mean "I'll tell
  you later" — context carries the future when a time word is present.

---

## 4. Emphasis & bends

- **Only the I-form takes `-go`.** *tengo* but *tienes/tiene/tenemos/tienen*. Don't sprinkle
  `-go` across the other persons.
- **Pronoun placement flips by command type.** Positive = glued to the **end** (*Póngalo*,
  *Tenlo*, *Dime*); negative = **before** the verb (*No lo ponga*). The mood form is the same
  either way.
- **`-dr-` is the future tell.** *tener/venir/poner/salir* all squeeze to a `d`. *hacer/decir*
  break that and go *haré/diré*; *poder* just drops its *e* (*podré*) and isn't even a go-verb.
- **`traer` opts out of contraction.** *traeré*, not *tradré*. So do *atraer/distraer*.
- **Point-past endings refuse the accent.** *tuve/tuvo*, not *tuvé/tuvó* — the giveaway that
  you're in the irregular family, not the regular *hablé/habló* one.
- **After `cuando` about the future, use the mood.** *Dime cuando vengas* (tell me when you
  come/arrive), *Dígame cuando lo haga*.
