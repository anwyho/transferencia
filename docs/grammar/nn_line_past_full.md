# Bundle Ñ — Line-Past, Full (Lessons 47–49)

The bundle where the **line-past** (the "was ___ing / used to ___" tense) goes from
half-built to complete. You already had `-aba` for `-ar` verbs; now you get `-ía` for
`-er`/`-ir` verbs, the three irregulars you can't avoid (*era, iba, veía*), and the
little word `se` — Spanish's catch-all for the missing English word "get".

Everything here snaps together from a handful of moving parts:

```
[ subject ]   [ se? ]   [ line-past verb ]   [ rest ]
  la cena       se          quemaba          (en el sol)
 (optional)   (when no    (-aba / -ía /
              object)      era/iba/veía)
```

> **Grammar terms** appear in *(italics)* next to the plain label, e.g. **line-past**
> *(imperfect)*. Plain label first so it reads like Mad Libs; the real term is there when
> you want it. **Slots** are written in `[brackets]` — fill them in.

---

## 1. Word groups

Words sorted by **the job they do in a sentence**, not by dictionary order. Drill the group, not the word.

### `-ar` verbs *(first-conjugation verbs)* — chop `-ar`, the line-past adds `-aba`
| Spanish | English | line-past (he/she/I) |
|---------|---------|----------------------|
| `cenar`   | to dine / have dinner | `cenaba` |
| `quemar`  | to burn               | `quemaba` |
| `hablar`  | to speak              | `hablaba` |
| `cambiar` | to change             | `cambiaba` |

Make a verb from a noun by attaching the ending: `la cena` (dinner) → `cenar` (to dine).

### `-er` / `-ir` verbs *(second/third-conjugation verbs)* — chop the ending, the line-past adds `-ía`
| Spanish | English | line-past (he/she/I) |
|---------|---------|----------------------|
| `comer`   | to eat   | `comía` |
| `vender`  | to sell  | `vendía` |
| `romper`  | to break | `rompía` |

### Irregular line-past verbs *(irregular imperfects)* — memorize, only three
| Spanish | English | line-past |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| `ver`  | to see                  | `veía` (keeps the extra `e` from `veo`) |
| `ser`  | to be (characteristics) | `era` (an "era" is a stretch of past time) |
| `ir`   | to go                   | `iba` (a blend of `-aba` and `-ía` sounds) |

### `se` *(reflexive / impersonal marker)* — the missing-word tool
| Spanish | English | job |
|---------|---------|-----|
| `se quema`   | it burns / gets burnt | wraps up a verb with no object |
| `se rompe`   | it breaks / gets broken | same |
| `se vende`   | for sale ("it sells itself") | impersonal |
| `se habla español` | Spanish is spoken | impersonal |
| `se queda`   | he/she stays | changes meaning (`quedar` → `quedarse`) |

All the `l-` words (`lo, la, los, las`) become `se` in the "self" version: `los quedan` → `se quedan`.

### `tener` family *(possession verbs; `-tain` → `-tener`)*
| Spanish | English | line-past |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| `tener`    | to have (possession) | `tenía` |
| `contener` | to contain | `contenía` |
| `obtener`  | to obtain  | `obtenía` |
| `mantener` | to maintain (`man-` like `mano`, hand) | `mantenía` |
| `sostener` | to sustain | `sostenía` |

`tengo que` + verb = "I have to / must"; line-past `tenía que` = "I had to" (see §2, S6).

### `llamar` *(reflexive for names: `llamarse`)*
| Spanish | English |
|---------|---------|
| `me llamo` | I call myself / my name is |
| `¿cómo te llamas?` | what's your name? (informal) |
| `¿cómo se llama?`  | what's his/her/your-formal name? |

### Little words
| Spanish | English | term |
|---------|---------|------|
| `todo el tiempo` | all the time | adverbial phrase |
| `aquí` | here | adverb |
| `ayer` | yesterday | adverb |
| `pero ahora` | but now | conjunction + adverb |
| `en` | in | preposition |
| `este / esta / estos / estas` | this / these (m/f, sing/pl) | demonstrative |
| `ese / esa / esos / esas` | that / those (m/f, sing/pl) | demonstrative |

`este → ese`: just pluck out the `t` to switch "this" → "that".

## The line-past, fully assembled — the big rule of this bundle

The line-past = the **"was ___ing / used to ___"** tense *(imperfect)*. One Spanish form
covers both English phrasings, plus I/he/she/you-formal all at once.

```
-ar verb  →  chop -ar, add -aba   →  cenaba   (I/he/she was dining / used to dine)
-er verb  →  chop -er, add -ía    →  comía    (I/he/she was eating / used to eat)
-ir verb  →  chop -ir, add -ía    →  vivía    (… was living)
```

**Don't confuse line-past `-ía` with "would":** for "would" you ADD `-ía` to the *full*
to-form (`comer → comería`, I would eat); for the line-past you CHOP first
(`comer → comía`, I was eating). Same sound, different build.

| would (full form + -ía) | line-past (chop, then -ía) |
|-------------------------|----------------------------|
| `comería` — I would eat | `comía` — I was eating |
| `rompería` — I would break | `rompía` — I was breaking |
| `vendería` — I would sell | `vendía` — I was selling |

## 2. Sentence structures (drill these)

Each structure: a **shape** line (the only "code"), the slots you can swap, worked examples, then a **Drill** the student finishes out loud or on paper.

---

### S1 — "was ___ing / used to ___": the `-aba` line-past *(imperfect, `-ar` verbs)*
**shape:** `(subject) + [verb-stem]+aba`

- verb-stem: `cen-` · `quem-` · `habl-` · `cambi-`

The `-aba` form covers **I / he / she / it / you-formal** — context tells you which.

**Paradigm (`-aba`, using `cenar`):**
| who | form | English |
|-----|------|---------|
| I / he / she / you-formal | `cenaba` | was dining / used to dine |
| you (informal) | `cenabas` | were dining |
| they / you-guys | `cenaban` | were dining |
| we | `cenábamos` | were dining (accent on `á`) |

```
cenaba              I was having dinner / used to dine
quemaba la cena     he was burning the dinner
hablaba             I/he/she was speaking
no se quedaban aquí they weren't staying here
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** I used to dine · he was burning the dinner · we were speaking · they weren't staying here

---

### S2 — Same idea, `-er`/`-ir` verbs: the `-ía` line-past *(imperfect, `-er`/`-ir` verbs)*
**shape:** `(subject) + [verb-stem]+ía`

- verb-stem: `com-` · `vend-` · `romp-`

**Paradigm (`-ía`, using `comer`):**
| who | form | English |
|-----|------|---------|
| I / he / she / you-formal | `comía` | was eating / used to eat |
| you (informal) | `comías` | were eating |
| they / you-guys | `comían` | were eating |
| we | `comíamos` | were eating |

```
comía       I was eating
lo vendía   I was selling it
lo rompía   I/he was breaking it
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** I was eating · I was selling it · he was breaking it · we were eating

---

### S3 — The missing-word `se`: "it gets ___ed" *(reflexive / mediopassive `se`)*
**shape:** `[subject] + se + [verb]`  *or*  `se + [verb] + [subject]` (order is flexible)

When the action **isn't done to something else** — it just happens — wrap the verb with
`se`. This is how Spanish covers English **"get"** (get burnt, get broken). Without `se`,
the sentence feels incomplete — Spanish is waiting to hear *what* got affected.

| with `se` (it just happens) | without `se` (done to an object) |
|------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| `la cena se quemaba` — the dinner was burning | `quemaba la cena` — he was burning the dinner |
| `se rompe` — it breaks (gets broken) | `lo rompo` — I break it |
| `me quemo en el sol` — I get burnt in the sun | `el sol me quema` — the sun burns me (already complete) |

```
la cena se quemaba / se quemaba la cena   the dinner was burning
se rompe todo el tiempo                    it breaks all the time
se rompen todo el tiempo                   they break all the time
esos se rompen todo el tiempo              those ones break all the time
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** the dinner was burning · it breaks all the time · I get burnt in the sun · those ones break all the time

---

### S4 — Impersonal `se`: "for sale", "is spoken", "how do you say…?" *(impersonal `se`)*
**shape:** `se + [verb] (+ thing)`

No specific subject — `se` makes it general. You see it on signs.

```
se vende             for sale (it sells itself)
se vendía            it was for sale
se habla español     Spanish is spoken
¿cómo se dice?       how do you say it? (how does it say itself?)
```

Don't say `¿cómo dices?` — that means "how do *you personally* say it?". `decir` is
slightly irregular: `dice, dices, dicen, decimos` (the "we" form escapes the irregularity).

**Drill (EN → ES):** for sale · it was for sale · Spanish is spoken · how do you say it?

---

### S5 — `quedar` / `quedarse`: "I have ___ left" and "I stay" *(indirect-object `quedar` vs reflexive `quedarse`)*
**shape (left over):** `(no) + me/le + queda(n) + [thing]`
**shape (stay):** `me/se/nos + queda(+ending)`

`quedar` alone = "to remain / be left over"; the verb agrees with **the thing remaining**.
Add reflexive `se` and the meaning shifts to "to stay".

| form | English | which job |
|------|---------|-----------|
| `me queda uno` | I have one left | left-over (one thing) |
| `me quedan dos` | I have two left | left-over (two things) |
| `le queda dos` | he/she has two left | switch the pronoun for who |
| `no me queda(n)` | I don't have any left | negated, both heard |
| `me quedo` | I stay | `quedarse` = stay |
| `nos quedamos` | we stay | |
| `se queda` / `se quedan` | he/she stays / they stay | |
| `nos quedábamos aquí` | we were staying here | line-past of `quedarse` |

**Drill (EN → ES):** I have one left · I have two left · I don't have any left · we were staying here

---

### S6 — `tener que` / `tenía que`: "I have to / had to ___" *(obligation; line-past for "had to")*
**shape:** `tengo/tenía que + [verb-toform]`

Present `tengo que` = "I must"; line-past `tenía que` = "I had to / was having to" — English
says "had", Spanish uses the line-past.

```
tengo que verte               I have to see you
tenía que verte               I had to / was having to see you
tenía que verte ayer          I had to see you yesterday
¿no tenías que hacerlo ayer?  didn't you have to do it yesterday?
```

`tener` line-past keeps the accent on `-ía`: `tenía, teníamos`. Possession too:
`tenía una casa grande` — I used to have a big house (adjective *after* the noun).

**Drill (EN → ES):** I have to see you · I had to see you · I had to see you yesterday · didn't you have to do it yesterday?

---

### S7 — The three irregular line-pasts: `era`, `iba`, `veía` *(irregular imperfects)*
**shape:** `(subject) + [era / iba / veía …]`

These don't follow `-aba`/`-ía` cleanly — learn the forms.

**`ser` (to be — characteristics):**
| who | form | English |
|-----|------|---------|
| I / he / she | `era` | was / used to be |
| you (informal) | `eras` | were |
| they | `eran` | were |
| we | `éramos` | were (accent on `é`) |

**`ir` (to go):**
| who | form | English |
|-----|------|---------|
| I / he / she | `iba` | was going |
| you (informal) | `ibas` | were going |
| they | `iban` | were going |
| we | `íbamos` | were going |

**`ver` (to see):** `veía, veías, veía, veíamos, veían` — keeps the extra `e` from `veo`.

```
era estudiante       I used to be a student (no word for "a" needed)
¿eras estudiante?    were you a student?
éramos estudiantes   we were students
veía / veíamos       I was seeing / we were seeing
iba a llamarte       I was going to call you
íbamos a hacerlo     we were going to do it
```

`iba a` + verb = "I was going to ___" — don't drop the `a` (motion verbs need it).

**Drill (EN → ES):** I used to be a student · were you a student? · we were seeing · I was going to call you

---

### S8 — `llamarse` for names, in the past too *(reflexive for naming)*
**shape:** `me/te/se + llama(+ending)`

Introduce yourself with `me llamo` (I call myself), not a word-for-word "my name is".

```
me llamo…             my name is / I call myself
¿cómo te llamas?      what's your name? (informal)
¿cómo se llama?       what's his/her/your-formal name?
¿cómo se llamaba?     what was his/her/your-formal name?
¿cómo se llamaban?    what were their names?
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** my name is… · what's your name? · what was his name? · what were their names?

## 3. Same idea, said other ways

- **Line-past = two English phrasings, one Spanish form:** `cenaba` is both "I was dining" and "I used to dine." Spanish doesn't split them.
- **Word order is flexible thanks to `se`:** `la cena se quemaba` = `se quemaba la cena`. Spanish has more grammar tools than English, so order matters less.
- **`era` covers "a":** `era estudiante` = "I used to be **a** student" — no separate word for "a" needed.
- **`-ía` does double duty by build:** chop-then-`-ía` = past (`comía`); full-form-plus-`-ía` = "would" (`comería`). Same ending, opposite tenses.
- **`no me queda` vs `no me quedan`:** both are heard for "I don't have any left."

---

## 4. Emphasis & bends

- **Drop the subject.** The verb ending already names the person; `él quemaba la cena` adds `él` only to stress **HE** was the one burning it.
- **`se` first, then build.** Before adding `se`, go back to the to-form so you don't mangle the ending: is it `-ar`, `-er`, or `-ir`? (Lesson 47, rule 12.)
- **`l-` words collapse to `se`.** In the "self" version, `lo/la/los/las` all become `se`: `se quedan`, never `los quedan`.
- **`quedar` agrees with the thing left:** `me queda uno` (one) vs `me quedan dos` (two) — the verb tracks the remaining item, not you.
- **The "we" form escapes irregularity.** `decimos` (we say) goes back toward `decir` because the accent lands elsewhere — a recurring pattern.
- **Demonstrative echo:** `este` echoes the `e` of `el`; `esta` the `a` of `la`; `estos` the `-os` of `los`. Pluck the `t` for "that": `este → ese`.
