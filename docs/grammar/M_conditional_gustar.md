# Bundle M — Preposition pairs + conditional + gustar (Lessons 40–43)

The bundle where Spanish stops borrowing English's prepositions and starts handing you
its own (*enamorarse **de***, *pensar **en***, *soñar **con***). Then comes the big payoff:
a single accent-on-the-end trick that builds **"would ___"** (*bailaría*) from any verb, plus
**"would have ___"** (*habría ido*) and **should** (*debería*). And *gustar*, the verb that
flips your whole sentence back-to-front: "I like it" → "it pleases me."

Everything here is built from a few moving parts you snap together:

```
[ pronoun ]   [ verb (full to-form) ]   [ ending ]      [ rest ]
   me           bailar                     -ía           con ella
 (who it's      (the action, kept          (-ía = would)  (preposition +
  pleasing to)   whole)                     -é = will)     its object)
```

> **Grammar terms** appear in *(italics)* next to the plain label, e.g. **would-form**
> *(conditional)*. Plain label first so it reads like Mad Libs; the real term is there when
> you want it. **Slots** are written in `[brackets]` — fill them in.

---

## 1. Word groups

Words sorted by **the job they do in a sentence**, not by dictionary order. Drill the group, not the word.

### Verb + its preposition *(verb-preposition collocations)* — the preposition is welded to the verb; it is NOT the English one
| Spanish | English | the trap |
|---------|---------|----------|
| `enamorarse de` | to fall in love **with** | Spanish says "of" (*de*), not "with" |
| `pensar en`     | to think **about**        | Spanish says "in/on" (*en*), not "about" |
| `soñar con`     | to dream **about / of**   | Spanish says "with" (*con*), not "of" |
| `depender de`   | to depend **on**          | Spanish says "of" (*de*), not "on" |

Learn the verb and its preposition as **one unit**. The English preposition is no guide.

### Conditional & future endings *(conditional / future verb endings)* — glue onto the **full** to-form verb
| Spanish | English | which "person" |
|---------|---------|----------------|
| `-ía`    | would (I / he / she / it / you-formal) | I rides with the he/she group |
| `-ías`   | would (you informal) | |
| `-íamos` | would (we) — accent stays on the **í** | |
| `-ían`   | would (they / you guys) | |
| `-é, -ás, -á, -emos, -án` | **will** (future) | same slots, "will" instead of "would" |

Accent always lands on the ending — Spanish "pushes the accent into the future."

### Object pronouns *(object pronouns: `me/nos` = to me/us · `lo` = it · `le/les` = to him-her / them)* — sit in front of the changed verb, or glue to the to-form
| Spanish | English | example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| `me`  | (to) me        | `me gusta` (it pleases me) |
| `nos` | (to) us        | `nos gustaría` (we'd like) |
| `lo`  | it / him       | `deberíamos hablarlo` (we should talk about it) |
| `le`  | to him/her/you-formal | `hablarle` (speak to him/her) |
| `les` | to them / you guys | `hablarles` (speak to them) |

### Preposition forms with mí / ti *(prepositional pronouns)* — the form a pronoun takes after a preposition
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `para mí` / `para ti` | for me / for you | |
| `de mí` / `de ti`     | from me / from you | |
| `en ti` / `en ella`   | about you / about her (with *pensar*) | |
| `conmigo` / `contigo` | with me / with you | **one word** — see rule below |

### Helper / modal verbs *(modal verbs)* — pull a second verb in the to-form behind them
| Spanish | English |
|---------|---------|
| `tengo que` | I have to |
| `puedo` / `puedes` | I can / you can |
| `podría`  | I/he/she could |
| `debería` | I/he/she should |
| `me gustaría` | I would like |

### Little words
| Spanish | English | term |
|---------|---------|------|
| `que`   | that (connector) | conjunction |
| `qué`   | what (question)  | interrogative — same word, accented |
| `si`    | if  | conjunction |
| `sí`    | yes | adverb — same word, accented |
| `más tarde` | later | adverb |
| `ñ`     | the n-y sound (*mañana*, *soñar*) | the one letter English lacks |

## Bundle-specific rules — the engine room

Not sentence structures, but the rules that power the drills below.

**1. The preposition is part of the verb.** *Enamorarse* (to fall in love) is really *enamorar* + *se*, so *me enamoro* = I fall in love, and it takes **de**: *me enamoro **de** ti*. Likewise *pienso **en** ti*, *sueño **con** ella*, *depende **de** ella*. Memorize the pair.

**2. `con` + me/you fuses into one word.** "With me" = `conmigo`, "with you (informal)" = `contigo`. The `-go` comes from nowhere — just memorize it. (Other prepositions stay split: *para mí*, *de ti*.)

**3. The would-/will-factory.** Take the **whole** verb (the to-form, accent and all) and bolt an ending on the end:
```
full verb        +   ending   →   result
 bailar              -ía           bailaría    (I would dance)
 bailar              -é            bailaré     (I will dance)
 comer               -ía           comería     (I would eat)
 ir                  -ía           iría        (I would go)
```
One set of endings for **all** verb types (-ar/-er/-ir). The accent goes on the ending.

**4. A few common verbs contract.** Heavy use wears a letter away: *poder* → ~~podería~~ → `podría`, *haber* → ~~habería~~ → `habría`. Same `-ía` endings, just a vowel dropped.

**5. `habría` + past participle = "would have ___."** Put the *-ado / -ido* form right after: `habría ido` (would have gone), `habríamos comido` (we would have eaten). Same shape as English.

**6. `debería` = should.** It's the conditional of *deber* (to owe/must) — literally "would owe," used for "should." The next verb stays in the to-form: *deberíamos hablar* = "we should (to) speak."

**7. `gustar` runs back-to-front.** It means **"to be pleasing,"** not "to like." "I like X" → flip it to **"X is pleasing to me"** before you build:
```
English "I like it"   →   Spanglish "it pleases me"   →   Me gusta
English "I like them" →   Spanglish "they please me"  →   Me gustan
```
The verb agrees with **the thing that pleases**, not with you. Start with the verb, attach the pronoun in front. (Never *me gusto* — that's "I like myself.")

## 2. Sentence structures (drill these)

Each structure: a **shape** line (the only "code"), the slots you can swap, worked examples, then a **Drill** the student finishes out loud or on paper.

---

### S1 — Verb + its own preposition: "I think about you," "I dream about her" *(verb-preposition collocation)*
**shape:** `[verb-with-pronoun] + [its preposition] + [object]`

- verb pair: `me enamoro de` · `pienso en` · `sueño con` · `depende de`
- object: `ti` · `él` · `ella` · `mí`

```
me enamoro de ti     I fall in love with you   (Spanish: "of you")
pienso en ti         I think about you         (Spanish: "in you")
sueño con ella       I dream about her         (Spanish: "with her")
depende de ella      it depends on her         (Spanish: "of her")
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** I fall in love with him · I think about her · I dream about you · it depends on you

---

### S2 — "with me / with you" fuses: `conmigo` / `contigo` *(prepositional pronoun, fused)*
**shape:** `[verb] + conmigo` *(or `contigo`)*

`con` + *mí/ti* becomes one word. Everything else stays normal.

```
¿Quieres venir conmigo?      Do you want to come with me?
voy contigo                  I'm going with you
tienes que venir conmigo     you have to come with me
¿por qué no quieres venir conmigo?   why don't you want to come with me?
```

**Drill:** Can you come with me? · I'm going with you · Don't you want to come with me?

---

### S3 — "I would ___" (the conditional) *(conditional tense)*
**shape:** `[full verb] + [-ía / -ías / -íamos / -ían]`

Full verb + ending. Accent on the ending. One endings-set for every verb.

| build | result | English |
|-------|--------|---------|
| bailar + -ía   | `bailaría`    | I would dance |
| bailar + -íamos| `bailaríamos` | we would dance |
| comer + -ía    | `comería`     | I would eat |
| ir + -ía       | `iría`        | I would go |
| hablar + -ían  | `hablarían`   | they would speak |

Worked sentences:
```
no iría                          she wouldn't go
comería, pero no tengo tiempo    I'd eat, but I don't have time
transformaría la situación       it would transform the situation
me imaginaría que no quiere ir   I'd imagine that he doesn't want to go
```

**Conditional paradigm (drill the whole column):**

| person | ending | *bailar* → |
|--------|--------|------------|
| I / he / she / it / you-formal | `-ía`    | bailaría |
| you (informal) | `-ías`   | bailarías |
| we | `-íamos` | bailaríamos |
| they / you guys | `-ían`   | bailarían |

**Drill:** I would speak · we would dance · they wouldn't speak · it would get transformed (`se transformaría`)

---

### S4 — "I would like ___" — `gustar`, back-to-front *(gustar inversion, conditional)*
**shape:** `[pronoun] + gustaría + [to-form verb]`

Flip "I'd like to ___" into "**it would please me** to ___," then build. Start with the verb; attach the pronoun in front.

```
me gustaría comer ahora      I would like to eat now    (it would please me to eat)
me gustaría verte            I would like to see you
nos gustaría comer ahora     we would like to eat now   (NOT gustarían — that's "they'd please")
```

**The gustar agreement table — the verb matches the THING, not the person:**

| Spanish | literal | English |
|---------|---------|---------|
| me gusta + [singular] | it pleases me | I like it |
| me gustan + [plural]  | they please me | I like them |
| nos habría gustado    | it would have pleased us | we would have liked it |
| nos habrían gustado   | they would have pleased us | we would have liked them |

**Drill:** I would like to eat now · we would like to see you · I like it · I like them

---

### S5 — "I would have ___" / "we would have ___" *(conditional perfect: habría + participle)*
**shape:** `habría/habrías/habríamos/habrían + [past participle]`

Past participle = *-ado* for -ar verbs, *-ido* for -er/-ir verbs. Build complex sentences block by block.

| build | result | English |
|-------|--------|---------|
| habría + ido      | `habría ido`            | I would have gone |
| habríamos + comido| `habríamos comido`      | we would have eaten |
| habríamos + podido + ir | `habríamos podido ir` | we would have been able to go |
| habrían + venido + a comer | `habrían venido a comer` | they would have come to eat |
| me + habría + gustado | `me habría gustado` | I would have liked it |

The verb after a verb of motion (*venir*) keeps its `a`: *habrían venido **a** comer*.

**Drill:** I would have gone · we would have eaten · they would have been able to eat · we would have liked it

---

### S6 — "should" and "could": `debería` / `podría` + to-form *(modal conditionals)*
**shape:** `[debería… / podría…] + [to-form verb] (+pronoun)`

After should/could, Spanish **keeps** the "to" form — literally "we should *to* speak." The little pronoun goes before the changed verb OR glued to the to-form.

```
deberías venir a mi casa             you should come to my house
deberíamos hablar                    we should talk
deberíamos hablarlo                  we should talk about it      (drop "about", use lo)
deberíamos hablarle / hablarles      we should speak to him-her / to them
¿podrías venir a mi casa?            could you come to my house?
¿me podrías decir algo? / ¿podrías decirme algo?   could you tell me something?
```

To dodge the ambiguity of *le* (him/her/you-formal), swap to `con`: *deberíamos hablar **con él / con ella***.

**Drill:** we should talk · you should come to my house · could you come to my house? · we should talk about it

---

### S7 — "they would give it to me": stacking pronouns *(double object pronouns, conditional)*
**shape:** `[me/nos] + [lo] + [verb-ía]`

Two pronouns line up before the verb: the "to whom" first, the "what" second.

```
daría            I would give
darían           they would give
me darían        they would give (to) me
me lo darían     they would give it to me
```

**Drill:** I would give · they would give me · they would give it to me

## 3. Same idea, said other ways

- **Three futures, one meaning.** "We'll dance later" = present + time word (*bailamos más tarde*), or *ir a* + verb (*vamos a bailar*), or true future (*bailaremos*). All land on the same idea.
- **"I imagine" = "I imagine myself."** *Imagino* and *me imagino* mean the same; the *me* adds nothing here.
- **"Right now" falling in love.** *Estoy enamorándome* OR *me estoy enamorando* — the pronoun rides the *-ando* tail or sits before *estoy*. Both correct.
- **Pronoun placement is free with modals.** *¿Me podrías decir?* = *¿Podrías decirme?* — before the changed verb or stuck on the to-form.
- **`debería` is "would owe."** It literally means "would owe" (from *deber*, to owe), repurposed as English "should."

---

## 4. Emphasis & bends

- **Start with the verb.** Especially for *gustar*: lock in *gustaría* first, THEN attach *nos*. Reaching for *gustarían* ("they would please") is the classic slip. Control the verb, control the sentence.
- **The verb agrees with the pleasing thing.** *Me gusta* (one thing) vs *me gustan* (many). Same with *habría/habrían gustado*. "Me/nos" never drives the verb — the *thing* does.
- **Accent rides the ending.** *bailaría*, *bailaríamos* — the í never moves, even in the "we" form. This is what marks would/will apart from the bare verb.
- **Keep the "to."** After *debería*, *podría*, *querría*, *me gustaría*, the next verb stays in the to-form. English drops "to"; Spanish keeps it.
- **`si` vs `sí`, `que` vs `qué`.** No accent = *if* / *that* (connectors). Accent = *yes* / *what* (the loud ones). Same word, two jobs: *¿podrías decirme **si** quieres venir?*

## Carried-in lexicon (from earlier bundles)

- `voy`, `vamos a` + verb — the going-to future, reused in S5/§3 and as a contrast to the true future.
- `haber` forms (*he, has, ha, hemos, han*) — the sound source for the future endings (perfect-tense bundle).
- Past participles *-ado / -ido* with *haber* — reused in S5's *habría ido / comido*.
- `-ción → -ar` verb factory (*imaginación → imaginar*, *transformación → transformar*) — extended here into conditional forms.
- `le / les` ambiguity (him/her/you-formal vs them) and the *hablar con* fix — carried in from the indirect-pronoun bundle (L37–39).
- Vowel-split verbs (*sueño* vs *soñamos*) — the stress-driven o→ue split seen earlier, shown here with *soñar*.
