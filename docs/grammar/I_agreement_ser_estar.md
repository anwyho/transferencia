# Bundle I — Full agreement + ser/estar split (Lessons 28–30)

The bundle where Spanish words start **agreeing** with each other. Nouns and adjectives
flex for gender (male/female) and number (one/many), you meet the second "to be" verb
(*estar*, for **states**), and you get the full set of subject pronouns plus three new
helpers (*tener que*, *deber*, *quedarse*).

Two big ideas drive almost every sentence below:

```
[ agreement ]                        [ which "to be"? ]
 noun + adjective + verb-ending        ser  = what it IS      (characteristic)
 all flex for gender & number          estar = what STATE it's in
   caro / cara / caros / caras         es bueno   vs   está cansado
```

> **Grammar terms** appear in *(italics)* next to the plain label, e.g. **state verb**
> *(estar)*. Plain label first so it reads like Mad Libs; the real term is there when
> you want it. **Slots** are written in `[brackets]` — fill them in.

---

## 1. Word groups

Words sorted by **the job they do in a sentence**, not by dictionary order. Drill the group, not the word.

### "To be" — states *(estar: present indicative)* — for what STATE something is in
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `estoy`   | I am          | ends in `-oy`, like `voy` |
| `estás`   | you (inf) are | accent on the LAST syllable (e-stás) |
| `está`    | he/she/it is, you (formal) are | accent on the last syllable too |
| `estamos` | we are        | accent back in its normal place (two syllables added) |
| `están`   | they / you guys are | |

The only present-tense verb that stresses the last syllable: **e-stóy, e-stás, e-stá, e-stán**. Won't happen again.

### "To be" — characteristics *(ser: present indicative)* — for what something IS
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `es`  | is / he is / she is / it is / you (formal) are | carried in from earlier |
| `son` | they are / you guys are | learn `es` / `son` as a **pair** |

`un ser` = "a being" (the verb's noun form); `un ser humano` = "a human being".

### Subject pronouns *(subject pronouns)* — emphasis / clarity only
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `yo`    | I | drop unless stressing *who* |
| `tú`    | you (informal) | |
| `él` / `ella` | he / she | |
| `usted` | you (formal) | |
| `nosotros` / `nosotras` | we (m / f) | `nosotras` for an all-female group |
| `ellos` / `ellas` | they (m / f) | |

The verb ending usually says who already (*estás* = "you"); pronouns are for emphasis or clarity.

### New helpers *(main verb + infinitive)* — start a sentence, take a to-form verb
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `tener que` + verb | to have to | needs the `que` — `tener` alone won't do it (see §2, S6) |
| `deber` + verb | must / probably | also expresses probability ("he must be here") |
| `quedarse` | to stay (lit. "remain oneself") | reflexive — carries `me` / `nos` (§2, S7) |

`tener` = "to have"; Spanish `-tener` shows up in English as `-tain` (*mantener* = maintain, *contener* = contain).

### Little words
| Spanish | English | term |
|---------|---------|------|
| `muy`     | very | adverb |
| `cómo`    | how  | question word |
| `dónde`   | where | question word |
| `siempre` | always | adverb (flexible position) |
| `aquí` / `acá` | here | adverbs (interchangeable) |
| `porque`  | because | conjunction |
| `más tarde` | later | adverb phrase |

`muy`: the `y` sounds like an `i` — "moo-ee".

## Agreement — the big rule of this bundle

Not a sentence structure, but it shapes the `[adjective]` and `[noun]` slots in every drill below.

```
            masc. sing.   fem. sing.   masc. plural   fem. plural
-o adjective  caro          cara          caros          caras
consonant adj. global       global        globales       globales   (no gender flip; +es plural)
person noun   profesor      profesora     profesores     profesoras
```

Rules to internalize:

- **Adjectives ending in `-o`** flip to `-a` for feminine: *caro → cara*. Adjectives ending in
  any other letter (like `-l`) **don't** flip: *la situación global* (never "globala").
- **Plural:** add `-s` to a vowel ending (*caros, caras*); add `-es` to a consonant ending
  (*profesor → profesores*).
- **Agreement follows the noun, not the real-world object.** Call a car *máquina* → *es cara*;
  call it *auto* → *es caro*.
- **Person-nouns** can take `-a` to mark a real person's gender: *profesor → profesora*,
  *doctor → doctora*. (Adjectives are stricter — only `-o` ones flip.)
- **Mixed group → masculine plural:** *son agresivos*, *son profesores*.
- **Masculine singular = the "neuter"** — the form for when you don't know what you're
  describing: *es intensivo* ("it's intensive", nothing specific in mind).

### Two spelling/sound watch-outs

- **Double-letter rule:** random English double letters (gg, ss, ff) collapse to single in
  Spanish — *aggressive → agresivo*. The **only** doubles that survive are **rr** and **nn**:
  *carro*, *con-notación*. (And `rr` vs `r` changes meaning: *carro* = car, *caro* = expensive;
  *perro* = dog, *pero* = but.)
- **`-tivo` words:** pronounce the `s`/`v` cleanly — *positivo* = "po-see-tee-vo", not "po-zee-...".

## The ser-vs-estar split — pick the right "to be"

English crams both into one word. Spanish makes you choose. **Don't** shorthand it to
"permanent vs temporary" — that fails often enough to frustrate you. Always ask:
**is it a STATE, or is it a CHARACTERISTIC?**

| Use `estar` (STATE) | Use `ser` (CHARACTERISTIC) |
|---------------------|----------------------------|
| tired — *estoy cansado* | being a good/bad person — *es bueno* |
| location — *¿dónde estás?* | profession — *es profesor* (even 5 min on the job) |
| dead / exhausted — *estoy muerto* | identity — *un ser humano* |

`siempre` doesn't change this: *siempre estoy cansada* still uses `estar` — "always tired" is
still a state, just one you keep landing in.

## 2. Sentence structures (drill these)

Each structure: a **shape** line (the only "code"), the slots you can swap, worked examples, then a **Drill** the student finishes out loud or on paper.

---

### S1 — "He/she/it is ___" / "They are ___" with agreement *(ser + adjective; gender & number agreement)*
**shape:** `[es/son] + [adjective]`  *(copula + agreeing adjective)*

The adjective agrees with the subject's gender and number.

- verb: `es` (one) · `son` (more than one)
- adjective: `caro` · `atractivo` · `creativo` · `agresivo` · `negativo` · `intuitivo`

```
es caro             it is expensive (masc. noun)
es cara             it is expensive (fem. noun)
son caros           they are expensive
son agresivos       they are aggressive
son intuitivas      they (women) are intuitive
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** she is creative · they are aggressive · it (la máquina) is expensive · they (women) are native

---

### S2 — Naming what someone IS *(ser + noun; no article needed)*
**shape:** `[es/son] + [person-noun]`

For profession/identity, Spanish drops the "a": *es profesora*, not "es una profesora".

| build | result | English |
|-------|--------|---------|
| es + profesor   | `es profesor`     | he is a teacher |
| es + profesora  | `es profesora`    | she is a teacher |
| es + doctora    | `es doctora`      | she is a doctor |
| son + profesoras | `son profesoras` | they (women) are teachers |

**Drill:** he is a doctor · she is a teacher · they (women) are teachers · they (mixed) are teachers

---

### S3 — "I am ___" (a state) *(estar + adjective)*
**shape:** `[estar-form] + [adjective]`  *(estar + agreeing adjective)*

State adjectives still agree in gender and number.

- verb: `estoy` · `estás` · `está` · `estamos` · `están`
- adjective: `cansado/cansada` · `muerto/muerta`

```
estoy cansada       I am tired (female)
estamos cansados    we are tired (mixed/male group)
estamos cansadas    we are tired (all female)
estoy muerta        I am dead / exhausted
```

**Drill:** I am tired (male) · we are tired (all female) · they are dead · I am exhausted (female)

---

### S4 — Ask "how?" and "where?" *(question word + estar)*
**shape:** `¿[cómo/dónde] + [estar-form]?`

State + location are both `estar` territory, so both questions use it.

```
¿Cómo estás?            How are you? (informal)
¿Cómo está?             How are you? (formal)
¿Cómo están?            How are you all?
¿Dónde estás?           Where are you?
No sé dónde estamos.    I don't know where we are.
```

**Drill:** How are you (formal)? · Where are you? · How are you all? · I don't know where we are

---

### S5 — "I want / am going to BE here" — estar as an infinitive *(helper + estar infinitive)*
**shape:** `[helper] (a?) estar + [place/state]`

`estar` slots into the to-form just like any verb. Motion-`a` rule still applies (`voy a`).

```
Quiero estar aquí.            I want to be here.
Voy a estar aquí más tarde.   I'm going to be here later.
¿Dónde vas a estar?           Where are you going to be?
```

**Drill:** I want to be here · I'm going to be here later · where are you going to be? · I want to be here later

---

### S6 — "I have to ___" and "must / probably ___" *(tener que + infinitive; deber + infinitive)*
**shape:** `[tener-form] que + [verb]`  ·  `[deber-form] + [verb]`

`tener que` always needs the `que` — *tienen estar* is wrong, must be *tienen que estar*.
`deber` is regular (*debe*, *deben*) and doubles as "probably/must be".

```
¿Dónde tienen que estar más tarde?   Where do you guys have to be later?
Debe estar aquí.                     He must be here.
Debe estar cansado.                  He must be tired.
Deben estar cansadas.                They (women) must be tired.
```

**Drill:** where do you all have to be later? · he must be here · they must be tired (women) · she must be tired

---

### S7 — Reflexive "to stay" *(quedarse: reflexive verb; pronoun position)*
**shape:** `[me/nos] + [quedar-form]`  OR  `... a + quedar+[nos]`  *(proclitic or enclitic reflexive)*

"I stay" is literally "I remain myself" — the reflexive piece can ride the **front** of the
conjugated verb or **glue to the end** of an infinitive. Both legal.

| form | English |
|------|---------|
| `me quedo` | I stay |
| `nos quedamos` | we stay |
| `No nos vamos a quedar.` = `No vamos a quedarnos.` | We're not going to stay. |

```
No vamos a quedarnos porque estamos muy cansados.
We're not going to stay because we are very tired.
```

**Drill:** I stay · we stay · we're not going to stay · we're not going to stay because we're tired

## 3. Same idea, said other ways

- **`es` vs `está`** — same English "is", different jobs: *es cansado* would mean "he's a
  tiresome person" (characteristic); *está cansado* = "he's tired" (state). Pick by meaning.
- **"Here"** → `aquí` or `acá` — interchangeable in many places.
- **`nosotros` / `nosotras`** — same "we", switches for an all-female group, just like adjectives.
- **Pronoun position with `quedarse`** — *no nos vamos a quedar* and *no vamos a quedarnos*
  are the same sentence; the reflexive just rides a different verb.
- **`siempre` floats** — *siempre estoy cansada* · *estoy cansada siempre* · *estoy siempre
  cansada* all work; the emphasis shifts but it's all legal.
- **Car / money** have regional twins: *carro / auto / máquina / coche*; *dinero / plata / pasta*.

## 4. Emphasis & bends

- **Agreement is the default.** The adjective and verb flex to match the noun every time —
  *cara*, *caros*, *caras* — there's no "neutral" form except the masculine singular.
- **Drop the subject pronoun.** *Estás* already says "you"; add `tú`/`ella` only to stress or
  disambiguate (*ella es muy atractiva*).
- **State or characteristic?** The one question to keep asking. Location and "tired/dead" →
  `estar`; profession and identity → `ser`.
- **`tener que` keeps its `que`.** Obligation needs the extra word — never drop it.
- **Reflexive can sit front or tail.** `me quedo` (front of conjugated verb) or `quedarnos`
  (tail of infinitive) — your choice.
- **Doubles collapse except rr/nn.** *agresivo*, not "aggresivo"; but *carro*, *connotación* keep theirs.

- `voy` / `voy a` + infinitive and `vamos` — the motion-`a` future, reused in S5 (Bundle B).
- `quiero` + infinitive — the helper from Bundle B, now taking `estar` (S5).
- Enclitic/proclitic pronoun position (`me`, `te`, `lo` on the verb tail) — extended here to the
  reflexive `me` / `nos` of `quedarse` (S7, Bundle B).
- Word-factory endings: `-ity → -idad` (*humanidad*), `-ive → -ivo` (*positivo*) join the
  `-ción → -ar` thread from Bundle A/B.
- `muerto` — first met as the "death-pact" inside *mortgage*; here it's the state "dead/exhausted".
