# Bundle P — Irregular participles + relatives + -emos/-imos (Lessons 54–57)

The bundle where the *-ado/-ido* word stops being only a past helper-partner and starts
pulling double duty as an **adjective** (*está cerrada*, *está roto*). You also pick up the
**irregular participles** that refuse the regular ending (*roto, dicho, hecho, visto, abierto*),
the **relative words** that flip prepositions to the front (*la chica con que trabajo*), the
one tiny spot where *-er* and *-ir* verbs disagree (*comemos* vs *vivimos*), and the *lo*
tricks (*lo interesante*, *lo de Juan*).

Everything here is built from three moving parts you snap together:

```
[ haber ]   [ participle ]        OR   [ estar ]   [ participle-as-adjective ]
   he          cancelado                  está          cancelada
 (helper)    (never changes)            (state)        (agrees: gender + number)
```

> **Grammar terms** appear in *(italics)* next to the plain label, e.g. **to-form verb**
> *(infinitive)*. Plain label first so it reads like Mad Libs; the real term is there when
> you want it. **Slots** are written in `[brackets]` — fill them in.

---

## 1. Word groups

Words sorted by **the job they do in a sentence**, not by dictionary order. Drill the group, not the word.

### Irregular participles *(irregular past participles)* — come after `haber`, refuse the regular `-ado/-ido`
| to-form | participle | English | also a noun? |
|---------|------------|---------|--------------|
| `romper` | `roto`    | broken  | |
| `morir`  | `muerto`  | dead    | |
| `abrir`  | `abierto` | open / opened | |
| `cerrar` | `cerrado` | closed  | (regular, paired here as opposite of *abierto*) |
| `decir`  | `dicho`   | said / told | `un dicho` = a saying |
| `hacer`  | `hecho`   | done / made | `un hecho` = a fact |

Memorize these — saying *rompido, morido, abrido* is the giveaway of a beginner.
*Dicho* and *hecho* both carry the **/ch/** sound — learn them as a pair.

### Relative words *(relative pronouns)* — link "the noun I do X with" to its verb
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `que`   | that / which / who | **easy mode** — covers all three, use it for everything |
| `cual / cuales` | which | match **number**; fancier (`con la cual`) |
| `quien / quienes` | who / whom | match **number**; for people |

The preposition (`con`, `para`, `por`, `de`) goes **in front** of these — never at the end (see §2, S3).

### `lo` + word *(neuter article `lo`)* — makes an abstract "the ___ thing"
| build | result | English |
|-------|--------|---------|
| `lo` + interesante | `lo interesante` | the interesting thing |
| `lo` + complicado  | `lo complicado`  | the complicated thing |
| `lo` + bueno / malo | `lo bueno / lo malo` | the good / the bad thing |
| `lo` + de + Juan   | `lo de Juan`     | Juan's business / the thing about Juan |

`lo` here is **not** "it" — it's a little machine that turns an adjective (or `de` + noun) into "the X thing".

### Little words
| Spanish | English | term |
|---------|---------|------|
| `así`   | like this / like that | adverb (everyday; `de esta forma/manera` are the heavy versions) |
| `junto/-a/-os/-as` | together | adjective (agrees with the people) |
| `se`    | itself / oneself | reflexive marker (`se ha complicado`) |
| `tengo que` | I have to | obligation hook |
| `sobre` | about / on | preposition |

## The -ado/-ido word, three jobs — the big rule of this bundle

Not a sentence structure, but it governs §2 below. One verb hands you three things:

```
verb (cansar)  →  me canso       (I tire myself / get fed up)   — conjugated
               →  me he cansado  (I have gotten tired)          — after haber
               →  cansado/-a     (the adjective: tired)         — describes a noun
```

Two rules keep the participle straight:

- **After `haber` it NEVER changes** — always `cerrado`: *lo he cerrado, la he cerrado, lo hemos cerrado*.
- **As an adjective it DOES change** — agrees in gender + number: *cerrado / cerrada / cerrados / cerradas*.

And the verb-of-choice: **these `-ado/-ido` adjectives almost always pair with `estar`**, not
`ser`. *La puerta está cerrada* — don't even weigh state-vs-characteristic, just reach for `estar`.

> **Exception — when you name a noun, use `ser`.** *Es una silla* (it's a chair) →
> *Es una silla mojada* (it's a wet chair) — still `ser`, because the **noun** dominates.
> But *La silla está mojada* (the chair is wet) → `estar`, because now it's the chair's **state**.
> Same with people: *Es una persona cerrada* (a closed person — `ser`, naming) vs
> *Está cerrada* (she's closed off right now — `estar`, state). `Muerto` only ever takes
> `estar` — Spanish sees death as a state.

## 2. Sentence structures (drill these)

Each structure: a **shape** line (the only "code"), the slots you can swap, worked examples, then a **Drill** the student finishes out loud or on paper.

---

### S1 — "It is ___" with a participle-adjective *(participle as predicate adjective + estar)*
**shape:** `[estar] + [participle](agrees)`

- estar: `está` · `están` · `estoy`
- participle: `cerrado` · `cancelado` · `roto` · `abierto` · `muerto` · `terminado` · `vendido`

Agree the ending with the noun: feminine `-a`, plural `-s`.

```
Está cerrada           It is closed (la puerta — fem)
Está cancelado         It is cancelled (masc)
La fiesta está cancelada   The party is cancelled (fiesta = fem)
Están vendidas         They are sold (las casas — fem pl)
Está hecho             It is done
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** It is broken (la silla) · They are open (masc pl) · I'm tired (fem speaker) · The movie is finished

---

### S2 — "It has got ___" with the `se ha` trick *(reflexive `se` + present perfect, inchoative)*
**shape:** `se + ha + [participle]`

Think "it has X-ed itself" → "it became X". With `morir`, the `se` is optional and barely changes meaning.

```
Se ha complicado       It has got complicated
Se ha roto             It has broken
Ha muerto. / Se ha muerto.   He has died (se optional)
```

**Drill:** It has got complicated · It has broken · He has died

---

### S3 — Relative clause: "the ___ I do X with" *(relative clause; preposition fronting)*
**shape:** `[noun] + [preposition] + que + [verb]`

The English habit is to leave the preposition dangling ("the girl I work **with**"). Spanish
**flips it to the front**: "the girl **with** which/that I work". `que` covers that / which / who.

| English | Spanish | why |
|---------|---------|-----|
| the girl I work with | `la chica con que trabajo` | `con` moves in front of `que` |
| the girl I was working with | `la chica con que trabajaba` | `trabajaba` = line-past (used to / was -ing) |
| the girl I was going to the party with | `la chica con que iba a la fiesta` | `iba` = irregular line-past of `ir` |

Fancier (all fine, all equivalent): *con la cual* · *con la que* · *con quien*. Don't bother
now — `que` works for everything. And **never repeat the preposition at the end**.

**Drill:** the girl I work with · the boy I was working with · the girl I was going to the party with

---

### S4 — Questions with `cuál` and `quién` (prepositions still fly to the front) *(interrogatives + preposition fronting)*
**shape:** `(¿)[preposition] + cuál/quién + [verb]...(?)`

- which one (choosing): `cuál` · plural `cuáles`
- who: `quién` · plural `quiénes` (use when you know it's more than one person; verb matches)

```
¿Cuál quieres?                       Which one do you want?
No sé con cuál quiere hacerlo.       I don't know which one he wants to do it with.
¿Quién va?  /  ¿Quiénes van?         Who is going? / Who (pl) are going?
No sé con quién tengo que hacerlo.   I don't know who I have to do it with.
No sé para quién tengo que hacerlo.  I don't know who I have to do it for.
```

The preposition rides up front: `con quién`, `para quién`, `por quién`, `de quién` — same flip as S3.

**Drill:** Which one do you want? · Who is going? · I don't know who I have to do it with · I don't know who I have to do it for

---

### S5 — "Should have / could have done it" *(conditional of deber/poder + perfect infinitive)*
**shape:** `[debería/podría] + haber + [participle] (+ [pronoun])`

- should: `debería` · you-should `deberías`
- could: `podría` · we-could `podríamos`

The little word (`lo`, `le`, `me`…) can go **in front** of the unchanged form OR **stick onto the end of `haber`** — both fine.

```
Debería haber cancelado            I should have cancelled
Podría haber ido                   I could have gone
Lo debería haber hecho.  /  Debería haberlo hecho.   I should have done it
Deberías haberlo hecho.            You should have done it
```

**Drill:** I should have cancelled · I could have gone · I should have done it (both placements) · You should have done it

---

### S6 — The one `-er` / `-ir` split: the "we" form *(present indicative, 1st-person plural)*
**shape:** `[-er stem]+emos`  vs  `[-ir stem]+imos`

In the **whole** language this is the **only** place `-er` and `-ir` verbs differ. Everywhere
else they share endings (he eats `come`, he lives `vive`; they eat `comen`, they live `viven`).

| verb | "we" form | English |
|------|-----------|---------|
| `comer` (-er) | `comemos` | we eat |
| `vivir` (-ir) | `vivimos` | we live |
| `hablar` (-ar) | `hablamos` | we speak |

```
No comemos así         We don't eat like that
No vivimos así         We don't live like that
Lo interesante es que viven juntos   The interesting thing is they live together
```

**Drill:** we eat · we live · we don't speak like that · the interesting thing is they live together

---

### S7 — `lo` + adjective and `lo de` + thing *(neuter article `lo` for abstractions)*
**shape:** `lo + [adjective]`  ·  `lo de + [noun]`

```
Lo interesante es que viven aquí.   The interesting thing is that they live here.
Lo complicado es que vienen juntos. The complicated thing is they come together.
Lo de la fiesta                     the thing about the party
Quiero organizar lo de mañana.      I want to organize the thing about tomorrow.
No quiero hablar de lo de Juan así. I don't want to talk about Juan's business like this.
```

Word order with the little word: `No quiero hablar de lo de Juan así` = `No quiero hablarlo así` — front or tail, both fine.

**Drill:** the interesting thing · the good thing · the thing about the party · I want to organize the thing about tomorrow

## 3. Same idea, said other ways

- **Relative `que` vs the fancy forms:** `la chica con que trabajo` = `con la cual` = `con la que` = `con quien` — same girl, just different formality. Use `que`.
- **`morir` and `se`:** `ha muerto` = `se ha muerto` — the `se` is optional here, meaning barely shifts.
- **`así` vs the heavy versions:** `así` = `de esta forma` = `de esta manera`. `Así` is the everyday word; the others are emphatic.
- **`lo de` is swappable:** `hablamos de lo de la fiesta` = `hablamos sobre lo de la fiesta` = `hablamos lo de la fiesta` — `de`/`sobre` both mean "about", and you can even drop it.
- **One verb, three outputs:** *cansar* → *me canso* (I tire myself) / *me he cansado* (I have gotten tired) / *cansado* (tired). Same root, three jobs.

---

## 4. Emphasis & bends

- **Estar is the default for these adjectives.** `está cerrada`, `está roto`, `está hecho`, `estoy muerta` — don't agonize over state-vs-characteristic; the `-ado/-ido` adjective almost always wants `estar`. Switch to `ser` only when you're **naming a noun** (*es una silla mojada*).
- **`muerto` = `estar` only.** Never *es muerto*. Death is a state.
- **`muerto` and `me muero` go emotional.** `Estoy muerta` = "I'm exhausted", not literally dead. `Me muero` = "I'm dying" (laughing / of embarrassment) — common slang.
- **Prepositions never dangle.** The single thing to internalize from S3–S4: `con`, `para`, `por`, `de` fly to the **front** of `que` / `cuál` / `quién` — *¿De dónde eres?*, *la chica con que trabajo*, never trailing at the end.
- **Drop the subject.** `-aba`/`-emos`/`-imos` already carry who; `yo` only for stress. `La chica con que trabajo` — no `yo` needed.
- **Build forms straight from the to-form.** Go directly from `comer` → `comemos`; don't detour through other forms or you'll get lost. Then check if it sounds right — if not, suspect an irregular.
