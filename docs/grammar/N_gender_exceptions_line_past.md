# Bundle N — Gender exceptions + gustar family + line-past intro (Lessons 44–46)

The bundle where the neat *-o*/*-a* gender rule grows a few exceptions, where a whole
family of verbs **flips the sentence around** (*me gusta*, *me interesa*, *me parece*),
and where Spanish's **three pasts** finally get named — starting with the "line in the
past" (*hablaba* = I was speaking / used to speak).

Three moving parts to keep separate:

```
[ gender exception ]   [ flip-verb ]   [ which past ]
  la mano / la foto      me gusta        he hablado  (have spoken)
  el problema            te interesa     hablaba     (was speaking — LINE)
  (looks wrong, isn't)   me parece       habría salido (would have — would-time)
```

> **Grammar terms** appear in *(italics)* next to the plain label, e.g. **line-past**
> *(imperfect)*. Plain label first so it reads like Mad Libs; the real term is there when
> you want it. **Slots** are written in `[brackets]` — fill them in.

## 1. Word groups

Words sorted by **the job they do in a sentence**, not by dictionary order. Drill the group, not the word.

### Gender-exception nouns *(nouns whose gender defies the -o/-a ending)* — memorize the article *with* the word
| Spanish | English | why it's an exception |
|---------|---------|----------------------|
| `el problema` | the problem | Greek `-ma` word → masculine despite `-a` |
| `el sistema` | the system | Greek `-ma` |
| `el paradigma`| the paradigm | Greek `-ma` |
| `el esquema` | the outline / scheme | Greek `-ma` (note the `e-`: no Spanish word starts `s`+consonant) |
| `el diagrama` | the diagram | Greek `-ma` |
| `el planeta` | the planet | masculine despite `-a` |
| `la mano` | the hand | feminine despite `-o` (root `man-`: manual, manage) |
| `la foto` | the photo | feminine — short for `la fotografía` |
| `la moto` | the motorbike | feminine — short for `la motocicleta` |

`la foto`/`la moto` only "break" the rule because they're clipped from a longer feminine word.

### Flip-verbs / gustar family *(verbs that take an indirect-object pronoun; the subject is the thing that pleases)* — start with `me/te/le/nos/les`, verb agrees with the THING
| Spanish | English (literal angle) | type |
|---------|------------------------|------|
| `gustar` | to be pleasing (to like) | `-ar` |
| `interesar` | to be interesting to (to interest) | `-ar` |
| `parecer` | to seem | **`-er`** — check before you change it! |

All three take `le/les` (not `lo/la`), because the meaning is "pleasing **TO** him" — the "to" hides inside `le`.

### Other verbs this bundle
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `poner` | to put | English `-pose` → `poner`: `componer, suponer, oponer, imponer` |
| `tomar` / `sacar` | to take (a photo) | `tomar/sacar una foto` |
| `manejar` | to handle / manage / (LatAm) drive | from `man-` "hand" |
| `salir` | to leave / go out | motion verb → `salir a bailar` |
| `haber` | to have (past helper, not possession) | `he, has, ha, hemos, han` |
| `esperar` | to wait / to hope | line-past drill verb |
| `encontrar` | to find / encounter | |

### bien vs bueno *(adverb vs adjective)*
| Spanish | English | job |
|---------|---------|-----|
| `bien` | well | adverb — `me parece bien` (seems fine) |
| `bueno` | good | adjective — describes a noun |

There's a fuzzy line between them; you absorb it from listening. `parece` always pairs with `bien`.

### Pointing words *(demonstratives)*
| Spanish | English | trick |
|---------|---------|-------|
| `este, esta, estos, estas` | this / these | has a `t` |
| `ese, esa, esos, esas` | that / those | drop the `t` of `este` → "that" |

### Little words
| Spanish | English | term |
|---------|---------|------|
| `que` | that (connector) | conjunction — **obligatory** after `parecer` |
| `qué` | what | question word (accent) |
| `aquí` | here | adverb |
| `un poco` / `más` | a little / more | quantity |
| `porque` | because | conjunction |
| `pero` | but | conjunction |

## The three pasts — panorama (the orientation for this bundle)

Not a drill, but the map every past sentence sits on. Spanish splits English's tangle of past forms into three jobs:

```
(a) HAVE-past   he comido          I have eaten            haber + participle
(b) LINE past   hablaba            I was speaking / used to speak / would (habitually)
(c) DOT  past   (later bundle)     I ate — one finished moment
```

This bundle finishes (a), adds **"would have…"** (`habría`), and *opens* (b) for `-ar` verbs only. The dot-past comes later.

- **Have-past:** `haber` changes (`he/has/ha/hemos/han`); the participle never does. Chop `-ar`→`-ado`, `-er/-ir`→`-ido`: `hablado, comido, salido, podido`.
- **Would have…:** put `haber` into would-time → `haber + -ía = habería → habría` (the `e` drops). `Habría salido` = I would have gone out. Translate in pieces, don't smoosh.
- **Line-past `-ar`:** chop `-ar`, add `-aba`. `Hablaba` alone covers I / he / she / it / you-formal.

`podría` / `debería` practice (would-time conditionals, carried in): `¿Me podrías dar una mano?` (Could you give me a hand?) · `Me deberías dar una mano` / `Deberías darme una mano` (You should give me a hand).

## 2. Sentence structures (drill these)

Each structure: a **shape** line (the only "code"), the slots you can swap, worked examples, then a **Drill** the student finishes out loud or on paper.

---

### S1 — Pointing at a noun *(demonstrative + noun, with gender agreement)*
**shape:** `[este/ese family] + [noun]`

- this/that picks form from the noun's **real** gender — so `la mano` takes feminine forms even though it ends `-o`.

```
este auto         this car
ese auto          that car        (drop the t)
esta noche        tonight / this night
estas manos       these hands
quiero ese auto   I want that car
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** this night · that car · these hands · I want that car

---

### S2 — Flip-verb: "it pleases / interests me" *(indirect-object verb; verb agrees with the thing)*
**shape:** `[me/te/le/nos/les] + [gusta/gustan · interesa/interesan]`

Start with the verb's pronoun. Singular thing → `gusta/interesa`; plural thing → `gustan/interesan`.

| build | result | English |
|-------|--------|---------|
| me + gusta | `me gusta` | I like it (it pleases me) |
| le + gustan | `le gustan` | he likes them (they please him) |
| te + interesa | `te interesa` | it interests you |
| les + gusta | `les gusta` | they/you-guys like it |

**Drill:** I like it · he likes them · it interests you · they like it

---

### S3 — Flip-verb + a second verb / question / negative *(indirect-object verb + infinitive)*
**shape:** `(no) [pronoun] + [interesa/parece] (+ [verb-toform]) (?)`

`no` goes in front; `¿…?` just wraps it. A following action stays in to-form.

```
¿te interesa?                       does it interest you?
¿por qué no te interesa?            why doesn't it interest you?
¿le interesa salir a bailar esta noche?   does she want to go out to dance tonight?
me interesaría verlo                it would interest me to see it
```

**Drill:** does it interest you? · why doesn't it interest you? · does it interest her to dance tonight? · it would interest me to see it

---

### S4 — "It seems to me that…" *(parecer + que + clause)*
**shape:** `[pronoun] parece + que + [clause]`

`parecer` is **`-er`** — check before changing it. The `que` is **obligatory** in Spanish even though English drops it. `parece` pairs with `bien` (well), not `bueno`.

```
me parece                          it seems to me
me parece que no quiere salir esta noche   it seems he doesn't want to go out tonight
me parece bien                     seems fine to me
no le parece bien                  it doesn't seem right to him
```

**Drill:** it seems to me · it seems he doesn't want to go out tonight · seems fine to me · doesn't it seem right to you (formal)?

---

### S5 — `poner` + glued pronoun: "put it / put salt on it" *(infinitive + enclitic pronoun)*
**shape:** `[helper] + poner+[lo/le(s)] (+ [thing])`

Glue the small word on the end of `poner`. Use `lo` for "it" directly, but `le` to carry "ON it / to it" (no separate word for "on").

| build | result | English |
|-------|--------|---------|
| quiero + poner + lo | `quiero ponerlo aquí` | I want to put it here |
| me gustaría + poner + le + sal | `me gustaría ponerle sal` | I'd like to put salt on it |
| me gustaría + poner + les + sal | `me gustaría ponerles un poco más sal` | I'd like to put a little more salt on them |

**Drill:** I want to put it here · I'd like to put salt on it · I'd like to put a little more salt on it

---

### S6 — Have-past and "would have…" *(perfect: haber + participle; conditional perfect)*
**shape:** `(no) [haber-form / habría] + [participle]`

Chop the to-form's ending first: `-ar`→`-ado`, `-er/-ir`→`-ido`. `haber` changes per person; participle never does.

```
he comido            I have eaten
han bailado          they have danced
no he podido comer   I haven't been able to eat
habría salido        I would have gone out
no habría podido salir   I wouldn't have been able to go out
```

**Drill:** I have eaten · they have danced · I haven't been able to eat · I would have gone out · I wouldn't have been able to go out

---

### S7 — Line in the past, `-ar` verbs *(imperfect, -aba)*
**shape:** `[stem]+[-aba ending] (+ [pronoun for "for X"])`

Chop `-ar`, add the `-aba` ending. One form (`-aba`) covers I / he / she / it / you-formal at once. To say "for you / for him," drop the "for" and use an object pronoun: `te esperaba`, `lo esperaba`.

The `-aba` paradigm (`-ar` verbs):

| who | ending | `hablar` → | English |
|-----|--------|-----------|---------|
| I / he / she / it / you (formal) | `-aba`  | `hablaba`   | was speaking / used to speak |
| you (informal) | `-abas` | `hablabas`  | you were speaking |
| we | `-ábamos` | `hablábamos` | we were speaking (accent stays on `-aba`) |
| they / you guys | `-aban` | `hablaban` | they were speaking |

```
hablaba              I/he/she was speaking, used to speak
hablábamos           we were speaking
te esperaba          I was waiting for you (informal)
lo esperaba          I was waiting for you (formal) / for him
intentaban encontrarlo   they were trying to find it
```

**Drill:** I was speaking · we were speaking · I was waiting for you (informal) · they were trying to find it

## 3. Same idea, said other ways

- **Take a photo:** `tomar una foto` and `sacar una foto` — both work; `sacar` is "take out."
- **`hablaba` is three English tenses at once:** "I was speaking," "I used to speak," "I would speak (every day)" — all one line-past form. Context picks the English.
- **"For me" two ways:** drop it into a pronoun (`intentaban encontrármelo` = they were trying to find it for me) **or** spell it out for emphasis (`…encontrarlo para mí`). Spanish usually prefers the pronoun.
- **`manejar`:** "to handle/manage" everywhere; "to drive" in much of Latin America — same word, context decides.
- **Stacked pronouns order:** `me/te/se/nos` first, `lo/la/los/las` second — `encontrármelo`, never the reverse.

---

## 4. Emphasis & bends

- **Memorize the article with exception nouns.** `el problema`, `la mano` — the ending lies, the article doesn't. Demonstratives then agree with the *real* gender: `esta mano`, `un planeta`.
- **Flip-verbs start with the pronoun, agree with the thing.** `me gustan` (plural thing), `me gusta` (singular). Person lives in `me/te/le`, never in the verb ending.
- **Always check the verb type before changing it.** `parecer` is `-er`, so its forms aren't `-ar` shaped. The to-form is your takeoff point.
- **`le` is the "on/to/for" pronoun.** `ponerle sal` = put salt *on* it — no separate word for "on." `lo` would mean the thing itself.
- **Pasts chop, futures add.** For `he hablado` / `hablaba` you chop the ending first; only *will/would* add onto the whole to-form. `habría` is the one tidy irregularity (`habería` → `habría`).

## Carried-in lexicon (from earlier bundles)

- `no` in front for negation — reused in S3, S4, S6 (Bundle B, S2).
- Enclitic pronoun glued to the to-form (`ponerlo`, `verlo`) — same mechanism as `cancelarlo` (Bundle B, S3).
- Verb of motion + `a` + infinitive: `salir a bailar` (Bundle B, S5).
- Gerund "right now": `estoy tomando una foto`, `estoy manejando el problema` (`-ando`/`-iendo`).
- `-ía` "would" sound → conditionals `gustaría, interesaría, podría, debería`, and `habría` (Lessons 41–43).
- `le/les` for "to him/her," not `lo/la` (Lessons 37–39).
- Spanish can't open with `s`+consonant → adds `e-`: `esquema`, `España`, `espagueti`.
