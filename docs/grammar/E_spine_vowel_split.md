# Bundle E — Spine roll-call + vowel splits (Lessons 14–17)

The bundle where you get the full **spine** of helper verbs — the five words you can park
any to-form verb behind — and the single biggest pronunciation rule of the present tense:
the **vowel split** (*e → ie*, *o → ue*). Master the split and a few hundred common verbs
stop being irregular and start being *predictable*.

Everything here still snaps onto the Bundle B skeleton — only the front of the sentence
grows:

```
[ subject ]   [ spine helper ]   [ verb (to-form) ]   [ pronoun ]
   yo          tengo que             hacer                lo
 (optional)   (the obligation/        (the action)     (glued to end)
              ability/intent)
```

> **Grammar terms** appear in *(italics)* next to the plain label, e.g. **to-form verb**
> *(infinitive)*. Plain label first so it reads like Mad Libs; the real term is there when
> you want it. **Slots** are written in `[brackets]` — fill them in.

---

## 1. Word groups

Words sorted by **the job they do in a sentence**, not by dictionary order. Drill the group, not the word.

### Spine helpers *(modal/auxiliary verbs + infinitive)* — park any to-form verb right after
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `quiero`    | I want to     | desire |
| `voy a`     | I'm going to  | near future — carries `a` (Bundle B, S4) |
| `tengo que` | I have to     | everyday obligation — carries `que` |
| `puedo`     | I can         | related to *power* (*el poder* = the power) |
| `debo`      | I must        | heavier/more serious than *tengo que* |

These five are the **spine**: any "to" form drops in right behind them. `quiero comer`,
`tengo que comer`, `puedo comer`, `debo comer`.

### Subject pronouns *(subject pronouns)* — emphasis only
| Spanish | English | when |
|---------|---------|------|
| `yo`  | I       | only when stressing *who* — the verb's ending already says it |
| `él`  | he      | for emphasis/contrast: *él sí* (he does) |
| `ella`| she     | `ll` is pronounced /y/ → "EY-yah" |

Drop them by default. *Quiere* already = "he/she wants"; add `él`/`ella` only to contrast.

### `-go` verbs *(irregular first-person `-go`)* — the "I" form ends in `-go`
| Spanish | to-form | meaning | note |
|---------|---------|---------|------|
| `pongo`    | poner    | I put     | base of the family |
| `supongo`  | suponer  | I suppose | *suppose → suponer* |
| `compongo` | componer | I compose | *compose → componer* |

The `-pose` family swaps to `-poner`: compose → *componer*, suppose → *suponer*,
impose → *imponer*, expose → *exponer*, oppose → *oponer*. All take the `-go` in "I":
*supongo*, *opongo*. Shared with `tengo`/`vengo`/`salgo` from earlier.

### `saber` — the odd one
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `sé`     | I know        | irregular, **with accent** (not *sabo*); often *lo sé* (I know it) |
| `sabe`   | he/she knows  | regular from here on |
| `sabes`  | you know      | informal |
| `saben`  | they know     | |

`no sé` = I don't know.

### Little words
| Spanish | English | term |
|---------|---------|------|
| `que`    | what / that | *supongo que sí* = I suppose so |
| `cuándo` | when        | question word |
| `qué`    | what        | question word |
| `sí`     | yes / "does"| *ella sí* = she does |
| `más tarde` | later    | adverb phrase |

## The vowel split — the big rule of this bundle

Not a sentence structure, but it reshapes almost every present-tense verb below.

```
Stress lands on the e/o in the ROOT  →  the vowel cracks open
            e  →  ie                         o  →  ue
        querer → quiero                   poder → puedo
```

**Why:** the present tense puts the accent on the **penultimate** (second-to-last) syllable.
That pressure lands on the root vowel and breaks it. Move the stress off the root and the
split disappears (see the `-amos` rule below).

**The boot / shoe shape.** The split hits "I / he-she / they / you-informal" but **not** "we"
— circle those four forms and they make a boot (or shoe) around the unsplit *we* form.

| English start | Spanish | split |
|---------------|---------|-------|
| tempo  | `tiempo` (time) | e → ie |
| concert| `concierto`     | e → ie |
| port   | `puerto`        | o → ue |
| force  | `fuerza`        | o → ue |
| school | `escuela`       | o → ue |
| corp-  | `cuerpo` (body) | o → ue |

**Splits don't *always* happen.** *comer → como* (no split); *comprender → comprendo*
(no split, vs *entender → entiendo* which does); *población* is an exception too. No rule
says which — most do, but you try and listen for feedback.

**The habit that saves you:** always run **back to the "to" form** before you change a verb.
Otherwise your brain "vowel-harmonizes" (makes a nice-sounding matching vowel) and invents
junk like *cuelge* for *cuelga*. Go back to *colgar* and rebuild.

**Reflexive `me duermo`.** *dormir → duermo* (I sleep). To say "I fall asleep," make it
reflexive: `me duermo` (literally "I sleep myself") — a very common pattern.

## 2. Sentence structures (drill these)

Each structure: a **shape** line (the only "code"), the slots you can swap, worked examples, then a **Drill** the student finishes out loud or on paper.

---

### S1 — The spine: "I have to / can / must ___" *(modal + infinitive)*
**shape:** `[spine helper] + [verb-toform](+[pronoun])`

- helper: `quiero` · `voy a` · `tengo que` · `puedo` · `debo`
- verb: `comer` · `hacer` · `verla` · `invitarlo`

```
quiero comer        I want to eat
tengo que comer     I have to eat
puedo comer         I can eat
debo verla          I must see her
voy a intentar comer más tarde   I'm going to try to eat later
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** I have to eat now · I can eat · I must see her · I'm going to try to eat later

---

### S2 — `tener` roll-call + "have to" *(present of *tener*; `tener que` + infinitive)*
**shape:** `tengo que + [verb-toform]`  ·  full present: `tengo / tiene / tienen / tienes`

`tengo` keeps its `-go` (no split — the `ng` holds it together). The other forms **split**
e → ie: *tiene*, *tienen*, *tienes*.

```
tengo que hacerlo     I have to do it      (also: lo tengo que hacer)
tengo que comer ahora I have to eat now
tiene                 he/she has
quiero saber qué tiene  I want to know what he has
```

**Drill:** I have to do it · I have to eat now · he/she has · they have · you (informal) have

---

### S3 — The split paradigm: take any boot verb through its forms *(stem-changing present)*
**shape:** `[root-split form] (yo/él/ella/ellos/tú)` vs `[unsplit] (we)`

This is the table to internalize. Notice the **we** column never splits.

| to-form | I | he/she | they | you (inf) | **we** |
|---------|---|--------|------|-----------|--------|
| `querer` (want)    | quiero   | quiere   | quieren   | quieres   | **queremos** |
| `pensar` (think)   | pienso   | piensa   | piensan   | piensas   | **pensamos** |
| `entender` (understand) | entiendo | entiende | entienden | entiendes | **entendemos** |
| `perder` (lose)    | pierdo   | pierde   | pierden   | pierdes   | **perdemos** |
| `empezar` (start)  | empiezo  | empieza  | empiezan  | empiezas  | **empezamos** |
| `cerrar` (close)   | cierro   | cierra   | cierran   | cierras   | **cerramos** |
| `poder` (can)      | puedo    | puede    | pueden    | puedes    | **podemos** |
| `dormir` (sleep)   | duermo   | duerme   | duermen   | duermes   | **dormimos** |
| `encontrar` (find) | encuentro| encuentra| encuentran| encuentras| **encontramos** |
| `mostrar` (show)   | muestro  | muestra  | muestran  | muestras  | **mostramos** |
| `colgar` (hang)    | cuelgo*  | cuelga   | cuelgan   | cuelgas   | **colgamos** |

\*`cuelgo` splits **and** takes a `-go`, like *tengo*/*vengo*.

```
empiezo             I start
¿cuándo empiezas?   when are you starting?
lo encuentro        I find it
me duermo           I'm falling asleep
```

**Drill:** I think · they think · we think · I lose it · I start · when are you starting · I find it · I'm falling asleep

---

### S4 — `pensar` + verb = "plan on doing" *(*pensar* + infinitive)*
**shape:** `pienso + [verb-toform]`

```
pienso comer            I plan on eating
pienso comer más tarde  I plan on eating later
¿qué piensas?           what do you think?
pienso que sí           I think so  ("I think that yes")
```

**Drill:** I plan on eating · I plan on eating later · what do you think? · I think so

---

### S5 — The `-amos` "we" form (and why it never splits) *(first-person plural)*
**shape:** chop `-ar/-er/-ir`, add `-amos / -emos / -imos`

Adding `-amos` puts **two** syllables on the end, so the stress moves to `-a-` and off the
root. The root vowel is no longer under pressure → **no split**.

| to-form | we form | (not) |
|---------|---------|-------|
| `encontrar` | `encontramos` | not "encuentramos" |
| `mostrar`   | `mostramos`   | |
| `pensar`    | `pensamos`    | |
| `cerrar`    | `cerramos`    | |
| `poder`     | `podemos`     | |
| `entender`  | `entendemos`  | |
| `hablar`    | `hablamos`    | |
| `tomar`     | `tomamos`     | |

```
hablamos      we speak
encontramos   we find
pensamos      we think
```

**Drill:** we speak · we find · we think · we close · we can

---

### S6 — Drop a pronoun in front OR on the tail *(clitic placement with modals)*
**shape:** `[pronoun] + [spine helper] + [verb-toform]`  **or**  `[spine helper] + [verb-toform]+[pronoun]`

With a spine helper + to-form, the object pronoun can ride the **tail** of the to-form *or*
jump to the **front** of the helper — both fine.

| tail version | front version | English |
|--------------|---------------|---------|
| `tengo que hacerlo` | `lo tengo que hacer` | I have to do it |
| `tengo que verla`   | `la tengo que ver`   | I have to see her |
| `debo verla`        | `la debo ver`        | I must see her |

**Drill:** I have to do it (both ways) · I have to see her (both ways) · I must see her (both ways)

---

### S7 — Emphasis & contrast with `yo / él / ella` *(overt subject pronoun for contrast)*
**shape:** `[subject] + [sentence]`

Add the subject only to stress *who*. Word order is flexible — emphasis can also sit in
voice, hands, face.

```
no quiero invitarlo, pero ella sí    I don't want to invite him, but she does
yo no quiero invitarlo, pero ella sí *I* don't want to invite him, but she does
no sé qué quiere                     I don't know what he/she wants
no sé qué quiere él                  I don't know what HE wants (emphatic)
```

**Drill:** I don't want to invite him, but she does · *I* don't want to, but she does · I don't know what HE wants

## 3. Same idea, said other ways

- **`tengo que` vs `debo`** — both "must," like English: *debo* is heavier/more serious;
  *tengo que* is everyday. *Tengo que comer ahora* vs *debo verla*.
- **`entender` vs `comprender`** — both "to understand." *entender* (splits → *entiendo*) is
  more common; *comprender* (no split → *comprendo*) is the easy-to-remember fallback.
- **`empezar` vs `comenzar`** — both "to start." *empezar* is more common; *comenzar* is the
  "commence" cousin.
- **`tomar` vs `beber`** — both can mean "to drink." *tomar* (also "to take") is far more
  common; *¿qué tomas?* can mean "what'll you have to drink?" *beber* exists but is less used.
- **`encontrar`** = to find / to meet / to encounter — one verb, several English shades.
- **`que sí`** — *supongo que sí* / *pienso que sí* = "I suppose/think so" (literally "…that yes").

---

## 4. Emphasis & bends

- **Go back to the "to" form, always.** Before you bend a verb, recall the infinitive
  (*colgar*, *pensar*, *encontrar*) so you split the *real* root and don't vowel-harmonize.
- **The boot shape.** The split hits I / he-she / they / you-informal; the **we** form is
  spared because its stress moved to `-amos`. Circle the four, leave *we* out.
- **`-go` beats the split.** Where a verb has both, the `-go` form doesn't split: *tengo*
  (not *tiengo*), *vengo*, *salgo* — and *cuelgo* (which then splits in its other forms).
- **Nouns wear "the."** *el poder* = the power (noun); *poder* alone = to be able (verb).
  *un encuentro* (an encounter) vs *encuentro* (I find); *una muestra* (a sample) vs
  *muestro* (I show). The `el/la/un/una` tells you it's a noun.
- **Spanish doesn't double `s`.** *posible*, *pasión* — one `s`. It *does* keep `rr` (rolled),
  distinct from a single `r`.

## Carried-in lexicon (from earlier bundles)

- `voy a` + verb — the near-future helper, motion verb pulls in `a` (Bundle B, S4–S5);

  to separate *el poder* from *poder*.
