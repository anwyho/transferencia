# Bundle L — Pronoun migration + reflexive se + prepositions (Lessons 37–39)

The bundle where the little pronouns earn their keep. You learn how *lo/la* flip to
*le/les* the moment they mean "to/for someone," meet the all-purpose self-word *se*
(*quedarse*, *darse cuenta*), and discover that after a preposition Spanish stops using
"him/her/them" and reaches for *él/ella/ellos* — with *mí* and *ti* as the two exceptions.

Everything here is built from a verb plus one or two little words that snap on, in order:

```
[ person word ]   [ thing word ]   [ verb ]
     me               lo           darán       →  me lo darán  (they'll give it to me)
   (to/for who)     (the it)       (built first)
```

> **Grammar terms** appear in *(italics)* next to the plain label, e.g. **person word**
> *(indirect object pronoun)*. Plain label first so it reads like Mad Libs; the real term is
> there when you want it. **Slots** are written in `[brackets]` — fill them in.

---

## 1. Word groups

Words sorted by **the job they do in a sentence**, not by dictionary order. Drill the group, not the word.

### Thing words *(direct object pronouns)* — stand for the "it/them" being acted on
| Spanish | English | example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| `lo`  | it / him (masc)   | `lo veo` (I see it/him) |
| `la`  | it / her (fem)    | `la veo` (I see her/it) |
| `los` | them (masc things)| `los doy` (I give them) |
| `las` | them (fem things) | `las veo` (I see them) |

These are the *thing* in "give **it**, cook **them**." Gender + number, regular `+s` for plural.

### Person words *(indirect object pronouns)* — the "to/for whom"; this is where migration happens
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `me`  | me / to me / for me   | never changes |
| `te`  | you / to you (informal)| never changes |
| `nos` | us / to us            | never changes |
| `le`  | to/for him, her, you (formal) | what `lo/la` **becomes** when it means "to/for" |
| `les` | to/for them, you guys | what `los/las` **becomes** when it means "to/for" |

`me, te, nos` already cover myself/yourself/ourselves/each-other, so they hold still. Only
**he/she/they** force you to choose: `lo/la` (it/him/her) vs `le/les` (to/for) — see §"Pronoun migration".

### Self-word *(reflexive pronoun)* — "himself / herself / itself / themselves"
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `se`  | himself, herself, itself, themselves, yourself (formal), yourselves | start of "**se**lf"; replaces `lo/la/los/las` for the self-meaning |
| `me` `te` `nos` | myself / yourself / ourselves | already carry the self-meaning — **no** `se` needed |

Dictionary form glues `se` to the to-form verb as one word: `quedarse`, `darse cuenta`.

### After-a-preposition words *(prepositional pronouns)* — used after `con/para/de`
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `mí`   | me      | **exception** — not `me`; carries an accent (m-í) |
| `ti`   | you (informal) | **exception** — not `te` (t-i) |
| `él` `ella` | him / her | literally "he/she", not "him/her" |
| `ellos` `ellas` | them (m/f) | |
| `nosotros` `nosotras` | us (m/f) | |
| `usted` `ustedes` | you (formal) / you guys | |

After a preposition Spanish says "with *he*, with *she*" — except *me → mí* and *you → ti*.

### Prepositions *(prepositions)* — the little words these pronouns follow
| Spanish | English |
|---------|---------|
| `con`  | with |
| `para` | for |
| `de`   | of / from |
| `en`   | in / on |

Show movement, position, or direction — one group together.

### Helper / action verbs used in this bundle
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `dar`   | to give | future: `daré, darás, dará, daremos, darán` |
| `quedar`| to stay / fit / suit | self-form `quedarse` = to stay |
| `querer`| to want | `quiero` (the /e/ splits when stressed) |
| `hacer` | to do / make | `hace` = he/she does/makes (not from *haber*) |
| `decir` | to tell / say | the *c* is a "th" sound in most of Spain |
| `comprar`| to buy | `compro, compra, compran` |
| `cocinar`| to cook | from `la cocina` (the kitchen) + `-r` |
| `ver`   | to see | `veo` (slightly irregular — not *vo*) |
| `hablar`| to speak | past participle `hablado` (spoken) |

## Pronoun migration: `lo/la → le/les` — the big rule of this bundle

Not a sentence structure, but it decides which person-word you reach for in almost every drill below.

```
direct (the thing acted on)       indirect (to / for someone)
   lo  la  los  las         →            le        les
   "it / him / her / them"           "to/for him/her/you(formal)/them"
   4 options                         collapse to 2
```

Don't translate "to/for" word-by-word. Listen for the **feeling**:

| feeling | form | example |
|---------|------|---------|
| "Am I being *seen / cooked / spoken*?" (acted on directly) | `lo / la` | `los cocina` — he's cooking **them** (the things) |
| "Am I being cooked *for*? given *to*? spoken *to*?" (the beneficiary) | `le / les` | `le cocina algo` — he cooks **him/her** something |

`me, te, nos` sidestep all of this — `me cocina algo` covers both "cooks me something"
**and** "cooks something for me." Only he/she/they force the choice.

> `le, les` are "imaginary prepositions" — they wrap up *to / for / from* in one word, which
> is why natural Spanish drops the explicit `para él` in favour of `le` (see §3).

## 2. Sentence structures (drill these)

Each structure: a **shape** line (the only "code"), the slots you can swap, worked examples, then a **Drill** the student finishes out loud or on paper.

---

### S1 — Build the verb first, then add one little word *(verb + object pronoun)*
**shape:** `[little-word] + [verb]`

Order to build: first ask *who is doing the verb?*, build the verb form, **then** hang the little words in front.

- little-word: `me` · `te` · `nos` · `lo/la/los/las` · `le/les`
- verb: `darán` · `veo` · `cocina` · `compro`

```
Lo veo.        I see it/him.
Las veo.       I see them (fem).
Les doy algo.  I give them something.
Le hablo.      I speak to him.
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** I see her · I give them (things) · I speak to him · They speak to them

---

### S2 — Stack two little words: person first, then thing *(IO + DO clitic cluster)*
**shape:** `[person-word] + [thing-word] + [verb]`

When you stack them, the **person** word comes first, the **thing** word second. Once the
thing-word is there, drop `algo` — `lo/la` already says "it".

| build | result | English |
|-------|--------|---------|
| me + lo + darán | `¿Me lo darán?` | Will they give it to me? |
| te + lo + darán | `Te lo darán.`  | They'll give it to you. |
| nos + lo + darán | `Nos lo darán.` | They'll give it to us. |
| nos + las + cocina | `Nos las cocina.` | He's cooking them (fem) for us. |

**Drill:** Will they give it to me? · They'll give it to you · They won't give it to us (add `no` in front) · He cooks them for us

---

### S3 — The self-word `se`: "do it to oneself" *(reflexive se)*
**shape:** `se + [verb]`  ·  for I/you/we use `me / te / nos`

To say himself/herself/itself/themselves, use `se`. `me, te, nos` already carry the
self-meaning, so they need no `se`.

```
Me hablo.     I speak to myself.
Se habla.     He/she speaks to him/herself.
Se hablan.    They speak to themselves.
Nos hablamos. We speak to each other.
```

Subject is ambiguous with `se` — clarify with a pronoun: `Él se habla`, `Ella se habla`.

**Drill:** I speak to myself · She speaks to herself · They speak to themselves · We speak to each other

---

### S4 — `quedarse` = to stay (literally "remain oneself") *(reflexive verb)*
**shape:** `[self-word] + quedo/quedas/queda/quedamos/quedan`

You literally say "I remain myself." Self-word matches the person: `me quedo`, `te quedas`,
`se queda`, `nos quedamos`, `se quedan`. Question = just intonation; negate with `no` in front.

```
Me quedo.            I stay.
Te quedas.           You stay.
Se queda.            He/she stays.
¿Te quedas?          Are you staying?
¿Por qué no te quedas?  Why don't you stay?
```

Note: bare `quedar` (no self) also means "to fit/suit" — `Me queda bien` = it suits me well.

**Drill:** I stay · He stays · Are you staying? · Why don't you stay?

---

### S5 — Self-word on the to-form verb: `quiero quedarme` *(infinitive + enclitic reflexive)*
**shape:** `quiero/quiere/quieren + [verb-toform]+[self-word]`

After `querer`, the second verb stays in the to-form. Glue the self-word onto its **end**,
written as one word: `quedarme, quedarte, quedarse, quedarnos`.

```
Quiero quedarme.    I want to stay.
Quiere quedarse.    He/she wants to stay.
Quieren quedarse.   They / you guys want to stay.
```

`quedarse` (with `se`) is the dictionary headword, because `se` is the most general self-form.

**Drill:** I want to stay · She wants to stay · They want to stay

---

### S6 — `darse cuenta` = to realize ("give oneself account") *(reflexive idiom)*
**shape:** `[self-word] + doy/das/da/damos/dan + cuenta`

Build it across all persons; negate with `no` in front.

```
Me doy cuenta.      I realize.
Te das cuenta.      You realize.
Se da cuenta.       He/she realizes.
Nos damos cuenta.   We realize.
No se dan cuenta.   They don't realize.
```

`¿Te das cuenta?` is everyday for "Do you know what I mean?". `se da/dan cuenta` is ambiguous
— clarify with the pronoun: `Ella se da cuenta`.

**Drill:** I realize · You realize · We realize · They don't realize

---

### S7 — Pronouns after a preposition *(prepositional pronouns)*
**shape:** `[preposition] + [él/ella/… / mí / ti]`

After `con/para/de` use the he/she/they words — but **me → mí** and **you → ti**.

| preposition | examples |
|-------------|----------|
| `con` (with)| `con él` · `con ella` · `con nosotros` · `con ustedes` |
| `para` (for)| `para él` · `para ella` · `para mí` · `para ti` |
| `de` (of/from)| `de mí` · `de ti` · `de ellos` · `de ustedes` |

```
Para mí.          For me.
Para ti.          For you.
¿Es de ustedes?   Is it from you guys?
Está con ustedes. It's (located) with you guys.
```

Use `es` (ser) for belonging-style ("it's *for/from* you"); `está` (estar) for location
("it's *with* you" = where it sits).

**Drill:** For me · For you · With us · Is it from you guys?

---

### S8 — Little word: before the first verb OR on the end of the second *(clitic placement choice)*
**shape:** `[person-word] quiero [verb]`  **or**  `quiero [verb]+[person-word]`

With a second verb in the to-form, the little word can go **either** in front of the first
verb **or** stuck on the end of the second — both are fine.

| before | attached | English |
|--------|----------|---------|
| `Le quiero decir.` | `Quiero decirle.` | I want to tell him. |

But for "I want to say **it**" (a thing), it's `Lo quiero decir` — `lo` is a thing, not a "to/for".

**Drill:** I want to tell him (both ways) · I want to say it

## 3. Same idea, said other ways

- **`me, te, nos` collapse meanings:** `me cocina algo` = "he cooks me something" **and**
  "he cooks something for me." No choice to make; only he/she/they split into `lo/la` vs `le/les`.
- **Drop the preposition.** `Compro para ellos` is possible, but Spanish prefers `Les compro`
  ("I buy for them"). "He buys it for me" → `Me lo compra`, not a `para mí` construction.
- **One little word covers to/for/from.** `Me lo compra` can mean "he buys it **for** me" *or*
  "he buys it **from** me" — context decides. English uses prepositions far more than Spanish.
- **`me lo darán` is a literal statement/question** ("are you going to give it to me?"), not a
  polite request. For "Can you give it to me?" reach for `poder` (can).
- **`nos hablamos`** most naturally means "we speak to *each other*" (since `nos` also means
  "each other"), not "we speak to ourselves."

---

## 4. Emphasis & bends

- **Migration is the whole bundle.** `lo/la/los/las` flip to `le/les` the instant the meaning is
  "to/for" someone. `me/te/nos` never flip. Listen for the feeling, don't translate "to/for".
- **Order is fixed when stacking:** person word, then thing word — `me lo`, `nos las`, never the reverse.
- **`se` = "self".** It replaces `lo/la/los/las` for the self-meaning; `me/te/nos` already self-cover.
  Add `él/ella/ellos` to disambiguate (`se queda` could be he, she, you-formal, …).
- **Accent the exceptions.** After a preposition it's **`mí`** and **`ti`** (with the accent on `mí`),
  never `me/te`. Everyone else is just the plain subject word: `con él`, `de ustedes`.
- **Clitic can ride either verb.** `Le quiero decir` ≡ `Quiero decirle`. The enclitic (end-of-to-form)
  form is the same gluing you've done before — it just now also has a "float to the front" option.

## Carried-in lexicon (from earlier bundles)

- `quiero` / `querer` — "I want" / "to want", the splitting /e/ (`quiero`, not *quero*) reused
  in S5/S8 (earlier bundles).
- The **future tense** (`daré, darás, dará, daremos, darán`) is the "to"-form + `-é, -ás, -á,
  -emos, -án` rule from a prior bundle, applied here to `dar`.
- **Enclitic pronoun on the to-form verb** (`quedarme`, `decirle`) extends the end-gluing first
  seen in Bundle B (S3).
- The front-loaded **`no`** for negation (`No nos lo darán`, `no me doy cuenta`) is the same
  pattern from Bundle B (S2).
- `ver` / `veo`, `hacer` / `hace`, `cocinar` (from `la cocina`) — recalled from earlier vocabulary.
