# Bundle K — Gerund + Future Tense (Lessons 34–36)

Two ways to bend a verb in time. First the real Spanish *-ing* (the **gerund**: *-ando /
-iendo*) — but it's *only* for "what I'm doing this very second," nothing like English's
loose *-ing*. Then the **future tense**, built by gluing the *haber* endings onto the whole
to-form verb and pushing the accent to the end.

Everything here is two moving parts snapped onto a verb you already know:

```
[ estar ]  +  [ verb-ing ]        →  doing it RIGHT NOW
 estoy         hablando               estoy hablando

[ verb (to-form) ]  +  [ -é/-ás/-á/-emos/-án ]   →  WILL do it
 comer                  -é                            comeré
```

> **Grammar terms** appear in *(italics)* next to the plain label, e.g. **-ing form**
> *(gerund)*. Plain label first so it reads like Mad Libs; the real term is there when
> you want it. **Slots** are written in `[brackets]` — fill them in.

---

## 1. Word groups

Words sorted by **the job they do in a sentence**, not by dictionary order. Drill the group, not the word.

### The "right now" helper *(estar as progressive auxiliary)* — pairs with the -ing form
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `estoy` | I am   | start of "I'm ___ing right now" |
| `está`  | he/she/it is | `está comiendo` = he's eating |
| `estamos`| we are | `estamos desayunando` = we're having breakfast |
| `están` | they are | `están vendiéndolo` = they're selling it |

We use **`estar`** (not `ser`) because doing something is a temporary **state**.

### Verbs to drop *-ing* on *(gerund-forming verbs)* — build the *-ando/-iendo* form
| Spanish | English | -ing form |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| `hablar`    | to speak    | `hablando` |
| `preparar`  | to prepare  | `preparando` |
| `desayunar` | to have breakfast (un-fast) | `desayunando` |
| `comer`     | to eat      | `comiendo` |
| `vender`    | to sell     | `vendiendo` |
| `ser`       | to be (characteristic) | `siendo` |

`-ar → -ando`; `-er`/`-ir → -iendo`. That's the whole rule (see §2, S1).

### Object pronouns *(direct-object pronouns)* — float before `estar` OR glue to the *-ndo* form
| Spanish | English | example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| `lo` | it / him | `lo estoy preparando` |
| `la` | it / her | |
| `los`| them (m)| `los compro` |
| `las`| them (f)| |

Glue them on and the *-ndo* word takes a written accent: `preparándolo`, `vendiéndolo` (see §2, S2).

### Future-tense endings *(future-tense suffixes; shaped like `haber`)* — tack onto the full to-form verb
| ending | who | echoes `haber` |
|--------|-----|----------------|
| `-é`   | I   | `he` |
| `-ás`  | you (informal) | `has` |
| `-á`   | he/she/it/usted | `ha` |
| `-emos`| we  | `hemos` |
| `-án`  | they / you guys | `han` |

The accent always lands on the ending — you're "pushing it into the future" (see §2, S5).

### Irregular `yo` form *(irregular first person)*
| Spanish | English | note |
|---------|---------|------|
| `salir` | to leave / go out | I-form is `salgo`, not "salo" (the `-go` trick) |

### Little words & time markers
| Spanish | English | term |
|---------|---------|------|
| `ahora` | now | adverb (right-now trigger) |
| `pronto`| soon | adverb (NOT right now → no -ing) |
| `mañana`| tomorrow | adverb (future → plain present or `voy a`) |
| `la semana que viene` | next week ("the week that comes") | time phrase |
| `con ellos` | with them ("with they") | preposition + pronoun |

## Two rules that govern this whole bundle

Not sentence structures, but they decide which form you reach for in every drill below.

```
RIGHT NOW, this moment        →  estar + -ando/-iendo   (estoy comiendo)
soon / tomorrow / general     →  plain present or voy a (como con ella mañana)
will, definitely later        →  future tense            (comeré con ella)
```

**The -ing trap.** English uses *-ing* everywhere; Spanish does NOT. "I'm leaving
tomorrow" is `salgo mañana`, never `estoy saliendo mañana`. Only use `-ando/-iendo` when
the action is literally happening *in this instant*. Reaching for *-ing* elsewhere is
**negative language transfer** — resist it.

**The future is regular.** Even verbs that are wild in the present (`ir` → `voy, vas...`)
go fully regular in the future: built straight off the to-form. Always think out the
whole infinitive *first*, then add the ending — `preparar` → `prepararé`, never "preparé".

## 2. Sentence structures (drill these)

Each structure: a **shape** line (the only "code"), the slots you can swap, worked examples, then a **Drill** the student finishes out loud or on paper.

---

### S1 — "I'm ___ing (right now)" *(present progressive)*
**shape:** `[estar] + [verb-ing]`  *(estar + gerund)*

- estar: `estoy` · `está` · `estamos` · `están`
- verb-ing: `hablando` · `comiendo` · `desayunando` · `vendiendo` · `esperando`

Build the *-ing* form: `-ar` drops to `-ando`, `-er`/`-ir` drop to `-iendo`.

```
estoy hablando         I'm speaking (right now)
estoy esperando aquí   I'm waiting here right now
estamos desayunando    we're having breakfast
está comiendo          he/she is eating
```

**Drill (EN → ES):** I'm eating · we're having breakfast · he's selling (it) · I'm waiting here right now

---

### S2 — Hook a pronoun on: "I'm ___ing it" *(progressive + object pronoun)*
**shape:** `[pronoun] + [estar] + [verb-ing]`  **OR**  `[estar] + [verb-ing]+[pronoun]`

Two legal spots — same meaning. Float the pronoun **before `estar`**, or **glue it to the
end** of the *-ndo* word. Glued on, the *-ndo* word gains a written accent.

| build | result | English |
|-------|--------|---------|
| lo + estoy + preparando | `lo estoy preparando` | I'm preparing it |
| estoy + preparando + lo | `estoy preparándolo` | (same) |
| los + están + vendiendo | `los están vendiendo` | they're selling them |
| están + vendiendo + lo | `están vendiéndolo` | they're selling it |

**Drill:** I'm preparing it (both ways) · they're selling it (both ways) · I'm buying them right now

---

### S3 — "I am BEING ___" *(progressive of ser → siendo)*
**shape:** `[estar] + siendo + [characteristic]`

For a characteristic happening *in the moment*, `ser` itself becomes `siendo` and rides on
`estar` — just like English "I am being honest."

- characteristic: `sincero / sincera` · `honesto / honesta` · `honestos`

```
estoy siendo sincero      I am being sincere (m)
estoy siendo sincera      I am being sincere (f)
estamos siendo honestos   we are being honest
```

**Drill:** I am being sincere · we are being honest · he is being honest

---

### S4 — Don't -ing the future: "I'm ___ing tomorrow" *(present for future)*
**shape:** `[present verb] + [future time word]`

Future context (`mañana`, `pronto`, `la semana que viene`) takes the **plain present**, not
the *-ing* form. English's *-ing* is a trap here.

```
salgo mañana                       I'm leaving tomorrow
salgo pronto                       I'm leaving soon
los compro la semana que viene     I'm buying them next week
desayuno con ellos mañana          I'm having breakfast with them tomorrow
```

Contrast the only legal *-ing*: `estoy saliendo` = I'm leaving *this very second*.

**Drill:** I'm leaving soon · I'm leaving tomorrow · I'm buying them next week · (now flip:) I'm leaving right now

---

### S5 — The real future tense: "I will ___" *(future tense)*
**shape:** `[verb-toform] + [-é/-ás/-á/-emos/-án]`

Take the **whole** to-form verb (the `-ar`/`-er`/`-ir` stays on) and add the ending. Stress
lands on the ending — accent pushed into the future. Works on every verb, regular or not.

```
comeré        I will eat
comeremos     we will eat
prepararé     I will prepare
esperaré      I will wait
organizará    he will organize
```

Pronouns on the future verb glue to the **end** like any to-form: `lo encontraré` /
`encontrarlo`. And `me quedaré` = "I will stay" (drop the `me` and `quedaré` is just "I will remain").

**Drill (EN → ES):** I will eat · we will prepare · he will organize · I will find it · I will stay here

#### Future-tense paradigm (verb `comer`, "to eat")
| who | form | English |
|-----|------|---------|
| I (yo)            | `comeré`    | I will eat |
| you (tú)          | `comerás`   | you will eat |
| he/she/usted      | `comerá`    | he/she will eat |
| we (nosotros)     | `comeremos` | we will eat |
| they / you guys   | `comerán`   | they will eat |

`ir` ("to go") is wild in the present but perfectly regular here: `iré, irás, irá, iremos,
irán`. (Hence `irán a Irán` = "they will go to Iran.")

---

### S6 — Future questions *(interrogative future)*
**shape:** `¿(question word) (no) + [future verb]?`

Same future form, just framed as a question. Word order matches English.

```
¿Irás?              Will you go?
¿No irás?           Won't you go?
¿Por qué no irás?   Why won't you go?
```

**Drill:** Will you go? · Won't you go? · Why won't you go?

## 3. Same idea, said other ways

- **"I'm ___ing it"** has two spellings: `lo estoy preparando` and `estoy preparándolo`. Same meaning — pick either.
- **The future, three ways:** `comeré` (true future) · `voy a comer` (going-to) · `como mañana` (present + time word). All say "I'll eat"; everyday Spanish leans on the last two.
- **`espero aquí` vs `estoy esperando aquí`:** both "I'm waiting here," but plain present tilts toward "I'll wait here," and the *-ing* form is firmly "right now, this moment."
- **`salgo`** = "I leave" / "I'm leaving" / "I'm leaving tomorrow" — the present covers all of them unless you mean *this instant*.

---

## 4. Emphasis & bends

- **The -ing form is narrow.** `-ando/-iendo` = *this very moment* only. For soon, tomorrow, next week, or general activities, use the plain present or `voy a`. This is the single biggest *-ing* trap.
- **Pronoun, two homes (progressive).** Before `estar` (`lo estoy preparando`) or glued to the *-ndo* word with an accent (`preparándolo`). Both fine.
- **Pronoun, one home (after `haber`).** Once a past-tense `haber` is in play, the pronoun MUST go *before* it — no gluing: `lo han recomendado`, never *han recomendádolo*.
- **Future = accent at the end.** Stress is always on the last syllable in the future, so you never have to think about where it goes. But think out the *whole* infinitive first: `preparar` → `prepararé`, not "preparé".
- **`salir` is `salgo`.** The `yo` form takes the `-go` twist; the rest behave.
